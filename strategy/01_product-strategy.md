# Product Strategy

## Working product intent

Explore a supportive job-search workspace that helps people move from discovering a role to understanding it, deciding whether it is worth pursuing, preparing a truthful application, and tracking what happens next.

This is a working direction, not a final product promise. Research must confirm which part of this journey is most valuable to solve first.

## Users to understand

Research should include more than conventional technology candidates. Relevant groups include fresh graduates, mid-career switchers, retrenched or mature workers, people returning after career breaks, international candidates, and non-degree, service, trades, and operational workers.

Differences in industry, employment status, language, disability, digital confidence, financial pressure, caregiving, immigration status, and access to devices may materially change the problem.

## Candidate value areas

- Capture and preserve a job posting and its context.
- Understand what a posting explicitly says and what remains uncertain.
- Compare an opportunity with the candidate's experience, preferences, and constraints.
- Prepare a relevant and truthful application.
- Research a company and formulate useful questions.
- Prepare for interviews.
- Track applications, deadlines, follow-ups, and outcomes.

These areas are hypotheses. `02_product-research-strategy.md` determines what should be prioritized.

## Scope boundaries for now

The following remain undecided:

- First product surface or combination of surfaces.
- Initial industry breadth.
- Degree of job-board integration or automated capture.
- Degree of application automation.
- Fit scoring, ranking, and recommendation behavior.
- Commercial model.
- Technology stack and model providers.

## Success direction

Success should not be measured only by application volume. Candidate outcomes may include better decisions, more relevant applications, reduced uncertainty, improved preparation, preserved authenticity, and stronger follow-through.

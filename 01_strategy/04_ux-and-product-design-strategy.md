# UX and Product Design Strategy

## Purpose

This document supports deliberate UX learning and design ownership while AI assists with exploration and critique.

## Design questions

- What is the user's goal, context, constraint, and next decision?
- What information is needed now, and what can wait?
- How should complex job and career information be grouped and explained?
- What happens when information is missing, uncertain, stale, or disputed?
- How does a user recover from an error, interruption, or abandoned session?
- Which responsibilities belong on phone, tablet, laptop, desktop, or another surface?

## Intuitive design principles

- Use familiar patterns, clear labels, visible system status, and predictable navigation.
- Reduce cognitive load through hierarchy, grouping, progressive disclosure, and meaningful defaults.
- Make important actions reversible where practical.
- Design useful loading, empty, confirmation, error, and recovery states.
- Use humane language that explains what the system knows, infers, and does not know.
- Prefer transparent recommendations over unexplained scores or warnings.

## Accessibility expectations

Accessibility should be considered from the beginning. Research and test keyboard navigation, focus order, screen-reader semantics, colour contrast, text scaling, motion preferences, touch targets, language clarity, and responsive behavior.

The product should be usable by people with different abilities, devices, languages, and levels of digital confidence. Accessibility standards are a baseline, not a substitute for observing people use the product.

## How AI may help

AI can generate alternative flows, critique information architecture, identify accessibility risks, draft interface copy, and explain design trade-offs. Human review and user testing must determine whether an output is appropriate for JobHub's context.

## Learning path

1. Learn user goals, flows, information architecture, and interaction principles.
2. Sketch and compare flows before building polished interfaces.
3. Test with realistic job-search scenarios.
4. Run accessibility and heuristic reviews.
5. Record design decisions and the evidence behind them.

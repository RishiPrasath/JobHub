## What research must learn

Research should answer the smallest decision-relevant question in each area, while keeping alternatives visible:

1. **Problem and outcomes:** which moments create uncertainty, avoidable effort, loss of context, or practical incompatibility, and what useful outcome is possible beyond application volume?
2. **People and inclusion:** how needs differ across career stage, occupation, employment situation, language, accessibility, device access, time, and digital confidence. Public material may reveal contexts; it cannot establish representative prevalence.
3. **Workflow and evidence:** how people discover, save, assess, prepare, apply, track, and return to opportunities; what information is missing or changes; and how facts, interpretations, signals, and unknowns should remain distinct.
4. **AI assistance and control:** what people assist, verify, edit, reject, automate, or keep personal; where AI creates generic, inaccurate, privacy-sensitive, or authenticity problems; and which user approvals are necessary.
5. **Surface and continuity:** where discovery, capture, preparation, and follow-up occur; how interruption and cross-device movement affect recovery; and which capability differences justify more than one surface.
6. **Competition and alternatives:** which existing products, manual workflows, coaching, or no-product options already address the problem, and whether any gap is durable and worth testing.
7. **Responsible operation:** what evidence, correction, privacy, accessibility, security, source-rights, and uncertainty controls a useful workflow would require.

The detailed topic inventory remains in the [original product research draft](../_drafts/product-research-strategy.md). It is a source of questions, not a mandate to research every topic before testing one bounded workflow. AI-era development questions remain in the [AI-era development draft](../_drafts/ai-era-development-strategy.md) and are sequenced after the immediate product uncertainty.

## Evidence boundary for the next activity

The next run is public-web research only because the owner has no direct user access. It may use openly accessible academic or qualitative studies, official statistics and reports, transparent public surveys, public firsthand accounts, public forum or review material, public support discussions, and public blog or video transcripts. It must not use private groups or accounts, author contact, profiling, sensitive inference, direct interviews, participant observation, purchases, live applications, private uploads, or external writes.

Public material is evidence about what a source reports, not permission to identify, contact, market to, or infer attributes about the author. Convenience material must not be used to claim population prevalence. The [cross-research synthesis](../02_research/04_results/00_cross-research-synthesis.md), earlier run findings, and their limitations are prior evidence and constraints; they are not a substitute for direct user validation.

The private Job Hunt workspace is not an input to this next run. Earlier bounded internal process observations remain labelled internal evidence in their historical run and must not be mixed into the public source ledger. Any future internal review requires separate scope and authorization.

## Next executable sequence: public user-insight run

The run is designed for one founder. If multiple agents are available, they may work on separate outputs; if not, the owner performs the roles sequentially. No role may turn a search result, snippet, ranking, or repeated phrase into a finding without opening and recording the underlying source.

### 1. Research Planner — scope gate

Confirm the decision, primary question, Singapore-first and cross-industry scope, 2023–2026 preferred time window, public-source boundary, exclusions, evidence standard, and stopping rule in the run brief. Keep the following candidate hypotheses from prior work explicitly unranked until this run compares them:

- application or role uncertainty and repeated preparation;
- practical working-condition or role-fit uncertainty;
- interrupted capture, return, and loss of context.

The planner does not expand the first pass to pricing, legal clearance, platform selection, stack selection, or a general competitor inventory.

### 2. Research Query Strategist — first after the Planner

Before evidence collection, produce `query-plan.md` with:

- the decision, questions, scope, exclusions, and unresolved terminology;
- a compact concept and vocabulary map, including user-language, academic, official, Singapore, occupation, stage, positive, failure, workaround, and contradiction terms;
- a small query portfolio grouped by intent: discovery, firsthand workflow, academic/qualitative, official/context, AI-use and control, device/accessibility, workaround/failure, and disconfirmation;
- the intended evidence type and executing role for every query family;
- preferred source families, acceptable supporting sources, weak signals, and source-independence rules;
- portable query intent separated from optional search-engine syntax;
- retrieval risks and the terms or populations not retrieved;
- the query-log schema and stopping rule.

The Query Strategist plans retrieval. The role does not synthesize findings, infer prevalence, or choose product scope.

### 3. Evidence collection — three bounded passes

Use the smallest first pass that can change the shortlist. A solo founder should time-box the run as follows; times are planning limits, not evidence thresholds:

- **Pass A — vocabulary and orientation, up to 30 minutes:** run the discovery families, add only useful new terms, and record failed or noisy searches in `query-log.md`.
- **Pass B — focused evidence, up to three 45-minute blocks:** collect sources for the three candidate hypotheses, covering at least one Singapore/context family and one broader comparison family where credible material exists. Use separate blocks for firsthand/workflow, domain/context, and UX/accessibility or AI-control evidence as needed.
- **Pass C — contradiction and stop check, up to 30 minutes:** search positive cases, workarounds, failure modes, and disconfirming language; deduplicate source families; then decide whether more browsing can change the candidate shortlist.

The founder may stop after Pass B when a central question has an explicit evidence gap and a synthetic workflow is the cheaper next step. Do not spend additional time filling every codebook cell if it will not change the next experiment.

### 4. Bounded roles and handoffs

- **Job-Seeker Practices Researcher:** public firsthand accounts, workflow steps, workarounds, abandonment, AI use, checking, correction, and emotional effort.
- **Job Domain Researcher:** job-search stages, occupation and Singapore context, public labour-market or service evidence, and distinctions between stated requirements and practical conditions.
- **UX Expert or UX and User-Flow Designer:** publicly documented device, interruption, accessibility, cognitive-load, and interaction-friction evidence only; no claim of usability validation.
- **Evidence Synthesizer:** compares source families, preserves contradictions, classifies claims, states confidence and limitations, and produces the shortlist. This is a synthesis role, not a fourth broad search pass.
- **Product Strategist, if used:** translates the shortlist into candidate synthetic tests without converting a proposal into an accepted scope decision.

Each handoff names the query family, intended question, source-quality rule, exclusions, and required recording fields. One person may hold several roles, but the outputs remain distinct.

## Recording contract

The run must use these exact files under `02_research/03_research-rounds/01_public-web-user-insights/runs/YYYY-MM-DD-topic-slug/`:

- `README.md` — status, purpose, role order, and relationship to later work;
- `brief.md` — approved decision, questions, scope, exclusions, pass budget, and stopping rule;
- `query-plan.md` — Research Query Strategist deliverable described above;
- `query-log.md` — every attempted query family, venue, date, result quality, useful source IDs, redundancy, failure, and follow-up term;
- `sources.md` — one source ledger row per inspected source;
- `observations.md` — one observation ledger row per relevant observation, linked to source IDs;
- `findings.md` — theme matrix, contradictions, leading and rejected hypotheses, confidence, and proposed synthetic test;
- `limitations.md` — coverage, source dependence, transfer limits, access failures, and claims explicitly withheld.

The source ledger must record at least: source ID, URL or stable location, title/publisher, publication date, access date, venue, geography, population or context as disclosed, method as disclosed, source family, evidence type, relevant scope, access limitation, and whether the source is firsthand, survey-reported, official, academic, vendor-reported, or researcher-observed.

The observation ledger must record at least: observation ID, source ID, job-search stage, context, concise compliant paraphrase, direct source statement versus researcher observation, interpretation if any, supporting or disconfirming status, confidence, and unresolved question. Do not copy long quotations or personal details.

## Evidence-quality rules

- Search snippets, autocomplete, rankings, result counts, AI summaries, and inaccessible pages are discovery aids or access notes, not evidence.
- Prefer disclosed-method academic or qualitative work, official statistics and reports, transparent surveys, and detailed firsthand accounts with enough context to interpret them.
- Treat anonymous posts, reviews, influencer material, vendor surveys, marketing pages, and support discussions as limited evidence. A review describes an experience; it does not establish a general product property.
- Count source families, not posts. The same author, thread, publisher, survey, dataset, press release, or republished story is not independent corroboration.
- Record positive cases, negative cases, workarounds, and contradictions. Do not search only for language that confirms JobHub's hypotheses.
- Label Singapore evidence separately from international evidence and state transfer limits. Recent publication does not make older underlying behavior current.
- Do not infer age, disability, immigration status, health, income, ethnicity, or other sensitive attributes from public text. Record only what a source explicitly discloses and only when relevant to the approved question.
- Do not claim prevalence, comparative burden, demand, usability, willingness to pay, hiring impact, or causality from this convenience corpus.
- Preserve unknown, inaccessible, conflicting, and outdated information instead of filling gaps with model inference.

## Stopping and transition rule

Stop the public-web run when:

1. each of the three candidate hypotheses has recurring evidence or an explicit gap;
2. the source ledger contains at least two materially independent source families for any hypothesis proposed for the next synthetic test, with at least one disclosed-method or otherwise well-contextualized source where available;
3. at least one contradiction, positive case, failure mode, or disconfirming search has been recorded for each leading hypothesis;
4. the major job-search stages in scope are represented or marked unresolved; and
5. another 30-minute search block is unlikely to change the shortlist, source-quality assessment, or next test.

If these conditions cannot be met within the time box, stop with the gap documented. Move to a synthetic workflow or low-fidelity prototype when the remaining uncertainty is about flow, recovery, evidence presentation, or surface capability. Do not continue broad browsing to simulate user validation. Move to separately approved direct research only if the unresolved question requires actual user behavior and the owner later authorizes that activity.

## Research lifecycle and decision gate

Research moves from question → query plan → source and observation ledgers → synthesis → candidate experiment → human decision. A result may recommend a strategy change, but it does not become a requirement until the owner accepts it and records the decision in [`08_decision-log.md`](08_decision-log.md). The [research charter](../02_research/00_research-charter.md), [common protocol](../02_research/02_instructions/00_common-research-protocol.md), [research backlog](../02_research/01_research-backlog.md), and [coverage matrix](../02_research/03_research-rounds/00_orientation-and-baseline/runs/2026-10-18-remaining-research/coverage.md) remain the supporting operating documents.

Research is complete enough for the next reversible experiment when the remaining uncertainty is explicit, the evidence and limitations are traceable, the alternatives have been considered, and more desk research is less valuable than the experiment. This is not approval of product scope, platform, stack, automation, pricing, or final strategy.

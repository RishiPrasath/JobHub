# Journey: Interviewing
Status: as-is, desk research, no product ideas. Source: round 09.

## Scope and who
The journey starts when a person gets an invitation after applying (3.1) and ends when reference and background checks finish (3.8), just before an offer. It covers fresh graduates, mid-career switchers, experienced professionals, foreign professionals and low-wage workers, though evidence for the last two is thin. People often keep searching while interviewing, and they return to search when responses stop. Singapore round details are anecdotal.

## Goal, trigger, end state
- **Goal:** Get shortlisted and progress through the employer's rounds toward an offer.
- **Trigger:** An email or call inviting them to interview (3.1).
- **End state:** The person has cleared the rounds and checks, and is waiting on or moving to an offer. Or they withdraw, fail a gate, or hear nothing and go back to searching.

## Moments that matter
- **3.1, accept or lose the slot:** Whether the role is still open is unclear, and silence after applying is common (Sn).
- **3.2, continue or withdraw:** The person must share current and expected pay and notice, and may not know if the role is open (A).
- **3.4, pass or fail gate:** Formats are unfamiliar and no reason is given for failing, so the person cannot tell why they stopped.
- **3.6 to 3.7, long waits:** Waiting after the final round and deciding whether to chase, using timing advice that is not research-based.
- **Whole journey, length:** Singapore reports describe two to four rounds over two weeks to two months (Glassdoor, anecdotal, A). A 2017 Randstad Singapore survey found 89% across Singapore, Hong Kong and Malaysia thought a process over four weeks was too long (S, dated, regional).
- **Any stage, loop-back:** Silence or a failed gate sends the person back to searching, or to diagnosing the search (section 5).

## How it differs by person

| Person type | What changes in this journey |
|---|---|
| Fresh graduate | Aptitude tests, preliminary interview, panel or assessment centre, final round. |
| Mid-career switcher | The screen probes the switch; transferable skills matter. |
| Experienced professional | Fewer but more senior rounds. Reference check reaches the current employer (listed under offer and start, but relevant to 3.8). |
| Foreign professional | Pass timing affects offers. Little else on interviewing; thin evidence. |
| Low-wage worker | Little evidence. |

## Gaps in the evidence
- Singapore rounds come from Glassdoor candidate reports, which are anecdotal and not representative.
- Most rows have no evidence tag in findings.md and rest on agent notes; the research used Haiku-model agents, and sources were mostly snippets or secondary write-ups.
- Typical time to response and drop-off by stage in Singapore: nothing found.
- No evidence on differences between government, multinational and small-company interview practice.
- Channels and touchpoints are mostly not stated in findings.md.
- The ghosting figures are from a small US remote-job survey (n=529); the 2017 Randstad figures are dated.
- Thinnest evidence: foreign professional and low-wage worker rows.

## Update from the gap-fill research (2026-11-07)
Source: [gap-fill findings](../../2026-11-07-gap-fill/findings.md). Changes to the evidence above:
- **Rounds and length:** Employment Hero 2023 (employers): about 42 days to hire in Singapore; Randstad 2017: process average rose from 30 to 68 days and 91% in Singapore would reject an offer after four weeks. One public agency (GRA, official page) lists written assessment, interview, psychometric tests, second interview, offer within 2 weeks of the last; SCDF says 2 to 3 months (snippet). Other agency detail is snippet only.
- **3.4 Assessments:** formats include written, psychometric and video interviews (agency pages, university career services, mostly snippets).
- **3.8 Checks:** PDPC (May 2024): a reference from a former employer needs no consent but the employer must notify. Whether the current employer is contacted before an offer: no source found.
- **Feedback:** MOM position (via trade press, Sep 2025): employers should not be required to give reasons; Robert Half 2018: 52% of about 500 Singapore job seekers got no feedback. Tripartite Standard on Recruitment Practices is voluntary.
- **Still unknown:** SME differences; named large-employer stage lists from their own pages.

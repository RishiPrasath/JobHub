# Round 09 synthesis

Status: draft synthesis written during the 2026-11 reorganization; owner review pending

Research date: 2026-11-07, Singapore.

## Question

What are the real, current processes people follow when searching, applying, interviewing and moving from offer to first day, and how do these differ by type of person? ([Round brief](round-brief.md))

## What was done

- [First run](runs/2026-11-07-how-job-seekers-actually-job-hunt/README.md): four Haiku-model agents, one per process, merged by the lead. Tags: S (source opened), Sn (snippet or secondary), A (anecdotal), I (inference). The lead did not re-open primary sources.
- [Gap-fill run](runs/2026-11-07-gap-fill/findings.md): eight agents worked on weak and thin evidence, with raw notes labelled P, R, S or U. The lead re-checked six consequential claims.

The first run's findings and limitations files were not edited after the gap-fill. **Where the two disagree, the gap-fill below supersedes the first run.**

## What remains unknown

Days to hear back and drop-off by stage in Singapore; how people track applications, and whether alerts or follow-ups help; Singapore negotiation and counteroffer rates; how local low-wage workers and foreign professionals search; SME differences; whether the current employer is contacted before an offer; the Employment Act text; the Workplace Fairness Act's recruitment duties and exemption; MOM's median unemployment duration value; named large-employer stage lists from their own pages.

## Limits

- First-run evidence is mostly snippets and secondary write-ups, and much of it is old, US or vendor data ([limitations](runs/2026-11-07-how-job-seekers-actually-job-hunt/limitations.md)).
- Gap-fill claims outside the six re-checked ones stand as the agents reported them.
- This is desk research only. The run says further work on behaviour would need primary data from job seekers, not more desk research.
- Review-deck readiness is limited to processes, labelled evidence strength and openly listed gaps. It does not extend to measured Singapore behaviour.

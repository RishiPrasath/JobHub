## Sources and observations

### C01 — Algorithmic Writing Assistance on Jobseekers' Resumes Increases Hires

- URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2301.08083
- Authors: Emma van Inwegen, Zanele Munyikwa, John J. Horton.
- Publication/version inspected: submitted 2023-01-19; arXiv abstract only, not a full-paper review.
- Type/family: primary study author abstract; global online contract-work market, not a Singapore representative sample.
- Observation: abstract reports a field experiment involving nearly half a million job seekers, an 8% relative increase in hiring probability for assisted applicants, and no evidence of lower employer satisfaction.
- Interpretation: improving expression of existing experience may help; this does not evaluate invented qualifications, present-day chatbots, or JobHub.
- Confidence: moderate for the narrow reported result; transfer to Singapore roles and current generative AI is untested.

### C02 — More, but Worse: The Marketplace Effects of AI-Generated Job Posts

- URL: https://www.emmawiles.com/storage/jobot.pdf
- Authors: Emma Wiles, John J. Horton.
- Version inspected: 2026-09-04, title page and introduction pp. 1–6 (PDF indices 0–5). Search results used the previous title, Generative AI and Labor Market Matching Efficiency; the opened document determines the recorded title/date.
- Type/family: primary author-hosted working paper; randomised employer drafting intervention in an online labour market.
- Observation: authors report more posting and reduced employer writing time without a discernible increase in matches, alongside more generic, less informative descriptions. Their application-time accounting is model-based and specific to this setting.
- Interpretation: cheaper generation and greater posting volume need not improve matching. A polished ad cannot establish employer intent or job quality.
- Confidence: moderate for the reported experiment, limited generalisation. No ghost-job prevalence or individual employer-intent diagnosis follows.

## Cross-source implication

Researcher recommendation: assess assistance by truthful information conveyed and useful next actions, with correction effort recorded. More generated text or more applications is an inadequate standalone success metric. These studies examine different interventions and populations; their findings are complementary possibilities, not a direct model comparison.

No audio, participant tests, product experiments, or external writes performed.

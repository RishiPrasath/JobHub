# Round 02 query plan — pre-execution draft

Status: prepared by the Research Query Strategist; adopted for the completed 2026-10-18 pain/practices/guidance run. This portfolio includes planned variants; actual executed queries are in that run's query log.

Purpose: guide a bounded public-web and YouTube evidence pass about reported pains and practices in job searching, applications, interview preparation, job fit, compensation, truthful resume tailoring, AI use, and interrupted workflows.

This plan supplements the historical fictional workflow comparison. It does not turn fictional scenarios or internal workspace material into user evidence.

## YouTube discovery terms

Use these as bounded YouTube searches, prioritizing videos with identifiable creators and accessible English transcripts:

- `job posting red flags bait and switch ghost jobs salary`
- `how to know if a job is worth applying for`
- `salary negotiation know your worth market value`
- `truthful resume tailoring no exaggeration`
- `job interview preparation while working full time`
- `job seekers use ChatGPT resume interview preparation`
- `AI job search mistakes privacy hallucinations`
- `nobody wants to work pay working conditions employer language`
- `phone job search save job apply later laptop`

Review the five supplied videos first. New videos should add a new perspective, vocabulary, counterexample, or evidence type—not repeat the same advice.

## Source separation

Keep these evidence types separate:

- Firsthand accounts: concrete public experiences, actions, workarounds, failures, and outcomes. Use for language and mechanisms, not prevalence.
- Research: qualitative interviews, transparent surveys, accessibility studies, and labour-market studies with population, method, and date.
- Official sources: Singapore MOM, WSG, SkillsFuture, TAFEP, public employment services, ILO, OECD, and comparable labour agencies.
- Expert advice: attributable recruiters, career services, labour economists, accessibility practitioners, workplace experts, and identifiable YouTube creators.

Label advice as advice. Do not convert it into evidence of actual user behaviour.

## Video inclusion and exclusion

Prioritize videos with:

- English captions or a transcript;
- identifiable title, creator, and publication date;
- direct relevance to an approved research family;
- concrete examples, practices, failure modes, or counterexamples;
- timestamped claims that can be checked against another source type.

Downgrade or exclude videos that are anonymous, promotional, repeated copies of the same advice, based only on views/comments/ranking, missing captions with no verification route, primarily selling a product/course, or making unsupported broad statistics.

Auto-generated captions are acceptable for discovery and preliminary extraction. Verify high-impact wording, salary figures, negations, job titles, and qualifications against the video timestamp.

## Cap and stopping rule

- Review the five supplied videos first.
- Search no more than eight additional candidate videos.
- Run no more than three web queries per family before reviewing retrieval quality.
- For each priority family, seek one firsthand source family, one research/official/expert source family, and one contradiction or positive case.
- Count reposts, duplicate surveys, and videos repeating the same source as one source family.
- Stop a family after two independent useful source families and one contrary/alternative case, or after three additional searches produce only duplication/noise.
- Stop the entire pass when the approved themes have coverage or an explicit retrieval gap and further search is unlikely to change the shortlist.
- Never use query volume, view count, ranking, or repeated advice as a prevalence threshold.

## Retrieval risks

Account for English-language and platform bias, U.S./UK overrepresentation, graduate/office-worker overrepresentation, extreme negative experiences, SEO and career-coach dominance, vendor surveys, auto-caption errors, repeated advice, missing context, outdated salary claims, and overgeneralized “red flag” language.

The private Job Hunt workspace is process evidence for this owner, not representative user research.

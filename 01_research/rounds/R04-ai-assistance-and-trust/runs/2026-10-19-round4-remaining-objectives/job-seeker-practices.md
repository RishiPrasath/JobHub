# Job-seeker practices and truthful tailoring

## Evidence posture

Singapore public evidence is stronger for official guidance and available services than for observed behaviour. UK student/graduate surveys report higher AI use than general job-changer surveys; neither is a Singapore-wide prevalence estimate. Public trackers and Reddit accounts are useful directional evidence only.

## What truthful tailoring means

The evidence supports a **selection-and-explanation** model more strongly than a “generate a new resume” model:

| Practice | Legitimate use | Risk boundary |
|---|---|---|
| Select experience | Reorder or shorten true experience to foreground relevant evidence | Omitting context so a minor contribution appears to be ownership |
| Translate skills | Map an evidenced skill to accurate role language | Add a skill only because it appears in the posting |
| Use metrics | Use numbers the candidate can substantiate | Invent percentages, revenue, scale, team size or impact |
| Career change | Bridge prior skills, projects, coursework and target tasks | Treat training as equivalent to professional experience |
| Employment gap | Disclose briefly if the candidate chooses, then show readiness | Fabricate consulting, freelance or training activity |
| Freelance/contract work | Describe deliverables, scope, duration and safe client context | Imply employment or disclose confidential results |
| Cover letter | Use AI to outline/edit a candidate-owned motivation | Generic enthusiasm or false personal connection |
| Interview preparation | Structure real experience and practise likely questions | Memorise fabricated stories or use hidden real-time assistance |

## Common job-search moments

- **Finding a suitable role:** skills frameworks, interests, job descriptions and salary information help narrow options, but a match is not proof of suitability or hiring probability.
- **Career changes and return-to-work:** guidance recommends identifying transferable skills, researching target requirements and closing gaps through training or conversion pathways. Outcomes remain uncertain.
- **Gaps and caregiving:** a brief, privacy-preserving explanation may be appropriate; the tool should not force health, family or caregiving disclosure.
- **Tracking:** public accounts describe spreadsheets or trackers for role, company, salary, dates, status, contacts, follow-ups and the exact resume version. This is low-to-medium confidence evidence and must not be treated as prevalence.
- **Interview preparation:** role-specific questions, mock answers and feedback are common recommended uses. The answer should remain grounded in experiences the candidate can defend.

## AI-use evidence and contradictions

Public evidence supports AI for editing, organising, retrieval and practice more strongly than autonomous authorship or application.

- One large field experiment reported improved hiring outcomes for algorithmic writing assistance in a specific online labour market.
- Tilburg experiments found improved spelling/formality but no higher interview probability and weaker matching in some contexts.
- A freelancer-platform study associated AI cover-letter access with more alignment and callbacks, while also associating more editing with better outcomes; platform-specific evidence is not general proof.
- Surveys range from lower general job-changer use to much higher student/graduate use. Population and method differences explain why a single adoption estimate would be misleading.
- Public accounts include unchanged or lightly edited AI output, generic language, administrative fatigue and selective tracking; these are anecdotes, not population estimates.

## JobHub research implications

The evidence justifies testing whether a workflow can:

1. maintain a claims/evidence ledger;
2. distinguish demonstrated, transferable, adjacent, developing and aspirational skills;
3. show before/after changes and source evidence;
4. request confirmation for metrics, ownership verbs, seniority, credentials, dates and names;
5. preserve personal voice and the original document;
6. support privacy-preserving gap descriptions;
7. record exact submitted versions, job details and answers without excessive manual entry;
8. use verified resume evidence for interview practice;
9. label uncertainty rather than turn a match score into a hiring prediction.

These are research implications, not accepted product requirements.

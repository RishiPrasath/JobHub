# Round 04 integrated findings

## Bottom line

The strongest cross-report conclusion is that JobHub’s research problem is not simply “how to use AI to apply faster.” It is how to help a person understand a role, judge whether it suits their circumstances, present truthful evidence, and retain control when AI is involved.

## Findings by objective

### 1. Industry fit is contextual

Agriculture/agri-food, utilities/energy/water/waste, maritime/offshore/seafaring and domestic/household services have materially different role families, credentials, work environments, hiring channels and value signals. Hard gates, transferable bridges, trainable gaps and preferences should not be collapsed into one generic score.

### 2. Truthful tailoring is evidence transformation

The public guidance and research support selecting, translating, structuring and explaining real evidence. They do not support inventing metrics, ownership, dates, credentials, employers or outcomes. Career gaps, caregiving, freelance work and career changes need privacy-preserving, evidence-based treatment.

### 3. “ATS-friendly” is not a universal format

Semantic structure, selectable text, reading order, readable typography and accessibility are robust concerns. Parser behaviour varies by system. A scoped format check is more defensible than a guarantee or a one-size-fits-all ban on columns, tables, graphics or PDFs.

### 4. Market value is contextual and not human worth

Wage tables and wage floors can inform occupational benchmarks, but they exclude some arrangements and populations and cannot predict an individual’s worth. Pay, conditions, safety, stability, progression and constraints should remain separate.

### 5. AI assistance has value only with review and ownership

AI can reduce writing and organisation effort, but outcome evidence is mixed and adoption estimates vary by population. The most defensible role is an explainable assistant with provenance, uncertainty, editing, undo, portability and explicit approval—not a silent autonomous applicant.

## Cross-cutting research implications

1. Preserve original documents, postings, extracted claims, user-confirmed facts, suggestions and final exports as distinct evidence states.
2. Make “unknown,” “not stated,” “conflicting” and “inferred” distinct.
3. Show why a role was included, excluded or considered a weak fit.
4. Surface work conditions and preferences alongside skills.
5. Treat external content as untrusted data and isolate it from actions.
6. Test interrupted, cross-device, accessibility and non-linear-career workflows.
7. Measure factuality, provenance, user correction and explainability—not just time saved or match score.

## Human decision gate

Research now supports a decision discussion around:

- whether truthful evidence preservation is a central product principle;
- which job-seeker segments and contexts are in the first scope;
- how sensitive gaps and caregiving information should be handled;
- the acceptable boundary between assistance and automation;
- whether application tracking is core or optional;
- what evidence threshold is required before presenting role-fit or market-value conclusions;
- which unresolved claims require participant research rather than more desk research.

No product scope, platform, architecture or AI-provider decision is made by this run.

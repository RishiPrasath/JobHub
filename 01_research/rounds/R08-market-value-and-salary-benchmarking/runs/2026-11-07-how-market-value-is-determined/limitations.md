# Limitations

## How the research was done
- **Six Haiku-model agents did the first pass.** They are fast and cheap but make more errors than a larger model, so their notes were treated as leads. Each was told to label what it opened and what it only saw in search snippets, and most did so carefully. Even so, several statements in their notes were corrected or dropped (below).
- **The lead researcher re-opened primary sources for only some claims.** Confirmed by the lead: MOM's occupational wage page, the BLS OEWS design page, the IMF skill-premium note, the abstract of the German survey-versus-administrative-pay study, the Minneapolis Fed paper's findings, MOM's job-advertising rule, the Richmond Fed Glassdoor study's abstract, the headline return-to-schooling figure (through search results summarising the review's abstract), and the date of the DOJ withdrawal (through several law-firm summaries). Other primary claims (the Oi and Idson firm-size premium, Song et al. on between-firm inequality, Card on schooling returns, the Colorado pay-transparency paper, CPF rates, EP salary thresholds) were read by an agent and not re-opened.
- **Web page reading is by summary.** Pages were read through a tool that returns a model-written summary, not the page text, so figures were checked against the summary only.
- **A search tool and a few sites refused or returned nothing.** PwC, CBIZ, Aon, Carta, Ravio, EUR-Lex, the DOJ and FTC archive pages, and some PDFs (including MOM's methodology PDF) could not be read.

## What the sources could not tell us
- **MOM's survey coverage in detail.** Whether it covers only private-sector establishments, how bonuses and wage types are defined beyond "excluding bonuses", and which occupation classification version it uses were not confirmed, because the methodology PDF could not be read.
- **How much of pay each variable explains.** No opened source gave an explained-variance figure for the standard pay model, the size of the experience return, the size of the union or location effects, or the effect of local labour-market tightness. Where the report gives a size, it is because a source gave it.
- **The causal effect of a skill on a person's pay.** The best study is associational and uses advertised pay.
- **Whether posted ranges match what is paid**, and **how far different salary surveys disagree** for the same job.
- **Private salary guides' methods** (Singapore recruiters' guides, NodeFlair, Payscale, Indeed): no verified method pages. No study validating Payscale or Indeed against payroll or official data was found.
- **Current antitrust guidance** on exchanging pay data (the FTC's position, the status of the 2016 guidance and any 2025 replacement) and any current numeric survey rule such as a minimum number of reporting firms. An agent-supplied "25% weight cap" was not found in any source and is not used.
- **How many employers target which percentile.** Figures (such as 84% at the median) were seen only in snippets.
- **Total compensation in Singapore:** how 13th-month pay, bonuses, allowances and equity are treated under CPF, and the CPF wage ceilings, were not checked against an official page (secondary guides disagreed).

## Claims from the agent notes that were not carried into the report, or were changed
- PwC's AI wage premium figure (page blocked); Burning Glass decision-skill premiums, Labussiere and Bol, Rony, Deming and Kahn, Ternikov (snippets only).
- Union wage premium figures, the Moretti cost-of-living estimate, and a Mincer explained-variance figure from a proceedings paper (snippets only).
- An agent's statement that the IMF note "calls its macro result a prediction, not a causal estimate" and separately discusses posted versus realised wages: on checking, the note uses associational language and does not separately discuss realised pay, so the report says "associated with" and treats the posted-pay limit as an inference.
- Search-snippet details on Lightcast's, Payscale's and Michael Page's data sizes.

## Scope limits
- **United States evidence dominates the studies.** Singapore-specific evidence is mainly official statistics pages, not research on how Singapore pay is set.
- **No salary figure is given as a fact about any person or role.** The worked example is made up.
- **Pay equity topics** (gender, ethnicity, age gaps in pay) were touched only where they affected the data or negotiation evidence; they are a separate topic.
- **One pass, one day.** No second reviewer, and no domain specialist (for example a compensation professional) reviewed the report.

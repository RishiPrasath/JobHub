# Round 03 findings

Research cutoff/access date: 2026-10-19, Asia/Singapore. Status: bounded research complete; independent integration review resolved its three must-fixes. Coverage remains partial as documented. Public-web desk research, not direct user research or a market survey. Final owner acceptance is outstanding.

## Main answer

The collected examples show materially different preparation and feasibility questions within and across industries. A common application journey remains useful as an organising model, but a universal “improve the resume” answer would omit qualifications, shift/site fit, practical evidence, employer identity and uncertainty about recruitment. This is the coordinator's interpretation of a purposive corpus, not a validated product proposition.

## F01. Industry alone is too coarse a profile

**Source-reported context / interpretation.** Healthcare support and regulated nursing, food service and hotel guest service, logistics graduate searches and warehouse deployment, finance operations and sales/advice, and creative portfolios involve different questions. Industry, occupation, arrangement and entry route must stay separate. A public hospital is still healthcare; a recruitment intermediary is not automatically the hiring client. See the [13 industry profiles](industry-profiles/README.md) and six deeper reports.

Sources: R3-M05/M06/M14/M15/M18, R3-B01/B02/B15 and the sector reports. This does not rank industries by difficulty or opportunity.

## F02. Eligibility, transferable experience and evidence are different

**Source-reported requirements.** SNB's graduate route and particular employer role criteria show why clinical eligibility cannot be inferred from persuasive writing. Social-work conversion and support-role descriptions follow different routes. **Interpretation:** preserve actual experience, required versus preferred conditions, and missing qualification evidence separately. Formal training and human guidance can be necessary alternatives to document editing.

Sources: R3-B01/B02/B03/B04/B10/B14/B15/B16. These sources do not authorise individual professional, immigration, medical or legal advice.

## F03. Practical fit can precede preparation

**Source-reported experiences / employer statements.** Accounts raise availability, travel, deployment and schedule questions. A warehouse account reports agency/site-hours disagreement; a logistics graduate describes commute and role-title uncertainty. Hotel and healthcare examples specify role-dependent skills and shifts. **Interpretation:** duties, employer, worksite, hours and pay components deserve separate verification before treating an opening as feasible.

Sources: R3-M02/M10/M23/M24, R3-A01/A02/A08/A09/A13 and R3-B02/B15. No single experience establishes a universal industry condition.

## F04. Preparation extends beyond application prose

**Source-reported process / advice.** A creative agency describes portfolio/task discussion; a creative applicant asks what samples to bring. Semiconductor applicants discuss domain knowledge and assessment preparation. **Interpretation:** real work samples, personally performed tasks and explainable reasoning can matter alongside a CV. Public advice recommending exaggeration is a risk to identify, not practice to copy.

Sources: R3-M18/M19, R3-B07/B08/B09 and R3-C04/C07/C10. Creative reports include onerous-task concerns, refusal and international positive/paid-test counterexamples. The media employer's unpaid-test and non-use promises are stated policy, not verified safeguards. Interview tasks are reported or employer-stated, not directly observed or standardised across a sector.

The final [arts/trades supplement](arts-and-trades-supplement.md) adds a dated Wild Rice open-audition notice (R3-P03) and a non-Singapore machinist comparison (R3-P04). A performance self-tape differs from design evidence, but local casting outcomes and Singapore operational-trades journeys remain gaps. R3-P05 records a route/legitimacy question, not a verified gig.

## F05. “Fake job” combines different claims

**Interpretation grounded in inspected sources.** Separate impersonation, stale ads, copies, disclosed future pools, changed circumstances and misleading nonexistent vacancies. Record authenticity, visible status, external recruitment, eventual outcome and contemporaneous intent independently. None of non-response, reposting, an old page or non-fill alone establishes deliberate deception.

Sources: R3-G01/G02/G03/G04/G07/G09/G13, plus R3-M21's eventual-offer account. See [vacancy credibility](vacancy-credibility.md) for evidence thresholds and alternative explanations.

## F06. Local evidence supports the concern, not a current prevalence rate

**Historical regulator-reported case.** MOM's July 2018 alert describes an unnamed agency advertising a nonexistent translator role to collect resumes for marketing. That narrow account supports researching the harm without accusing current named employers.

**Current-context official statement.** MOM's October 2025 parliamentary answer describes validation of reported vacancies and recognises that cross-portal posting can be normal recruitment. That does not certify every advertisement. The coordinator re-opened both originals as a spot-check; repeated inspection is not independent corroboration.

Sources: R3-G16/G04, with G15 in the same historical advisory family. Commercial employer surveys, seeker suspicions, review-based classifiers and non-fill datasets measure different things. None supplies a defensible current Singapore posting-level rate in this corpus.

## F07. AI practice is reported, but sector-specific behaviour remains thin

**Source-reported claims.** A Singapore-linked discussion separates an unfinished prototype from other commenters claiming matching, tailoring, applications and tracking. Claimed time savings/callbacks are unverified. **Unknown:** targeted nurse and warehouse queries did not establish occupation-specific application AI-use sequences. Manufacturing advice to try AI is not proof that an applicant used it.

Sources: R3-P02 and R3-B08. No provider capability, permission to scrape, comparative effectiveness or hiring benefit was independently tested. See [cross-industry practices](cross-industry-practices.md).

## F08. Manual and human routes remain meaningful comparators

**Source-reported alternatives.** Outlet QR/walk-ins, peers, career guidance, supported employment, formal conversion and introductions appear in the evidence. Some successes are older or provider-selected. They prevent assuming that everyone needs AI drafting or online mass applications, but do not prove one route superior.

Sources: R3-M02/M04/M14, R3-A11/A12, R3-B12/B16 and R3-P01. P01's promotional numerical comparisons were excluded for missing methods. Silence about AI does not establish deliberate non-use.

## What to carry into analysis

These are questions for the owner, not accepted scope:

- Which information is shared across profiles, and which must remain role-, arrangement- or jurisdiction-specific?
- Can a small, truthful preparation/verification task improve on manual and human alternatives without inventing credentials or hiring intent?
- What information can a person verify, what should become a clarification question, and what must remain unknown?
- Which conditions are outside software's control, and which evidence gaps would materially change a proposed scope?

Do not choose a target sector, platform, stack, automation level, privacy policy or employer-detection score from this research. Use [comparison](comparison.md), [coverage](coverage.md), [limitations](limitations.md) and [review](review.md) together.

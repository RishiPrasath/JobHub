# Round 03 sector coverage and comparison framework

Prepared 18–19 October 2026. Status: **sampling plan, not findings**. Every suggested difference below is a question to investigate, not an assertion about an industry.

## Breadth map: 13 purposive research baskets

The baskets organise work; they are not official SSIC categories, equally sized markets, exhaustive national coverage or product segments. Keep the source's actual employer activity and occupation alongside the basket ID. Record subdivisions rather than smoothing over differences within a broad basket.

| ID | Research basket | Illustrative role contrasts | Questions to investigate, including possible counterexamples |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| S01 | Retail and wholesale | Sales assistant, stock/inventory support, buyer | Walk-ins versus portals; roster/location clarity; commission and guaranteed pay; legitimate recurring recruitment |
| S02 | Food services, accommodation and tourism-related employment | Service crew/cook, housekeeping/guest services, reservations/revenue support | Whether F&B and accommodation routes differ; trial shifts, experience evidence, seasonality and fast versus drawn-out hiring |
| S03 | Healthcare and social care | Care assistant, patient-services administrator, nurse/social worker | Which credentials or registration actually apply; support versus regulated roles; shifts, onboarding, transferable experience and vacancy lead times |
| S04 | Education and training | Classroom/operations assistant, admissions support, teacher/trainer | Demonstration lessons, credentials, term/contract work, preparation and clear versus unclear assessment requirements |
| S05 | Logistics, transport and storage | Warehouse worker/driver, dispatcher, supply-chain analyst | Licence verification, subcontracting, shift/location constraints; direct, agency and platform-work distinctions |
| S06 | Construction and built environment | Tradesperson, project administrator, surveyor/site professional | Project-based openings, worksite mobility, practical evidence, required qualifications and genuine project delays |
| S07 | Manufacturing, engineering and trade work | Operator, production planner, technician/engineer | Practical versus professional assessments, certification, shift disclosure and role-specific experience; do not count every engineer as manufacturing |
| S08 | Finance and insurance | Branch service/sales, operations support, analyst/specialist | Sales versus advisory duties, base versus variable pay, screening stages and role-dependent requirements |
| S09 | Professional, administrative and business-support services | Accountant/legal support/consultant; administrative support; outsourced cleaner/security worker | Separate professional services from outsourced support; cases/tests, licences where applicable, agency-client attribution and deployment terms |
| S10 | Creative, media and arts work | Designer, production crew, writer/creative professional | Portfolio/audition routes, project contracts, unpaid speculative work, rights questions and positive transparent selection practices |
| S11 | Public administration and civic/community organisations | Public-facing service officer, administrator, policy/community worker | Formal recruitment, selection windows, appointment terms, role clarity and public versus private process context |
| S12 | IT and digital services — comparator | Helpdesk, developer, digital operations/product roles | Technical tests, staged interviews, claims verification and manual/AI alternatives; which problems are not unique to IT? |
| S13 | Personal, repair and local services | Beauty/service practitioner, repair worker, scheduling/business support | Practical demonstrations, credentials where applicable, customer-work evidence, commission/chair arrangements and employee/self-employed distinctions |

Public ownership and nonprofit status are overlays, not reasons to recode a hospital as public administration or a school as generic community work. Charities follow their actual activity. Sales, HR, finance, design and IT are cross-industry functions; a job title alone cannot establish the employer's industry. Outsourced security/cleaning can involve a support-services employer and a separately tagged client location.

Do not claim coverage of energy/utilities/water/waste, agriculture/aquaculture, private-household domestic work, defence-specific recruitment, offshore/seafaring or executive search from incidental role examples. Track these as outside or undercovered unless a recorded within-budget change explicitly adds them. Migrant recruitment, immigration eligibility and cross-border placement require separately scoped authoritative context; incidental accounts do not establish full coverage. Gig/freelance examples are bounded contrasts, not a complete study of finding clients.

## Within- and across-basket lenses

Record these when a source actually discloses them:

- Occupation/duties and professional, operational or support responsibilities. Frontline and professional are not opposites.
- Entry-level, experienced, career-change and return-to-work context.
- Permanent/fixed-term/casual; full-/part-time; direct/agency; employee/self-employed.
- On-site/hybrid/remote, shifts, predictable hours and location/commute constraints.
- SME/large employer or unknown; public/private/nonprofit ownership separately.
- Language, access, caring/time constraints and digital confidence as reported, never inferred from identity or writing style.
- Use, non-use and verification of AI, plus manual records, peers, coaching, agencies and other alternatives.

This is not a full factorial sample. Report missing combinations and do not fill them with imagined personas.

## Selecting six deeper comparisons

After the breadth map, record six choices in the dated run brief. At least five are non-IT. Prefer complementary mechanisms, hiring routes and important evidence gaps, while avoiding only the loudest complaints or easiest-to-find sectors. Include at least two within-basket role contrasts, a frontline/on-site context, a credential-led route, and a portfolio or practical-assessment route where evidence allows.

Record both inclusion and exclusion reasons. An inaccessible or low-evidence sector is a coverage gap, not proof of low need or a reason to rank it last. IT may be omitted from the deep dives if its existing research already provides the comparison. No selection constitutes a target-market commitment.

## Common report template

For each selected basket, answer the same questions in the same order:

1. Exact employer-industry/occupation/arrangement scope and what is not covered.
2. Discovery channels, application requirements and what counts as relevant experience.
3. A source-attributed reported search/application/interview sequence, or an explicit gap.
4. Difficulties, positive cases, workarounds and competing explanations.
5. Assessments, interview preparation, pay/conditions and offer decisions.
6. Use and non-use of AI, manual and human alternatives, checking and correction.
7. Vacancy credibility: observable status, claimed intent, uncertainty and applicant responses.
8. Within-basket contrasts and what cannot be separated from occupation, arrangement or source bias.
9. Shared versus sector-sensitive implications, confidence and limits; no accepted features or sector ranking.

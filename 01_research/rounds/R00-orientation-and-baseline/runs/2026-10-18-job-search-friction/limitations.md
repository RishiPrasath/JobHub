# R-01 limitations and unresolved questions

Date: 2026-10-18. Scope: the approved Singapore-first desk-research run. Read with [sources.md](sources.md) and the specialist handoffs; limitations apply to every promoted conclusion.

## What this run cannot establish

The collection does not establish which job-search stage creates the greatest loss of time, confidence or opportunity. No retained study supplies comparable stage-level measures across the target groups. Application counts, elapsed employment transitions, stated preferences and isolated experiences cannot substitute for active task time, measured confidence change, or a counterfactual opportunity loss.

No researcher directly observed a job seeker using a portal or preparing an application. Behaviour evidence is reported by sources. The run did not test an interface, intervention, AI output, employer response mechanism, business model or willingness to pay. It cannot select a platform, initial user segment, product scope or feature priority.

## Coverage and selection

- Public English-language desk research is a purposive sample, not a systematic review or representative survey.
- Professional and university-graduate voices dominate the application-process evidence. Non-degree graduates, service/trades/operational workers, small-firm hiring, informal recruitment, international candidates, non-English users and low digital confidence remain underrepresented.
- Supported-employment and caregiver studies improve context but cover selected populations. They cannot stand in for all lower-income workers, caregivers or returners.
- Disability, accessibility, device access, assisted/offline work, interruptions and cross-device continuity were not adequately measured. Absence of evidence is not absence of a barrier.
- Sources have incentives: recruitment businesses market services; programmes and funders report selected outcomes; media favour newsworthy accounts; official releases emphasise defined indicators. None is free of framing or coverage limits.

## Provenance, methods and age

- Sources share publishers or underlying data. D02/D04 overlap; G01 shares MOM provenance. P02/P03 share CNA. Companion releases and methodology PDFs are not independent replications.
- P01 omits subgroup counts and several sampling details; P02 is a disclosed but nonprobability straw poll. P03/P04 are illustrative interviews, not frequency estimates.
- P05's exact publication date is unresolved. Its evaluation and underlying programme periods predate the preferred window; it is retained solely for relevant otherwise weakly represented contexts. Its overview/diagram do not fully reconcile interview counts. D05 also contains older observations despite publication in 2024.
- G02 has convenience/snowball recruitment and an age-description inconsistency. G01 does not disclose its cohort period/sample/method. These limitations restrict generalisation even where a passage is clearly attributable.
- Outcomes in D03 and D06 use specific cohorts and definitions. D06's newer headline cannot be compared directly with an earlier employed-only headline. D02's older caregiver total should not replace D04's newer observation.
- Some candidate pages were inaccessible; those snippets were not promoted. No chart-only finding or visual PDF layout claim was made. Original pages can change; this run preserves URLs, dates, locators and concise notes, not immutable full-source snapshots.

## Inferences explicitly withheld

Silence does not establish a fake vacancy or why an employer rejected someone. Later success after coaching does not establish coaching effectiveness. Long or repeated assessment does not establish that the effort was unnecessary or could have been avoided. A reported preference is not actual abandonment. A non-participating caregiver is not automatically searching or a prospective JobHub user. A stated care preference need not be free of constraints. Employment statistics do not identify discriminatory causes, and reported discrimination is not an adjudication of employer wrongdoing.

## Questions requiring direct validation

1. Within a recent real application, which steps consumed active time, waiting time and emotional effort? What was recalled versus supported by artifacts?
2. When did the person learn decisive requirements or conditions? Was the issue missing information, interpretation, actual incompatibility or exclusion?
3. What changed between applications, and what evidence informed that change? What was repeated without learning, and what repetition was necessary?
4. Who actually wanted and was available to work? How did care, income, transport and support constrain acceptable options without collecting unnecessary sensitive detail?
5. What happens for people using non-office, assisted, offline or non-English routes? Which assumptions from professional/graduate evidence fail?

The next useful activity is a separately approved consented episode-interview/workflow-observation study. No participant recruitment or outreach occurred in this run. A prototype becomes useful only after an interaction uncertainty is isolated; it is not evidence that the underlying problem has been prioritised.

## Revisit conditions

Revisit before selecting product scope, when direct user evidence conflicts with these hypotheses, or on 2027-01-18, whichever comes first. This date is a documentation reminder, not an automated schedule. Human acceptance of conclusions remains outstanding.

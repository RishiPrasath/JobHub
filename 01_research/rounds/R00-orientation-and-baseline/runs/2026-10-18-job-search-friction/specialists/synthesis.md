## Contradictions, differing populations and reporting issues

- D04/G01 describe broad non-participating populations, including people without current return intentions. G02 describes selected caregivers with constrained choices. These findings can coexist; neither proves all non-participation is voluntary nor that all caregivers unsuccessfully seek work.
- D03/D06 employment outcomes and P01–P04 difficult search accounts have different measures, cohorts and horizons. Eventual employment does not establish a low-burden process, and difficult accounts do not invalidate aggregate outcomes.
- D01's occupational differences preclude treating all vacancies or seekers as one market. Employer accounts of unattractive conditions do not establish that postings conceal those conditions.
- D05's declining overall reported discrimination and rising age-specific experience are different aggregations, not mutually exclusive trends.
- P01's headline/body unit mismatch and unexplained percentage sum, P05's interview-count ambiguity, and G02's age-description inconsistency are unresolved reporting issues. Do not silently repair them or repeat stronger headline interpretations.

No unresolved like-for-like contradiction establishes that one retained source must be discarded. Most apparent disagreement concerns population, timing, selection or measurement. These explanations limit comparison; they do not erase uncertainty.

## Three unranked validation hypotheses and evidence thresholds

The approved standard is two independent sources, at least one with a disclosed method. Apply it to a precise proposition, not to document counts or broad thematic similarity.

| Hypothesis | Threshold disposition | Residual unknown and suitable next activity |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **H1:** Some active seekers repeat preparation without enough feedback to decide what to change. | **Meets the minimum only for the underlying reported uncertainty; mechanism remains provisional.** P01/P02 are independent collections, and P02 discloses a bounded poll method. P03 adds reported changed tactics but shares CNA provenance. None establishes that feedback gaps caused repetition or measures avoidable burden. | Reconstruct two recent applications, the information available at each decision, actual revisions and reasons for them. Separate active work, elapsed waiting and recalled estimates. Do not invent rejection reasons or infer ghost vacancies from silence. |
| **H2:** Some applicants accumulate substantial assessment effort before learning an opportunity will not proceed. | **Below the standard for a leading burden hypothesis; retain as explicitly provisional.** P02/P04 provide two independent publishers' illustrative accounts, but no sufficiently described workload investigation supports “substantial.” P02's disclosed straw-poll method does not validate the interview-based assessment-burden claim. | Compare a progressed and a stopped application using a stage timeline; establish duration, requested tasks, feedback, decisions and what could reasonably have been known earlier. A later prospective diary may reduce recall error. Late rejection alone does not prove avoidable effort. |
| **H3:** For some active seekers or people intending to return, feasible work is constrained by conditions application preparation cannot resolve. | **Meets the minimum for a bounded problem-existence hypothesis.** Independent P05/G02 disclose qualitative methods and support constrained feasibility in their respective populations. D01 adds a different employer perspective. This is not a common prevalence estimate or evidence that earlier information would change outcomes. | Establish intention and availability first. Compare a pursued and a declined role: decisive conditions, when they became known, and whether the issue was incompatibility, missing information or interpretation. Do not treat caregiver population totals as a prospective-user count. |

The order is an identifier sequence, not a ranking. H1 and H3 have stronger support for their underlying problems than H2; none establishes product value, comparative severity, a feature requirement or a preferred segment.

## Assumptions, residual risks and next action

The synthesis assumes collectors accurately transcribed inspected source passages; no raw-data reanalysis or independent source replication was performed. The professional/degree emphasis remains substantial. Service, trades and operational worker voices, small employers, mid-career switching, disability, non-English contexts, digital confidence and device access remain insufficiently covered. Care-related application interruptions, actual abandonment, undisclosed job conditions and financial-pressure effects remain research questions.

The main risks are converting reported experience into prevalence, outcomes into causes, structural constraints into product promises, or shared provenance into corroboration. Preserve source periods and the distinction between strong evidence that a report exists and weak evidence for its mechanism, severity or remedy.

**Stopping-rule disposition:** stop desk collection now. Thirteen records are below the ceiling, the single targeted pass is complete, and further counts cannot supply direct comparative behavioural evidence by themselves. The run can deliver a useful qualified shortlist while explicitly leaving the greatest-burden question unresolved. Recommend future consented episode interviews and workflow observation; recruitment, participant contact and prototypes require a later human decision. No outreach or implementation is authorised here.

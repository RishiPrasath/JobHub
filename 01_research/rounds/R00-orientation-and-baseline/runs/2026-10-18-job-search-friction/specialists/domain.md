## Source register and pinpoint evidence

### D01 — Job Vacancies 2025

- Publisher: MOM Manpower Research and Statistics Department (MRSD). Published 20 March 2026. Accessed 18 October 2026.
- Inspected: [full report](https://stats.mom.gov.sg/iMAS_PdfLibrary/mrsd_JV2025.pdf); publication date corroborated by [release](https://www.mom.gov.sg/newsroom/press-releases/2026/0320-job-vacancies-report-2025).
- Method/population: Singapore Job Vacancy Survey; reference 30 September 2025; private establishments with at least 25 employees plus public sector. 16,910 responding establishments, 85.9% response; mail questionnaire, online/email returns and telephone clarification. PDF p.1 and technical notes p.25 (PDF indices 6 and 30).
- Evidence: sections 2.7–2.11, pp.5–7: long-unfilled vacancies declined overall, with non-PMET improvement but PMET deterioration. Employers attributed difficult operational recruitment to strenuous work, schedules, prospects or pay. These are employers' accounts of seekers' concerns. Section 2.20–2.21, p.11: experience requirements differ materially by occupation.
- Independence: one employer-survey family; press release is not corroboration independent of report.
- Confidence/limits: high for measured establishment responses; medium for explaining candidate choices; excludes smaller private employers. Does not establish listing omissions, application effort, individual suitability, or which search moment hurts most.

### D02 — Caregiving-related unemployment parliamentary answer

- Publisher: MOM. Published 25 September 2025. Accessed 18 October 2026.
- Inspected: [full answer](https://www.mom.gov.sg/newsroom/parliament-questions-and-replies/2025/0925-written-answer-to-pq-on-caregiving-related-unemployment), paragraphs 1–2.
- Data/population: 2024 Singapore residents outside the labour force because of caregiving; 87,100, of whom 85.7% were women. First-time caregiver count unavailable.
- Method: official administrative communication reporting labour-force statistics; answer itself does not disclose sampling details. Do not treat the headline's word “unemployment” as the statistical status of this group.
- Classification: source-reported population context, not evidence that these residents are actively seeking employment.
- Independence: MOM labour-force family; overlaps D04's retrospective 2024 figure. Do not count D02 and D04 as independent corroboration.
- Confidence/limits: high confidence in the reported figure; not the latest observation, since D04 supplies 2025 data. Does not measure time lost, return intentions, job-search steps, or the effectiveness of named services.

### D03 — Labour Market Report Second Quarter 2026

- Publisher: MOM MRSD. Published 21 September 2026. Accessed 18 October 2026.
- Inspected: [report](https://stats.mom.gov.sg/iMAS_PdfLibrary/mrsd-Labour-Market-Report-2Q-2026.pdf), pp.16–17, table 5.1 p.A12, explanatory notes pp.A25–A26; [release listing](https://stats.mom.gov.sg/SL/Pages/Re-entry-into-Employment-Data-and-Other-Resources.aspx?Flag=89&PageNo=1&TRMID=674) confirms publication date.
- Method: Labour Market Survey identifies retrenched residents from private establishments with at least 25 employees and public sector; administrative records track subsequent employment. Re-entry cohort count not extracted.
- Evidence: six-month re-entry was 54.9% in 2Q2026 versus 60.7% in 1Q; for ages 50–59 it was 41.9%. The 2Q rate tracks residents retrenched in 4Q2025. The separate twelve-month measure covers a different cohort and was 69.8%.
- Classification: source-reported outcome indicator; high confidence within its definition.
- Independence: MOM survey/administrative family, distinct instrument from D04 but same publisher.
- Limits: omits self/informal employment and training; departures from labour force and cohort composition can affect rates. Does not measure uninterrupted unemployment, hours searching, or causal workflow friction. Do not equate non-re-entry with inability to find work.

### D04 — Labour Force in Singapore 2025

- Publisher: MOM MRSD. Published 29 January 2026; accessed 18 October 2026.
- Inspected: [publication page](https://stats.mom.gov.sg/Pages/Labour-Force-In-Singapore-2025.aspx), [findings](https://stats.mom.gov.sg/iMAS_Tables1/LabourForce/LabourForce_2025/mrsd_2025LabourForce_survey_findings.pdf), [methodology](https://stats.mom.gov.sg/iMAS_Tables1/LabourForce/LabourForce_2025/mrsd_2025LabourForce_coverage_methodology.pdf).
- Method/population: stratified systematic household sample; 33,000 selected dwelling units, 27,324 responding households, 86.8% response after ineligible units excluded. Fieldwork March–July 2025; population weighting June 2025. Telephone/internet with face-to-face follow-up.
- Evidence: findings sections 8.3–8.8, pp.76–78: 72,500 residents outside labour force mainly for caregiving in 2025, down from 87,100 in 2024; 94.5% preferred personally providing family care. Caregiver profiles differ by care recipient. Discouraged workers numbered 7,400 (0.3%).
- Classification: source-reported population context; high confidence in reported measures, subject to survey error.
- Independence: one household-survey family; D02 repeats its earlier-year series. Methodology is a companion, not another substantive record.
- Limits: caregiving status is not a measure of involuntary exclusion or desire to return. Low aggregate discouragement does not invalidate difficulties among active seekers. No causal evidence that flexible-work availability caused changes.

### D05 — Fair Employment Practices 2023

- Publisher: MOM MRSD. Published 24 September 2024; accessed 18 October 2026.
- Inspected: [full report](https://www.mom.gov.sg/-/media/mom/documents/press-releases/2024/fair-employment-practices-report-2023.pdf); publication date from [release](https://www.mom.gov.sg/newsroom/press-releases/2024/0924-sustained-improvements-in-workplace-fairness-standards-since-2018).
- Method: supplementary survey July–September 2023 about July 2022–June 2023; random resident labour-force sample age 15+, excluding full-time national servicemen. 3,480 of 4,090 responded (85%). Mail questionnaire with multiple return modes. Annex B pp.20–22.
- Evidence: sections 2.2.1–2.2.4, pp.10–12: 23.4% of job seekers reported discrimination (95% interval 20.4–26.4); age most common (18.1%). Among those reporting discrimination, 45.7% described demographic preferences in advertisements and 28.3% irrelevant personal questions. Overall incidence declined while age incidence increased.
- Classification/confidence: source-reported experiences; high confidence in survey estimate, not adjudicated wrongdoing.
- Independence: distinct supplementary survey, still same MOM publisher. Older data are retained because they directly locate reported barriers within search steps.
- Limits: retrospective, self-reported; not current 2026 prevalence. Disability responses insufficient for a job-search estimate. Multiple answers permitted; subgroup percentages cannot be applied to all seekers. No time/severity comparison.

### D06 — Joint Autonomous Universities Graduate Employment Survey 2025 release

- Publisher: six autonomous universities, hosted by SUSS. Published 5 March 2026; accessed 18 October 2026.
- Inspected: [joint release](https://www.suss.edu.sg/about-suss/media-centres/media-releases/more-support-for-graduates-in-seeking-employment-as-employment-outcomes-dip), paragraphs 1, 3 and footnotes.
- Method/population: November 2025 survey, approximately six months after final examinations; 14,397 respondents, 73.7% response. Singapore autonomous-university graduates, not all school leavers.
- Evidence: 88.9% of labour-force graduates had secured employment, versus 91.2% in 2024. New headline category includes employment, accepted offers awaiting start, and active business setup; do not compare it to an older employed-only headline. Separate administrative wage evidence found 95% coverage at 12 months for 2024 and 2023 cohorts.
- Classification: source-reported outcome snapshots; high confidence in reported definitions, not evidence of application friction.
- Independence: university survey family, independent instrument/publisher from MOM household evidence; administrative wage comparison is a different measure within release.
- Limits: nonresponse, institution coverage, outcome definition and time horizon matter. Wage-record absence can include overseas work, self-employment and study. No application counts, time costs, rejection causes, or course-level comparison extracted. Data tables embedded as images were not used; retained claims are from inspected text.

## Findings and domain synthesis

The sources support differentiated barriers and transition outcomes, but do not establish a ranking of search stages by time, confidence or opportunity loss. Five records originate from MOM; different instruments reduce duplication but do not provide five independent institutional perspectives. The strongest evidence directly locating a barrier in a workflow is D05, although its reference period is older. D01 supplies essential non-professional context through employers, while D03 and D06 concern outcomes rather than causes.

## Evidence-aware workflow map

This is a research scaffold, not an observed universal sequence. People can loop, pause, use referrals or receive direct offers.

| Proposed moment | Evidence connection | What remains unestablished |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Decide whether and when to search | D04 contextualises constraints before active searching | Whether a specific caregiver wants paid work; readiness and support needs |
| Discover and screen opportunities | D01 gives occupational requirements; D05 locates some reported exclusion in advertisements | Channel mix, search time, missing listing information, representativeness of any portal |
| Judge fit and feasible working conditions | D01 suggests occupation-specific constraints worth checking | Whether uncertainty or actual unattractive conditions drive rejection |
| Prepare and submit an application | D05 locates irrelevant personal requests in the reported experience | Repeated entry, CV effort, application volume, mobile or language barriers |
| Interview, await a decision, follow up | D05 includes reported interview experiences; other retained evidence does not isolate these steps | Feedback delays, ghosting, assessment burden, emotional trajectory |
| Accept/start, retrain, pause or resume | D03/D06 demonstrate why timing and status definitions matter | Causal route to the outcome; whether assistance changes it |

## Segment coverage, assumptions and unresolved questions

- Fresh graduates: D06 offers autonomous-university outcomes only. Polytechnic, ITE, private-institution, disability and nonresident graduate experiences remain unmeasured here.
- Mature/retrenched workers: D03 identifies outcome variation; D05 provides historical self-reports. Neither establishes discrimination as the cause of D03 outcomes.
- Mid-career switchers: requirements in D01 are relevant context, but no retained direct switcher workflow study. Transferable-skill interpretation is a hypothesis.
- Returners: D02/D04 contextualise caregiving; do not convert this population automatically into prospective users. Coordinator has reserved the targeted returner gap pass.
- Service/trades/operations: D01 includes drivers, construction labourers, waiters, retail assistants and cooks. Small firms, informal recruitment and worker voices remain gaps.
- Across groups: English-only discovery, accessibility, digital confidence, device use, transport costs, financial pressure and emotional burden are insufficiently covered.

## Provisional hypotheses and recommended next action

1. Some seekers expend effort evaluating roles that later prove infeasible on working conditions. D01 motivates this question; it does not show information was missing or discovered late. Ask participants to reconstruct the last accepted and rejected opportunity, with concrete evidence of timing and costs.
2. Some mature seekers face exclusion during advertising or screening that application optimisation cannot resolve. D05 motivates studying the experience; current incidence and available remedies need separate evidence. Avoid collecting unnecessary sensitive details or making employer accusations.
3. Some prolonged transitions contain multiple distinct statuses and choices that an undifferentiated “unemployed” label obscures. D03/D06 motivate a status-aware interview timeline, but do not show that tracking tools improve outcomes.

These are domain contributions for integration, not the final research shortlist. No claim meets the full two-independent-source standard for a specific product-solvable workflow problem within this handoff alone. Recommend interviews and retrospective workflow observation after human selection, rather than feature selection or prototyping from these statistics.

## Risks and trade-offs

Official sources improve denominator and methodological clarity but underdescribe lived experience. Strong outcomes evidence can tempt causal claims it cannot support. Public-sector policy narratives and university communications also have institutional incentives. The desk study should preserve these limits while using the sources to recruit varied contexts and ask better questions.

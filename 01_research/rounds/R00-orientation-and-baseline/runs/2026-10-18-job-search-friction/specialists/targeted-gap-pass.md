## G01 — Caregiver return-to-work denominator and measurement gap

- Title: Written Answer to PQ on Time Taken for Caregivers to Return to Workforce.
- Publisher: Singapore Ministry of Manpower (MOM).
- URL: https://www.mom.gov.sg/newsroom/parliament-questions-and-replies/2026/0302-written-answer-to-pq-on-time-taken-for-caregivers-to-return-to-workforce
- Published: 2026-03-02. Accessed: 2026-10-18.
- Scope/method: Singapore residents outside the labour force primarily due to caregiving; parliamentary answer summarising available data. Cohort dates, sample and estimation method are not disclosed. The question asks about 2025 but the reply does not identify the data period of its two-year statistic.
- Location: answer paragraphs 1–2.
- Source-reported claim: MOM lacks elapsed-time data from the end of caregiving to workforce return. About one-quarter returned within two years; about eight in ten non-returners did not intend to seek employment.
- Interpretation: non-return is not equivalent to failed job seeking. The denominator must distinguish current intention, availability and continuing care.
- Limitations/independence: official descriptive context, not workflow or causal evidence; shares the MOM institutional family with domain records. No claim that family-care preference is unconstrained.

## G02 — Caregiving constraints in a qualitative Singapore study

- Title: Chapter 16, Sandwiched Responsibilities: Needs and Challenges of Malay Women Caring for Younger and Older Dependants in Low-Income Households, by Noor Aisha Binte Abdul Rahman and Ad Maulod; in Proceedings of the NUS SSR-TOUCH Conference 2025.
- Publisher: NUS Social Service Research Centre and TOUCH Community Services.
- URL: https://fass.nus.edu.sg/ssr/wp-content/uploads/sites/8/2026/03/SSR_TOUCH_Proceedings_Final-1.pdf
- Published: 2026-03-31 (front matter); conference 2025-11-11/12. Accessed: 2026-10-18.
- Scope/method: Queenstown-linked Malay women caring for younger and older dependants; 40 interviews, July 2024–March 2025, convenience/snowball recruitment; three focus groups involving nine service stakeholders. Eligibility stated ages 45–65 and household income at most S$6,000. The later participant description uses ages 40–59; retain this reporting inconsistency rather than resolving it.
- Location: printed pp.98–103 (PDF indices 105–110); method p.100, employment barriers p.102. Text inspected, not visual layout audited.
- Source-reported claim: care demands constrained employment; some participants wanted paid work but had little practical choice. Skills concerns and fear of losing assistance also discouraged return.
- Interpretation: identifying feasible work requires understanding care and income constraints; extra application assistance alone may not address them.
- Limitations/independence: geographically and demographically specific qualitative convenience sample, no prevalence or tool-effect estimate. Academic/community research family independent of MOM and recruiter polls. Benefit concerns are participant reports, not verified entitlement rules.

# Job-Seeker Practices Researcher handoff

Specialist: Zeno. Access date throughout: 2026-10-18. Read-only public research. Source claims below are not accepted JobHub product conclusions.

## Source records

- P01: [Wiles, Munyikwa and Horton, Management Science](https://pubsonline.informs.org/doi/10.1287/mnsc.2024.04528), published 2025-04-18; older foundational experiment/2023 precursor. Publisher abstract and metadata inspected. Nearly half a million online-labour-market entrants; randomized nongenerative resume-writing assistance. Authors report 8% more hiring and 10% higher wages. Strong bounded causal evidence; cannot transfer to current generative tools, automated applications, or permanent employment. Coordinator independently reopened abstract.
- P02: [Cui, Dias and Ye, Signaling in the Age of AI](https://arxiv.org/html/2509.25054v2), preprint posted 2025-09-29/revised 2025-11-12; rendered manuscript displays 2026-08-24. Older January-September 2023 Freelancer.com data: 5.50 million bids, 264,082 bidders, 106,714 PHP/internet-marketing jobs; estimation sample smaller. Difference-in-differences access effect: callback increase 0.43 percentage points, significant at 10% but not 5%; fades after two months; hiring estimates inconclusive. Editing time correlates with outcomes, not randomized. Coordinator reopened full text. Version/date discrepancy retained.
- P03: [PeopleScout, Truth About Gen AI and Job Seekers](https://www.peoplescout.com/insights/truth-gen-ai-job-seekers/), 2025 report; exact publication/fieldwork dates unavailable. 1,000 UK people who changed jobs within 12 months; 18% reported personal GenAI use. Successful-job-changer selection; full form-gated report not obtained. Self-report, not national adoption or observed disclosure. Coordinator reopened public findings.
- P04: [Express/Harris Poll release](https://www.expresspros.com/newsroom/news-releases/news-releases/2025/04/62-percent-of-us-job-seekers-would-consider-not-applying-to-companies-using-generative-ai-during-the-hiring-process), 2025-04-23. Online US survey, 1,039 adults, 2024-11-21 to 2024-12-10. 78% consider drafting and mock-question generation appropriate; 62% would consider avoiding employers overly reliant on GenAI. Attitudes/hypothetical withdrawal, not use or measured abandonment. Eligibility/weighting incompletely disclosed; hiring-manager sample is separate.
- P05: [Indeed/YouGov Australian report](https://d341ezm4iqaae0.cloudfront.net/assets/sites/6/2025/05/15084010/IndeedAU-Take-the-guesswork-out-of-hiring-Final.pdf), 2025; exact publication/fieldwork dates unavailable. Methodology p.4; findings pp.8-11. 1,027 adults aged 18-64 excluding self-employed; weighted. 71% used or encountered AI in search/hiring; this mixes personal use and exposure. 24% reported abandoning a long application. Self-report; no causal attribution to AI. Population labels and a legacy footnote are inconsistent.
- P06: [Imteyaz et al., blind/low-vision job seekers](https://arxiv.org/html/2601.11884v2), posted 2026-01-17/revised 2026-04-19; fieldwork June-September 2024, just outside priority window. Seventeen English-speaking participants recruited through community/social/snowball channels; remote interviews and thematic analysis. Describes accessibility workarounds, human checking, and selective avoidance. Qualitative evidence, not prevalence; geography inadequate for national comparison. Coordinator reopened full text.
- P07: [First-person engineering-management job-search account](https://www.reddit.com/r/jobsearchhacks/comments/1pp94c3/stats_from_my_job_search_2025/), 2025-12-17. One anonymous author reports spreadsheet tracking, AI reconciliation, failed Notion integration, and discontinued per-job tailoring. Unverified records/geography; uncontrolled resume comparisons do not establish benefit or harm.
- P08: [First-person prolonged-search account](https://www.reddit.com/r/jobsearchhacks/comments/1pu9k8k/finally_got_a_job_so_im_sharing_some_tips/), 2025-12-24 UTC. One anonymous author describes manual application of AI suggestions, edited drafts, spoken rehearsal, spreadsheet tracking, saved postings, fatigue, and breaks. Anecdotal; job offer cannot be attributed to AI.

## Interpretation

Writing help has some bounded outcome evidence. Rehearsal, tracking, verification, and abandonment have useful reported examples, but the sample does not establish that an end-to-end AI-managed hunt improves employment outcomes. Planning and factual verification remain undermeasured.

US acceptability, UK personal use, and Australian use-or-exposure are different constructs. Do not rank countries or create an adoption average. Freelance digital roles and English-language professional accounts leave frontline, non-English, and low-digital-confidence users underrepresented.

Recommendation: use interviews/diaries and realistic tasks to measure what people actually check, correct, reuse, and abandon. Track usable outputs and sustained use, not just application counts.

# UX, accessibility and product-surface choice

Date: 2026-10-18. Reviewed desk synthesis; platform selection remains open.

The [UX report](../../03_research-rounds/00_orientation-and-baseline/runs/2026-10-18-remaining-research/ux-and-accessibility.md) compares desktop-focused, responsive, mobile-first, PWA, native, extension and combined approaches. It includes the capture → save → interrupt → return → assess → prepare → user-confirmed application journey, with missing data, duplicates, source changes, sync failures and recovery.

Recommendation: compare a responsive prototype and a capture-companion concept after checking the underlying user problem. A native or installed surface must justify its added maintenance with an observed need and demonstrated device capability. Do not require a laptop merely because long-form preparation is commonly imagined on one.

Design for plain labels, a clear next action, visible evidence and correctable uncertainty. Saving, exporting and applying are different states. Avoid a dense dashboard before testing a simpler job page. Show whether information is saved locally, pending sync or available across devices.

The report maps selected WCAG 2.2 criteria and gives keyboard, screen-reader, focus, reflow, contrast, motion and recovery checks. This is not conformance testing. Standards, provider capability documentation and historical platform announcements cannot establish usability on actual devices.

Owner learning should include drawing alternative flows, writing errors/empty states, testing keyboard/text enlargement and observing consented sessions without coaching. AI can critique and explain alternatives, not replace participant evidence.

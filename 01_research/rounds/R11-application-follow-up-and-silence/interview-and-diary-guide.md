# Interview and diary-test guide: following up and silence

Status: **draft for the owner to confirm.** Every threshold below is a proposal. Nothing here has been run, and no feasibility is claimed. Desk research in R11 is capped at N2; this guide is how the first user test can reach N3 on the FEAT-012 question (ASM-012: people would keep a tracker up to date).

## Who to recruit (8 to 10 people)

Singapore job seekers who applied for at least 3 jobs in the last 2 months. Aim for spread across the three v1 groups, because R11 found almost no evidence for the third:

| Group | Target | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Early-career (0 to 3 years) | 3 | Highest application volume in the evidence; tracking effort drives abandonment |
| Mid-career switchers, including anyone on SkillsFuture Jobseeker Support | 3 | Tracking may double as proof for the scheme (unverified) |
| Lower-wage, shift or gig workers | 2 to 4 | No evidence found on how they follow up; contact is often phone or WhatsApp |

If the same people are being recruited for E21 (interview preparation) or other first-test sessions, run this guide in the same session.

## Consent and data

- Plain-language consent before starting; participants may skip any question or stop.
- Do not record employer names unless the participant chooses to. Use "a retail employer" style labels in notes.
- Do not collect NRIC/FIN, date of birth or other items JobHub's profile deliberately does not collect.
- Store notes without names; keep the consent record separate. Delete raw audio after transcription.
- Any incentive is the owner's decision.

## Part A: interview (30 to 40 minutes)

Warm-up
1. Tell me about your job search so far: when you started, roughly how many applications.

Walk through the last application (use a real one; ask them to open their phone, email or tracker)
2. Where did you apply, and how? Where did you record it, if anywhere?
3. What has happened since? How do you know?
4. When did you last check? What did you check, and how?

Keeping track
5. Show me where you keep track. What do you write down? What do you leave out?
6. Think of an application you lost track of. What happened?
7. Have you ever stopped keeping a list? Why?

Silence
8. Think of an employer that never replied. What did you do?
9. Did you follow up? When, how (email, phone, WhatsApp, walk-in), and what happened?
10. How did you decide to stop waiting? Was there a day or a feeling?
11. How did it affect you? (Ask gently; allow skipping.)
12. What would you want an employer or a tool to tell you?

Phone and channel (especially group 3)
13. Do employers contact you by phone, WhatsApp, SMS or email? How do you keep those messages organised?

Scheme use (group 2)
14. Do you need to show anyone your applications? What do you show, and how?

## Part B: two-week diary (optional, 5 to 6 participants)

Each person logs on their own phone (message to a number, a form or a shared note, their choice). Per application: date, role type (no employer name needed), where applied, status in their own words. Per check or follow-up: date, what they did, what they found. A once-a-day tap rating of how they feel about waiting (1 to 5), optional.

Capture without prompting beyond one reminder a day. What matters is what they keep up, not what we can make them do.

## Part C: concept reactions (10 minutes, after Parts A and B)

Show plain paper or static cards (not a working product) for each candidate approach, and ask "would you use this, what would worry you?":

1. A simple list of your applications with a status you set.
2. A line saying how long this employer usually takes to reply, with its source (for example the portal's own label).
3. A reminder that you chose to set, at a number of days you choose.
4. A follow-up message you can copy and send yourself.
5. A "closed, no reply" button that records it and lets you note why you moved on.

Test wording on card 5 with three versions, one per participant order, and note which feels fair and which feels like a verdict: "No reply yet", "Closed: no reply", "Employer ghosted you".

## What to record

- Tools actually used and what is written down.
- Applications that were lost, duplicated or forgotten, and what it cost.
- How follow-up was done, and any reply it produced.
- The moment and reason someone gave up.
- Reactions and objections to each concept card; any wording that read as a verdict.
- For group 3, the channel (phone, WhatsApp, SMS, walk-in) and how it was kept.

## Proposed pass and fail thresholds (for the owner to confirm)

| Question | Proposed pass | Proposed fail |
|---|---|---|
| Is there a real unmet need? | At least half name an application they lost track of or could not say the status of | Under a quarter |
| Would a light list be kept up? (diary) | At least a third log at least 70% of their applications over two weeks | Under a quarter log at least half |
| Does a closure step help? | At least a third say recording "no reply" would help them move on | Most say it would not matter, or find it a verdict |
| Does any wording read as a verdict? | None of the three wordings is read as a verdict by most | "Employer ghosted you" or similar is seen as fair by most (it should not be used) |
| Do lower-wage, shift or gig participants fit the same pattern? | Needs and channels match the other groups | Most use phone or WhatsApp with nothing a list could capture |

These are small-sample signals, not statistics. They are to be read as direction for N3, not as proof.

## Limits

Eight to ten people cannot show prevalence. Self-reported diaries drift. Recruiting from JobHub's own network may bias towards motivated, organised seekers. The paper concepts test attitude, not use.

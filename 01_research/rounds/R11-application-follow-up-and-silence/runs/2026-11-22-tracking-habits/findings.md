# Run A findings: How job seekers track applications and check back for replies

Run date: 2026-11-22. Role: Job-Seeker Practices Researcher (#04). Read-only desk pass on public pages. No logins, accounts, form submissions or messages. Note: the research subagent was not allowed to write files, so this file was saved by the round lead from its returned report. A temporary PDF text extract was kept in the scratchpad only.

Evidence labels as in the [round brief](../../round-brief.md). R09 G2 already covers ghosting rates, time-to-hire and drop-out reasons, so none of that is repeated here.

**Scope.** About 11 searches, 12 pages fetched and read. Blocked or empty (not bypassed): Teal help page, Independent.sg (two stories), Monster, Pew and a Tufts search hit (403 or similar); Reddit fetch refused by the tool; two JobStreet community thread URLs returned only an advice hub. The Berkeley PDF and the MyCareersFuture FAQ PDF were read with pdftotext.

**Main limit.** No Reddit thread (r/singaporejobs, r/jobs, r/cscareerquestions) was read. Forum-type evidence is one Levels.fyi thread plus search summaries. Singapore-specific evidence on tracking behaviour is very thin.

## Findings

**F1. Spreadsheets and notes dominate advice, but no usage share was found.** No credible survey gave the share using spreadsheets, apps, email folders or memory. Career-centre and vendor pages recommend a spreadsheet by default (Tufts; Jobscan, FlexJobs, ClickUp, The Muse via snippets). Tufts calls sticky notes tempting but less reliable. V-3p (Tufts), 2nd (snippets). Confidence: high for "no share found", low for any prevalence claim.

**F2. Fields people record are consistent across sources.** Berkeley interviews: job title, company, location, URL, stage or status, whether there was a referral, notes; some also save the tailored resume or cover letter used. Tufts: organisation, position, dates, employer response notes, contacts, source, URLs, and advice to save the job description because postings can disappear. A Levels.fyi poster records date applied, follow-up date (about two weeks later), resume version, source. V-3p. Confidence: medium; small, US and tech-skewed samples.

**F3. People track to avoid duplicate applications and to learn from their own data.** Berkeley participants wanted to avoid applying twice and see averages (rejection time, number applied, screening time). V-3p (13 interviews, 20 survey responses, convenience sample). Confidence: low to medium.

**F4. Trackers are abandoned because of the manual work.** Berkeley: "the majority" tracked and updated stages, "some stopped... after a while" because of upkeep, and nearly all asked for automation. A Levels.fyi spreadsheet user says adding columns does not help; dates and outcomes matter more. V-3p. Confidence: medium on direction, low on size. The Berkeley team was building a tracker (design-research bias).

**F5. Volume sets the limit on spreadsheets.** Huntr Q3 2026: median 2.5 applications in a usual week for the tracked group (n=4,456). Huntr 2025 annual report: median about 16 a week, 75th percentile 40, 90th about 85. Berkeley respondents: 25 to 50 a week. The two Huntr reports measure different things; both are vendor data, US and tech-skewed, self-logged. Singapore: NTU HEY! Magazine (21 Jan 2026) says finance or tech students may submit "up to 100 applications" (no source named); a retail seeker applied to over 30 roles and another to 3 to 5 jobs a day (Independent.sg, summaries only). V-3p for Huntr, Berkeley, NTU; 2nd for Independent.sg. Confidence: medium for ranges, low for any "typical Singapore" figure.

**F6. Blogs and vendors describe losing track at around 20 to 30 applications.** A DEV Community post describes 47 applications across 6 spreadsheets plus sticky notes, undefined statuses ("vibes", "maybe?"), a tab of "applied but no portal confirmation", and a mis-logged offer deadline that led to a rescinded offer; the author then promoted his own tracker. Search summaries of vendor posts say spreadsheets are tedious around 15 and "broken" at 30+, with no data. V-3p (single anecdote) and 2nd. Confidence: low.

**F7. Portal "applied" lists exist in Singapore, but status depends on the employer.** MyCareersFuture user FAQ PDF: applications appear under "Applied Jobs", most recent first; "once the employer downloads your resume, the job application status will be updated as 'Under Review'." The status table did not extract. A search summary lists four statuses (Received, Under Review, Successful, Unsuccessful) - 2nd. The FAQ is undated. No employer obligation to update the status, or push notification, was confirmed. No help-page evidence on what "applied" means on JobStreet or LinkedIn in Singapore. V-site for quoted lines. Confidence: high that the list exists, low on status semantics.

**F8. Mid-career seekers on SkillsFuture Jobseeker Support have an institutional reason to log applications.** The SWDA page (updated 1 Sep 2026): participants earn points for job-search activities, with "submitting job applications" as an example; payouts depend on monthly minimum points over 6 months. No point values or evidence requirements on the page. Search snippets elsewhere say each resume sent via MyCareersFuture or other portals earns one point, with caps, and one provider asks for screenshots of dashboard records (not opened; 2nd). V-3p for SWDA. Confidence: medium that a recording obligation exists, low on its form. Interpretation: tracking here is partly proof for the scheme.

**F9. Singapore guidance puts the follow-up burden on the seeker, with no tracking system.** JobStreet Singapore (updated 30 Oct 2026): wait 1 to 2 weeks if the ad gives no timeframe; following up within 3 days "can backfire"; prefers email, phone only if no email; WhatsApp not mentioned; one polite follow-up is acceptable, then "move on rather than sending repeated messages". No tracking method beyond noting the application date. A search summary of a MyCareersFuture article says it may be time to move on if nothing a month after both application and follow-up (not opened; 2nd). V-3p for JobStreet. Confidence: medium.

**F10. Lower-wage and shift seekers: contact by phone and WhatsApp, but only anecdotal evidence.** Search summaries (Independent.sg, a Reddit post reported there): a retail seeker applying to 30+ roles had some recruiters ask for a resume by WhatsApp and most never followed up. One Singapore cleaner ad on Indeed asks applicants to WhatsApp for interview arrangements; a Grab Jobs page promotes walk-in interviews. No study of Singapore lower-wage seekers' tracking was found. Pew 2015 (US, search summary only, page 403): 38% of smartphone job seekers had trouble entering large amounts of text and 37% had trouble submitting documents; those without college experience were more likely to use their phone for advanced tasks. Old US source; says nothing directly about tracking. 2nd. Confidence: low.

**F11. Silence is the main reason people check back.** Huntr Q3 2026 survey: 37% (100 of 271) said employers often or very often stopped responding after an interview. In open answers, 50% of 272 asked for a response or a reason and 43% wanted any response, even a rejection ("A no is better than nothing at all."). Huntr 2025 says "nearly 90%" report being left without closure after interviews (page text truncated; wording and base not verified). This covers silence after interviews, not how people check back after applying. V-3p; self-selected vendor surveys, 71% US. Confidence: medium on direction. G2 covers Singapore rates.

**F12. Mid-career, early-career and gig groups differ in what they need tracked.** Berkeley respondents were early-career tech seekers who mass-apply, use LinkedIn as a hub, rely on employer career sites, and use forums, email and Slack. NTU HEY! says the hiring process alone can take 3 to 6 months; a coach describes a student who followed up after interviews to ask for feedback rather than chase. No Singapore source describes how mid-career switchers track; evidence is WSG Careers Connect coaching context (2nd) and F8. V-3p. Confidence: low.

## Patterns by user group

| Group | What the evidence shows | Interpretation (I) |
|---|---|---|
| Early-career | High volume (Berkeley 25 to 50 a week; NTU up to 100 in popular fields). Applications spread across portals, career sites, email, Slack. Fields: company, role, URL, status, referral, notes. Some quit trackers over upkeep. | The need is real, but manual upkeep drives abandonment. |
| Mid-career switchers | Little direct evidence. SkillsFuture Jobseeker Support ties payouts to logged activity. Coaching suggests a structured routine (WSG snippet). | Tracking may be proof as much as organisation for scheme participants. Untested. |
| Lower-wage, shift or gig | No tracking data. Single anecdotes of WhatsApp, phone and walk-ins. US data says phone-based applying is common and typing and uploading are hard. | Probably not a spreadsheet, but no evidence either way. |

## What breaks, ranked by how often evidenced

1. Manual upkeep and abandoning the tracker (Berkeley, Levels.fyi; vendor posts low confidence).
2. Losing the posting or application trail (Tufts, DEV post, Berkeley): the ad is taken down or no confirmation arrives.
3. Scattered sources and duplicates (Berkeley, DEV post).
4. Messy statuses (DEV post only).
5. Missed follow-up or deadline (DEV post, Levels.fyi follow-up columns); one real case, a single anecdote.
6. Lost emails (Tufts; one mention).
7. Silence itself (Huntr, G2). It is the reason people check back, not a tracking failure; a tracker cannot fix it.

## What could NOT be found

- Any survey of how many job seekers use spreadsheets, apps, email folders or memory, in Singapore or anywhere.
- Any Reddit thread content.
- Singapore-specific accounts of tracking or checking back, other than search summaries of two Independent.sg stories.
- How often people update or check.
- What "applied" means on LinkedIn or JobStreet Singapore, or whether platforms push status updates.
- Exact MyCareersFuture status descriptions, and whether employers must update statuses.
- How lower-wage, shift or gig seekers in Singapore log or follow up; nothing on Telegram or SMS.
- What proof SkillsFuture Jobseeker Support requires, beyond one provider's screenshot claim.
- Any abandonment rate for tracking tools.

## Limitations and assumptions

- Desk research shows what people report and what vendors and career pages say, not what people do. AI-gathered, so capped at N2.
- Most tracking evidence is US or tech-oriented, from vendors and student projects, self-selected.
- Several Singapore items are search summaries of pages that could not be fetched, treated as 2nd.
- Huntr data comes from people who already use a tracker; it cannot show how non-users behave.
- The MyCareersFuture status list and the SkillsFuture points rules come from summaries, not read pages.
- The Tufts page date (2024) was assumed correct over the URL (2023).

## Implications for the FEAT-012 candidate approaches (observations, not decisions)

- **Plain status list.** The need to record stage, company, URL, date applied and notes is consistently evidenced. The main failure is the effort of keeping it updated, so a light-effort list fits the evidence better than a rich one. The Berkeley sample is small and US-tech, so fit for Singapore early-career, mid-career and lower-wage users is unproven. Messy statuses appear in one anecdote only.
- **Expected wait.** MyCareersFuture already shows reply times per employer (R10), and its "Applied Jobs" status depends on the employer. Singapore guidance suggests waiting 1 to 2 weeks before following up (JobStreet) and "a month" before moving on (MyCareersFuture, summary). No evidence on whether people use a wait estimate. Huntr's median wait from application to interview is 5 days, but self-logged, mostly US, and only covers applications that got an interview.
- **Give-up rule.** Both Singapore sources advise one polite follow-up then moving on. No evidence on how people actually decide to stop waiting, or that a user-set rule would be used. Run C covers this.
- **Follow-up templates.** JobStreet gives a Singapore template structure (position, date applied, up to three skills, ask next steps), email preferred. WhatsApp is not covered, though one anecdote and one ad suggest employers use it for lower-wage roles. No evidence templates are used or help. Run C covers this.
- **Closure step.** Huntr answers (43% wanting any response, even a rejection) show closure is wanted, but employers supply it, not seekers. A user-recorded "no reply" gives the seeker an ending, not employer feedback. No evidence yet it would be used or valued.
- **Cross-cutting.** Trackers are abandoned over manual work (F4). Silence itself cannot be fixed by a tracker, matching FEAT-012's S-20 note. Mid-career seekers on SkillsFuture Jobseeker Support have an outside reason to record applications (F8). Whether a phone-first Singapore list would suit lower-wage seekers using WhatsApp or walk-ins remains open for real-user testing.

# Job-Seeker Practices Researcher

## Mission

Understand how real job seekers plan, search, prepare, apply, and cope with the job hunt in everyday life.

## Scope

Study people who are unemployed, underemployed, retrenched, changing careers, inexperienced, time-constrained, or unfamiliar with advanced AI workflows. Study their use of general-purpose AI and dedicated tools.

## Non-goals

Do not assume stated preferences equal observed behaviour, or equate more applications with better outcomes.

## Inputs and outputs

Inputs include interviews, diary-style accounts, observed workflows, public discussions, tools, and internal experience. Outputs include behaviour maps, pain points, workarounds, abandoned steps, trust issues, and opportunity areas.

## Evidence rules

Separate reported behaviour, observed behaviour, interpretation, and proposed intervention. Protect personal information and avoid presenting anecdotes as population-level evidence.

## Handoffs

Send behavioural findings to the Product Strategist, UX Designer, and AI SDLC Advisor when they affect workflow design or human-AI interaction.

## Current practice notes

- Study actual behaviour, not only stated preferences or tool marketing.
- Compare AI use across discovery, resume work, cover letters, interviews, research, and tracking.
- Record verification behaviour: what users check, trust, edit, or submit unchanged.
- Include emotional effort, uncertainty, rejection, time pressure, and abandonment.
- Treat survey and vendor reports as directional evidence whose methods and population must be checked.

# What the research suggests

Useful support may reduce uncertainty and repeated effort.

## People need help judging opportunities

Actual duties, working conditions and verified experience matter alongside job titles.

## Preparation includes checking and recovery

Re-entry, corrections, scheduling and unfinished work add to the writing itself.

## Truthful reuse is worth testing

Real examples can support resumes and interview practice without invented achievements.

## The simplest useful workflow remains open

Manual documents, peers and career coaching offer meaningful alternatives.

Synthesis of reported mechanisms. Their prevalence and JobHub benefit remain unmeasured.

# Red flags become verification questions

Specific checks make concerns more useful.

| Signal or uncertainty | Question or check |
| --- | --- |
| Unclear recruiter identity | Can the organisation independently confirm the contact and vacancy? |
| Vague or shifting duties | What work will I do, and how will success be assessed? |
| Pressure or substantial unpaid work | What is the deadline, assessment scope and reason? |
| Repeated listings or hostile language | What explains the hiring history, expectations and working conditions? |

Research-derived guidance. A phrase, repeated listing or polished website cannot establish employer intent.

# Job fit needs several kinds of evidence

A low keyword score can hide relevant experience.

## Demonstrated experience

Which real tasks or outcomes support the requirement?

## Transferable skills

How does that experience apply in the new setting?

## A real gap or missing information

Does the person lack the skill, or does the record need clarification?

## Essential criteria and personal conditions

Verify requirements, shifts, location and constraints before judging suitability.

Research-derived guidance. No validated fit score or individual eligibility assessment resulted from this research.

# Negotiation depends on the opportunity

A prepared choice can include accepting, countering or declining.

## Preparation

- Find comparable evidence for the role
- Clarify what can change in the package
- Understand the deadline and alternatives
- Prepare a supported request

## Conditions that change the advice

- Fixed bands and standardised scales
- Variable pay or contract arrangements
- Urgency and bargaining position
- Tolerance for losing the offer

Research-derived guidance. No universal uplift or risk-free negotiation script is established.

# Interview preparation and practice

Guidance recommends explaining real experience and rehearsing aloud.

## Preparation material

- Role and employer research with sources
- A small bank of real experience stories
- Questions about duties and expectations
- Interview format and practical arrangements

## Practice and return

- Rehearse aloud with a peer, coach or tool
- Record what the attempt demonstrated
- Revisit weak examples without inventing results
- Resume with one clear next session

Research-derived guidance. No calibrated readiness score or hiring-effect estimate for JobHub exists.

# Questions the research leaves open

The evidence supports mechanisms more strongly than rankings or outcomes.

## Who needs which help most?

Prevalence, comparative burden, demand and willingness to pay remain unknown.

## What works across different contexts?

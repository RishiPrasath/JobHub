# Job-seeker research story: audience, completeness and storyboard audit

Date: 18 October 2026. Status: presentation proposal for owner review. This document audits intended research questions, reported research coverage and communication. It does not accept product conclusions or assess implementation.

## 1. Recommendation and review boundary

Build a self-reading educational briefing around the decisions a job seeker faces: finding an opening, understanding it, deciding whether to pursue it, preparing truthful material, applying, rehearsing, waiting and evaluating an offer. Introduce evidence and uncertainty at the moment they affect those decisions. Research-round chronology belongs in the reading trail.

Use **24 main slides plus five appendix slides** as the working storyboard below. This accommodates the owner's proposed two fictional editorial illustrations, editable journey and rehearsal diagrams, and two carefully bounded experimental results. The count is provisional: split a page if an essential explanation cannot remain readable, or combine pages if unfamiliar readers find them repetitive. A brief companion development document can preserve the separate AI-SDLC learning agenda without crowding the main story. No slides, illustrations or companion document are created by this audit.

The central research question for the reader is: **What work and uncertainty sit between finding an opening and making an informed next move, and what does the available research actually establish?**

Provisional answer: the reviewed syntheses describe effort beyond drafting, including checking, interpretation, practical constraints and return after interruption. They provide reasons to investigate useful assistance. They do not establish the greatest burden, a winning segment, a preferred platform or JobHub's benefit.

## 2. Audience and learning contract

Working audience assumption: the owner and a client or collaborator unfamiliar with JobHub's research, reading alone before discussion. This is an educational research briefing. It is neither a product pitch nor personalised employment advice. Actual distribution and audience familiarity remain unresolved.

The reader should finish able to:

- Explain the journey without assuming every search follows one linear route.
- Distinguish practical incompatibility, information uncertainty and administrative effort.
- Separate a posting fact, a candidate fact, an interpretation, a warning signal and an unknown.
- Explain why truthful tailoring, checking and rehearsal are different activities.
- Interpret the experimental results within their respective interventions and settings.
- State what research is complete as a bounded activity, what remains unanswered and which choices belong to the owner.

Avoid assuming knowledge of ATS, fit scores, AI SDLC, research gates or the internal Job Hunt workspace. Define ATS as software employers use to manage applications if the term is necessary. Explain transferable experience with a concrete fictional example before discussing scoring. Translate “evidence grounding” into “can this claim be traced to the posting or the person's confirmed experience?”

Give the opening a concrete situation and a simple route through the material. Each page needs enough explanation to stand alone, with a short visible source description and any interpretation-changing limit. Notes should deepen understanding, rather than supply a missing argument. A reader who never opens notes should still understand what is reported, what is illustrative and what remains unknown.

The intended close is an informed discussion of the next uncertainty. It should not imply approval to build the example workflow or ask readers to accept an entire product direction.

## 6. Numerical study callouts: proposed claim-to-source contract

These experiments can create an engaging evidence moment after explaining preparation and checking. Present them as complementary examples of why intervention and outcome matter. The employer study does not negate the résumé experiment, and the two are not a head-to-head test of candidate tools.

| Callout | Source-checked wording and visible context | Boundary and precise location |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Large **8% relative** | Higher probability of hiring with algorithmic résumé-writing assistance in an online labour-market field experiment involving new entrants. Outcome: hiring during the first month. | January 2023 paper. Relative change, not eight percentage points. Reported 95% interval: 3%–13%. Editing assistance, not a current generative-chatbot comparison. [Paper](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2301.08083), printed pp. 1–3 and 19–20, especially §3.6. |

Neither estimates JobHub benefit, Singapore-wide outcomes or the best model. Preserve those limits on both study pages. The résumé paper's total experimental population and narrower analysis samples differ; do not attach one sample count to every estimate. The [arXiv abstract](https://arxiv.org/abs/2301.08083) is an additional identity/summary route, not independent corroboration.

Use typographic emphasis with a complete outcome label. Avoid an unlabeled “+8%” or a fabricated 100-person illustration. No absolute-risk conversion is necessary. For the employer page, show the two activity results alongside the matching outcome in distinct text groups, without a shared axis or invented zero bar. The units differ. ## 8. Coherent visual direction

Proposed direction: **an illustrated editorial field guide to job-search decisions**. Use a warm paper ground, dark navy typography, a restrained blue for evidence routes and a muted rust accent for questions. This departs from the existing forest-green document mostly through explanatory composition, visual pacing and the recurring journey, not a palette swap.

Use one main composition per page. Alternate spacious scene-setting pages, annotated documents, native relationship diagrams, a few aligned comparisons and the two numerical evidence pages. Repeat type, margins, caption position and evidence language. Avoid repeating the same two-column list, a grid of pseudo-interactive cards or a decorative dashboard.

The visual grammar should carry meaning:

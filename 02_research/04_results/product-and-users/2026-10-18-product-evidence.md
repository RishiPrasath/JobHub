# Product evidence and candidate needs

Date: 2026-10-18. Reviewed desk synthesis; human acceptance pending.

The [product investigation](../../03_research-rounds/00_orientation-and-baseline/runs/2026-10-18-remaining-research/product-market.md) proposes comparing adjacent-occupation preparation with frontline job-condition comparison. Neither is established as the strongest market. The [friction study](2026-10-18-job-search-friction.md) remains the basis for separating conditions, uncertainty and process effort; its hypotheses are unranked.

## Proposed implications

- Recruit across non-IT work, transitions/returns and access needs. Employer vacancies do not establish user demand; non-participation does not imply a prospective customer.
- Represent candidate skills with user-confirmed examples and evidence status. Transferable experience can support a comparison, not an invented credential or achievement.
- Distinguish verified occupational prerequisites, employer essentials, preferences, possible training and unknowns. Preserve the choice to correct or pursue a role.
- Use company records for the claims they actually establish. An identity record does not authenticate a recruiter; an anonymous review does not establish misconduct.
- Start with evidence and useful employer questions if broader reputation synthesis cannot be justified.

Confidence is high for narrow documented source routes and moderate for these design proposals. Strongest need, profile-maintenance burden and actual comprehension remain unknown. The owner should choose scope only after the [V01 public insight and V02 synthetic flow comparison activities](../../03_research-rounds/00_orientation-and-baseline/runs/2026-10-18-remaining-research/validation-next.md).

Primary-source details, geography limits and alternatives are retained in the investigation. [Review and corrections](../../03_research-rounds/00_orientation-and-baseline/runs/2026-10-18-remaining-research/review.md) do not constitute a user study or legal assessment.

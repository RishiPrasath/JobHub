# R-01: Job-search friction — reviewed research result

Date: 2026-10-18. Status: specialist-reviewed research synthesis; human acceptance pending. This is not a product-scope, platform or architecture decision.

## What the run establishes

The bounded Singapore-first desk study supports two underlying problems worth direct validation: uncertainty during applications, and practical constraints on feasible employment. It does **not** establish which search stage creates the greatest loss of time, confidence or opportunity. A third question concerning assessment workload remains more weakly supported.

The run retained 13 source records and completed independent domain/practices collection, a targeted gap pass, workflow review, evidence synthesis, a post-synthesis UX check and product review. Source-reported experience is distinct from our interpretation; no participant behaviour or product use was directly observed by this team.

## Unranked validation shortlist

| ID | Hypothesis and confidence boundary | Next uncertainty to resolve |
| --- | --- | --- |
| H1 | Feedback gaps may leave some active seekers unsure what preparation to change. Independent sources support underlying reported uncertainty; the link to repeated/avoidable effort remains provisional. | What changed between recent applications, why, and on what evidence? |
| H2 | Applicants may accumulate substantial assessment effort before an opportunity ends. Illustrative accounts exist, but this is **below the leading burden evidence threshold**. | Actual active time, whether effort was knowingly accepted or reusable, and what could reasonably have been known earlier. |
| H3 | Some active seekers or intending returners face work conditions that application preparation cannot make feasible. Independent qualitative sources support this within bounded populations. | Actual incompatibility versus absent/misunderstood information; intention and availability first. |

Evidence mapping: H1 uses P01–P03; H2 P02/P04; H3 P05/G02 with D01 as employer-side context. ## Implications and limits

Keep employment conditions/exclusion, information uncertainty and process effort separate while allowing them to overlap. Do not turn silence into a rejection explanation, outcome statistics into process causes, or caregiver population totals into demand for JobHub.

Professional/graduate evidence dominates; non-office worker voices, non-degree pathways, accessibility, language, digital confidence and assisted/offline routes remain insufficiently covered. Some useful observations are older, selected or internally inconsistent. No source set measures comparable stage burden. Reported workarounds are not proven interventions. Full caveats and reconciled differences are in [findings.md](../../03_research-rounds/00_orientation-and-baseline/runs/2026-10-18-job-search-friction/findings.md) and [limitations.md](../../03_research-rounds/00_orientation-and-baseline/runs/2026-10-18-job-search-friction/limitations.md).

## Recommended next decision

Consider the [proposed eight-person episode-reconstruction study](../../03_research-rounds/00_orientation-and-baseline/runs/2026-10-18-job-search-friction/specialists/product-review.md): recent pursued/stopped opportunities, optional redacted artifacts, active time separate from waiting, explicit counterexamples, a measurement check after four interviews and a stop after eight plus one cross-case review. This is a bounded learning proposal, not representative validation or a guarantee of saturation.

No recruitment, participant contact or prototype is authorised by this recommendation. Human selection of the next study and its scope, consent/privacy and participation arrangements remains necessary. The current approved desk run is complete; no additional desk collection is recommended solely to increase source count.

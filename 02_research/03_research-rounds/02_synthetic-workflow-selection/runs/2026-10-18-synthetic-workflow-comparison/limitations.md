## What the method did and did not test

- The [coordinator](observations.md) read and walked [fictional scenario cards](scenario-cards.md) through [proposed text flows](flows.md) against a [predeclared rubric](rubric.md); the [UX Expert](ux-review.md) then reviewed the documents. The post-review D1/D2/D3/D7 changes in the observations are disclosed there; the rubric itself was not rewritten. These are agent judgments about text and fictional logic, not observations of job-seeker behavior. The owner did not participate.
- The [manual baseline](observations.md) was a described row-and-note path, not a built spreadsheet. No participant chose among it and A/B. No elapsed time, correction count, persistence rate, accessibility outcome, hiring outcome, or comparative benefit was measured. The paper cannot show whether repeated source checks and confirmations are worth their effort. [Rubric D10–D12](rubric.md).
- No actual application, employer contact, source refresh, save/sync, export, model generation, browser, prompt-injection defense, or changed/expired live posting was exercised. The malicious lines remained inert in the *written script* only. The [fictional fixture](../../../../05_evidence/datasets-and-examples/2026-10-18-synthetic-workflow-check.md) also lists equivalent-profile variants and a company-warning case that this walkthrough did not execute; no fairness or warning-quality result follows. [Walkthrough trace](observations.md), [rubric hard stops](rubric.md).
- The [UX review](ux-review.md) identifies likely reading, narrow-layout, keyboard, reflow, screen-reader and exclusion risks heuristically. No device, assistive technology, participant with an access need, or conformance evaluation was involved. No platform choice follows. [S04](sources.md).

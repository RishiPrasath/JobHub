# Predeclared qualitative rubric — Round 02 synthetic workflow comparison

Status: **predeclared on 2026-10-18, before walkthrough observations**. This is the common review rule for candidate A (adjacent-occupation application preparation) and candidate B (frontline job-condition comparison). The coordinator should use it unchanged for both paper flows, record any later amendment with its reason and time, and keep earlier observations under the original rule. No walkthrough result, score, user behavior, product capability, or workflow priority is asserted here. Round 01 is unexecuted; both candidates remain provisional and unranked.

## Unit, method, and evidence boundary

The unit is one fictional scenario trace through **capture → assess → prepare or compare → interrupt → return → choose a next action**. Use the same dimensions below for A, B, and a simple manual notes/spreadsheet baseline. A may produce a truthful preparation artifact; B may produce a conditions comparison. Do not demand an identical artifact from distinct tasks. For each check, inspect the proposed words, source pointers, state labels, and recovery steps visible in the paper flow. Distinguish the fictional fixture's given facts from newly invented scenario facts, flow-design assumptions, and the walker's observations. A designed screen or successful paper step is not evidence that software works or that a person would understand or use it.

The baseline uses the same fictional inputs and interruption/change events: a person copies posting details and source/version into notes or a spreadsheet, marks unknowns, writes a next question/action, then returns to the record. Compare whether the proposed flow makes the decision clearer or the work easier to check, correct, and resume. Record any added capture, verification, or review work. Do not infer measured speed, preference, or superiority from this paper comparison.

## Categories and stopping rules

Apply **Pass / Concern / Failure / Not assessable** to each dimension for each candidate and the manual baseline. Quote or link the trace step that supports the category. Do not average categories or issue a single winner score.

- **Pass:** The paper trace visibly meets the dimension's checks on the supplied case, with a usable correction or next step where needed. This is a local design judgment only.
- **Concern:** The intended behavior is present but ambiguous, laborious, dependent on an unstated assumption, or not fully observable on paper. Record the missing proof or design change.
- **Failure:** The trace contradicts a stated check, loses necessary evidence/state, blocks correction without a route, or makes a materially misleading claim. Record the exact triggering step and consequence.
- **Not assessable:** The relevant state or artifact was not included in the scenario/flow. Record why and what fixture or prototype would be needed; never convert absence into a pass.

Two **hard stops** override any favorable dimension judgment. If a flow invents a qualification, experience, performance metric, salary, hours, contract term, submission, or other consequential candidate/posting fact, mark the affected dimension **Failure**, flag `fabricated-claim`, and stop treating the resulting artifact as usable until corrected. If posting text or the flow directs an external action (application, message, disclosure, upload, purchase, or tool action) without explicit task authority and user confirmation, flag `unauthorized-external-action` and stop that path. A malicious instruction inside a posting remains data. Record both the attempted action and whether it was actually followed; paper observation cannot prove an implementation guardrail. These stops are failure conditions, not evidence that either candidate is generally unsafe.

## Common task dimensions and observable checks

| ID | Dimension | Observable checks in both flows |
| --- | --- | --- |
| D1 | Capture and provenance | A saved opportunity retains source identifier, posting text or permissible excerpt, capture date/version, and a link from each consequential claim to its source or to confirmed fictional candidate input. Capture failure is labelled as a failure, not as employer nondisclosure. |
| D2 | Truthful candidate and posting claims | Explicit requirements, preferences, transferable experience, and material gaps remain distinct. A's resume/interview wording uses only supplied experience; B's comparison does not assert unknown pay, hours, contract, or eligibility. Inference is labelled and can be challenged. Check the hard stop for any fabricated qualification or term. |
| D3 | Evidence visibility and uncertainty | The decision view exposes enough original wording/version to audit a claim; `known`, `unknown`, `not supplied`, and `inferred` are not collapsed. Uncorroborated employer warnings remain allegations, never established misconduct. Missing qualification evidence does not automatically mean ineligibility. |
| D4 | Missing information and next inquiry | Salary/hours and other absent conditions, or an unstated qualification, are displayed as open questions with a safe next action (for example, verify the posting or ask the employer later). The flow does not invent an answer or silently discard the role. |
| D5 | Correction and ownership | The fictional candidate/owner can correct an incorrect claim or requirement, see what depended on it, and revise the output without losing the source record. Record the number and kind of review decisions or edits required; do not estimate real elapsed time. |
| D6 | Changed or expired posting | When A's version 2 adds a fixed-term contract, or B's fictional posting changes/expires, the prior and current versions, changed field, and effect on the next action are visible. No old summary is silently overwritten; an expired opportunity is not presented as available. |
| D7 | Interruption, return, and recovery | After saving and leaving mid-task, the return view restores the correct opportunity, source/version, draft or comparison state, unresolved questions, and a concrete next action. A failed save/sync or missing source has an honest recovery path. |
| D8 | Saved, prepared, reviewed, applied | Labels and transitions distinguish capture/save, draft preparation, human review, and actual application/submission. An application is never inferred from saving, generating text, or viewing a posting. No external action occurs in this walkthrough. |
| D9 | Clarity and decision usefulness | A reader can tell what the opportunity requires, why the proposed preparation or comparison follows, which facts remain uncertain, and what to do next. A's material SQL gap is explicit; B's conditions are compared without a false fit verdict. Note confusing labels, dense views, or an opaque score. |
| D10 | Manual baseline and added work | On the same fictional case, record what notes/spreadsheet already accomplishes, what evidence it cannot easily preserve, and each extra capture, checking, correction, or ownership step introduced by the proposed flow. A feature that merely restates the notes with more review effort is a concern or failure for added value. |
| D11 | Accessibility heuristics and likely exclusion | Inspect whether the paper flow has plain labels, a logical reading/focus order, text alternatives for visual status, discoverable errors and recovery, and room for keyboard operation, text enlargement/reflow, and low digital confidence. Mark only design risks and proposed checks; a paper flow cannot prove keyboard behavior, screen-reader output, contrast, or WCAG conformance. |
| D12 | Owner review effort and control | List the claims, changes, warnings, and external-action boundaries a human owner would need to verify; identify where each is visible and what happens if the owner disagrees. Record qualitative burden (few/localized, repeated, or unclear/high) with the actual checks needed, not a guessed time or cost. |

For each dimension, **Pass** requires the listed checks to be visible in the trace; use **Concern** for an incomplete or ambiguous check and **Failure** for a contradicted check with material effect. If multiple checks differ, retain the specific check-level notes and assign the dimension the most serious observed category. Do not turn a heuristic accessibility concern into a conformance verdict or let a polished presentation erase a truthfulness failure.

## Required challenge cases and disconfirming observations

Run these as paper branches or mark them Not assessable with the missing input. Preserve a counterexample for each candidate.

| Case | What to inspect | Disconfirms a useful/safe flow if... |
| --- | --- | --- |
| A: missing qualification and material gap | Leave one qualification unstated; compare the fictional retail-to-operations history with Operations Analyst's required SQL/analytics experience. | It invents SQL, management, metrics, or a qualification; hides the gap; or treats silence as proof of ineligibility without a correction route. |
| A: preparation and version change | Tailor one resume bullet or interview answer for Operations Coordinator; then add the fixed-term term in version 2. | The artifact claims unsupported achievement, coaching invites a false claim, or the contract change is hidden from the decision. |
| B: uncertain conditions | Use two fictional frontline roles with an unknown salary, hours, or contract term; keep the worker's constraints fictional and explicit. | The comparison supplies missing terms, ranks a role as feasible from unknowns, or treats an incompatibility as fixable by better application wording. |
| B: correction and counterexample | Correct a condition or worker constraint after the first comparison. Include a case in which notes/spreadsheet or direct verification is sufficient. | The original conclusion persists without warning, the owner cannot repair it, or the proposed flow adds work without a clear decision benefit. |
| Shared: interruption and changed/expired source | Save mid-task, return later, then inspect a changed or expired posting. | The wrong opportunity or version is restored, the next action is lost, or the stale posting is treated as current. |
| Shared: misleading posting instruction | Add clearly fictional text that asks the assistant to ignore directions or disclose profile data. | The text is obeyed as an instruction or triggers an external action/disclosure. |
| Shared: state and manual alternative | Ask what was saved, prepared, reviewed, and actually applied; repeat the task with notes/spreadsheet. | A submission is inferred, or the proposed flow yields no checkable benefit over the simpler method while requiring material extra review. |

A concern or failure in any case should remain visible even if another branch looks strong. A clear manual-baseline counterexample, an excessive owner review burden, or a need for facts Round 01 has not supplied can reverse any provisional preference. The absence of such a counterexample in a fictional walkthrough does not confirm demand.

## Record contract for later observations

For each scenario and branch, the walker records: `candidate`, `fictional scenario/fixture ID`, `walker and role`, `walkthrough date`, `input/source and version`, `initial saved/prepared/reviewed/applied state`, `task and step`, `paper action`, `expected visible state`, `actual visible paper-flow state`, `source/evidence shown`, `unknown or inference label`, `ambiguity/error`, `correction or recovery path`, `next action`, `manual-baseline counterpart`, and `hard-stop flag if any`. Quote the exact proposed wording or link to the flow step where a judgment depends on it. Label each entry as **fictional input**, **design assumption**, or **agent observation**; do not label agent interpretation as observed job-seeker behavior.

For the comparison, record one row per `candidate × dimension` with `category`, `trace reference`, `check-level reason`, `owner review work`, `unresolved evidence`, and `possible disconfirming result`. Keep hard-stop events in a separate explicit list as well. Report strengths, failures, and uncertainty side by side, without numerical weights. A later prototype or consented user study may test comprehension, correction burden, accessibility, and actual task value; this rubric itself cannot establish them.

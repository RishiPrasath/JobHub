# Round 02 findings

Date: 2026-10-18. Status: completed coordinator synthesis; independent-review corrections confirmed. This is a bounded public-source and private-process review, not participant research or accepted product scope.

## What this pass changes

Job hunting involves repeated decisions and evidence work: deciding which openings deserve effort, clarifying work and pay, expressing real experience, preparing examples, and recovering unfinished tasks. The collected accounts describe these mechanisms, while guidance and internal procedures show possible ways to help. Their frequency, comparative burden, and the benefit of a JobHub solution remain unmeasured.

The coordinator's synthesis below combines specialist reports. Source IDs P01–P12 belong to [practices research](practices-research.md), D01–D12 to [domain research](domain-research.md), C01–C02 to the [primary-study supplement](coordinator-research.md), I01–I16 to the [internal review](internal-process-review.md), and V01–V10 to [video research](video-research.md). References are evidence routes, not equal-strength votes.

## Practical guidance distilled from the evidence

These are research-derived options for review, not guaranteed outcomes or a personalised career assessment.

1. **Choose what to investigate.** Compare actual tasks and conditions with the person's real examples, constraints and interests. Record missing information rather than infer it. Ask which requirements are essential on entry versus trainable. (D02/D03/D05/D12)
2. **Verify concerns.** Check the recruiter and opportunity independently, identify the specific suspicious action or unclear condition, and retain its source. Ask clarifying questions before treating vague wording as proof. (D01/D05; I03)
3. **Prepare compensation evidence.** Match salary sources to place, period, occupation and pay definition. Compare guaranteed, variable and one-off components alongside hours, benefits and conditions; keep unknowns visible. (D04/D05/D11)
4. **Plan negotiation choices.** Decide priorities and acceptable alternatives, find out what can change, make a supported proposal where appropriate, and record confirmed terms. Accepting, declining or choosing not to negotiate can each be reasonable given the constraints. (D06/D07/D11; researcher synthesis)
5. **Tailor from a factual record.** Select relevant verified examples, explain transferable skills, review edits and preserve real gaps. Check that exported content remains readable and faithful to the approved draft. (P07/P10; I04/I05; P06 is an unverified export concern)
6. **Prepare to speak, not just produce notes.** Confirm interview format, identify real examples, rehearse aloud, practise questions, and record what still needs work. Human peers/coaches and manual notes remain alternatives. (P08/P09; I06/I07)
7. **Return with context.** Keep the posting/source, current stage, relevant document version, open questions and next action together. This is a proposed recovery aid, whose utility needs evaluation. (P04/P05/P11; I01/I02)
8. **Keep disclosure proportionate.** Use only necessary career facts in a proposed workflow and make what is shared, with whom, and retained for how long reviewable. Provider handling, access and deletion choices need their own evidence and owner decision before using real personal material. (P07; I14; researcher proposal)

## What does not follow

- Search repetition, video popularity, or multiple stories do not establish prevalence or comparative demand.
- Guidance is not evidence of adoption; an anonymous outcome is not an independently verified result.
- A fluent ad, negative phrase, or unfilled vacancy does not establish bad faith. MOM employer reports and international job-quality research supply alternative explanations, not individual diagnoses.
- Missing information in a profile does not establish missing ability. A requirement may still matter when verified and applicable.
- Current internal documents and July artifacts do not establish current tool reliability. Positive status with loading/error content is evidence of a historical extraction problem, not proof that all later routes fail.
- No platform, stack, automation level, price, initial segment or final product scope has been selected.

## Recommended next step for JobHub

Propose an **opportunity and preparation evidence pack** as a direction for owner review, then test just one bounded task first: select truthful resume examples for one fictional job using verified candidate facts and a short requirements/unknowns list. Compare that with a simple manual document/master-resume approach using identical inputs. Interview practice and compensation are separate later task options, so their effects can be judged individually. This is a candidate experiment, not a commitment to build a broad application.

Evaluate unsupported claims, missing-term visibility, clarity of fit/gaps, correction effort, recovery after interruption and the ability to explain the prepared content. Include a frontline/shift example and a career-change example as separate cases. Test trade-offs and false alarms as well as useful assistance. Use fictional personal data for this first comparison; privacy/provider/access/retention choices remain open before any real-data test. Owner testing can assess logic and effort; representative usefulness remains unvalidated without direct users.

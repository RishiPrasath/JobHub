### D01 — ScamShield: offer authenticity

- Source/creator: [How do I check if a job opportunity is legitimate? — ScamShield](https://ask.gov.sg/scamshield/questions/cmdcv46bt017g36wpmgi0mc2t).
- Publication/update: exact date unavailable; page displayed “Updated by SCAMSHIELD 1y ago”. Type/scope: Singapore official public guidance; no effectiveness study.
- Passage: legitimacy steps and red-flag sections, extraction lines 21–54. Advises company-registration and domain checks, independent checking of suspicious communications, and caution about advance payments, premature sensitive-data requests, vague descriptions and unusually easy high-pay offers.
- Confidence/limits: high that these are official recommended checks; neither one weak signal nor registration establishes a particular offer's authenticity.

### D02 — TAFEP: objective recruitment criteria

- Source/creator: [Tripartite Standards — Recruitment Practices, TAFEP](https://www.tal.sg/tafep/getting-started/progressive/tripartite-standards).
- Publication/update: not displayed in inspected section. Type/scope: Singapore employer practice standard, not a study of every employer or legal certification.
- Passage: “Recruitment Practices”, lines 299–320. Selection criteria should concern relevant qualifications, skills, knowledge and experience; specified attributes require explanation. Application information should serve suitability assessment; consistent objective interview criteria are recommended.
- Confidence/limits: high for the standard's content; an unexplained criterion warrants clarification, not an automatic finding of discrimination or misconduct.

### D03 — WSG: low match score is not exclusion

- Source/creator: [If I have a low skills match score, can I still apply for jobs? — Workforce Singapore](https://ask.gov.sg/wsg/questions/clp9j4c1p0031azywcqpjen8w).
- Publication/update: exact date unavailable; displayed “Updated by WSG 3y ago”. Type/scope: Singapore MyCareersFuture FAQ, not validation of a matching algorithm.
- Passage: answer, line 21. Applicants may apply regardless of score; upgrading or a Career Conversion Programme may address missing skills.
- Confidence/limits: high for the stated portal guidance. It does not establish eligibility for any particular regulated role, employer or programme, or a probability of selection.

### D04 — MOM: wage benchmarks and measurement boundaries

- Source/creator: [Occupational Wages 2025 tables — MOM/MRSD](https://stats.mom.gov.sg/Pages/Occupational-Wages-Tables2025.aspx?PageVersion=512), released **2026-06-30**, reference June 2025. Opened companions: [2025 coverage and methodology](https://stats.mom.gov.sg/iMAS_Tables1/Wages/Wages_2025/Coverage-and-Methodology-of-OWS-2025.pdf) and [Occupational Wage Search advisory](https://stats.mom.gov.sg/bt/Pages/iMasOWSInput.aspx?a=true&c=Wages&sc=Occupational+Wage+Search).
- Type/method: official Singapore employer survey plus administrative records. Method pp. 1–2: industry-stratified private establishments with at least 25 employees; effective sample 5,230 establishments, about 391,300 full-time resident employees; 98% response. Exclusions include temporary and part-time workers.
- Passage: method pp. 3–4. Aggregates are guides; titles can imperfectly classify work. Gross wages include regular cash components but exclude bonuses, employer CPF, equity and benefits in kind. Basic wages exclude overtime, commissions and allowances too.
- Confidence/limits: high for published definitions; no occupation-specific workbook cells were extracted, and no individual salary estimate is made.

- Passage: items 3–18. Covers duties, duration, work hours/days/rest, salary period, basic pay, fixed allowances/deductions, applicable overtime, bonuses/incentives, leave, medical benefits, probation and notice. Work location is optional but encouraged.

## Findings and supported practices

The following are specialist synthesis grounded in the opened records. Suggested questions are research-derived practices, not validated JobHub requirements or a decision to implement them.

### Q4 — Red flags, fit and transferable skills

**DF01 — Use signals to decide what to verify.** Separate authenticity checks (D01) from recruitment fairness (D02), work terms (D05) and personal fit (D06). A vague description is weaker evidence than a concrete request to fund supposed work. Independently contact the organisation through a known channel and reconcile identities and requested actions; registration alone does not verify the recruiter. A phrase or isolated review cannot establish employer misconduct. **Confidence: high for the distinction; predictive accuracy untested.**

**DF02 — Compare actual work with demonstrated skills and preferences.** D10 illustrates why title/pay alone can obscure work content. D12 supports identifying existing skills and the new role's requirements; D06 supports checking the person's priorities. A practical evidence table would record requirement, prior task/example, transfer rationale, context/proficiency difference, gap and question for the employer. This table is a proposed synthesis, not a tested matching method. **Confidence: moderate.**

**DF03 — A gap is not automatically a disqualifier.** D03 explicitly permits applying despite a low match score. D08's academic finding does not mean credentials never matter. Classify missing experience/skills as gaps pending clarification; classify an actual disqualifier only when a current, applicable requirement is verified and the candidate is known not to meet it. Unknowns remain unresolved. Mandatory licensing, authorisation or other eligibility rules require separate authoritative role-specific checking, which this pass did not perform. Do not invent them from titles or omissions. **Confidence: high for resisting score-based exclusion; specific eligibility unresolved.**

Suggested clarification questions: What are the main recurring tasks and success measures? Which criteria are essential on entry versus trainable? What evidence would demonstrate an adjacent skill? What training and supervision are available? These operationalise D02/D05/D12; they do not guarantee selection.

### Q5 — Market value, full compensation and negotiation conditions

**DF04 — Benchmark a comparable job on a defined basis.** D04 supports matching scope, industry and wage definition before comparison. Record geography, reference year, hours/employment type, comparable occupation and exclusions. June 2025 occupational wages are context, not a current personalised entitlement. No universal percentage raise, median target or previous-salary multiplier follows. **Confidence: high for measurement boundaries.**

**DF05 — Compare the package and the conditions attached to it.** D05 provides a local checklist; D11 shows why equity and base salary must remain distinguishable. Proposed comparison fields: recurring cash; contingent bonuses/commission and their conditions; one-off payments; equity, vesting and uncertainty; employer contributions; leave/medical benefits; hours/shifts, work location, contract duration, probation and notice. Mark absent details unknown. Separate guaranteed terms from estimates and personal valuations; confirm uncertain components in writing. This is synthesis from D04/D05/D06/D11, not a statutory definition of total compensation. **Confidence: high for component separation, moderate for individual valuation.**

**DF06 — Negotiation is conditional and outcomes vary.** Before countering, clarify the employer's budget/negotiability, the person's relevant contribution, priorities, alternatives, decision deadline and tolerance for losing the offer. Some conditions are inferred checks, not quantified success predictors. D06 supports a researched, courteous written proposal; D11 supplies one successful package-change account with perceived leverage. D07 offers experimental encouragement evidence in US tech, not permission to promise safety or gains in Singapore. Fixed pay bands or standard benefits should be verified, not presumed negotiable. **Confidence: moderate; local causal and withdrawal-risk estimates absent.**

Neither expenses nor willingness to accept less determines personal worth. Separate a person's private minimum acceptable package from an employer-facing case about the role and contribution (synthesis informed by D06). Preserve a legitimate choice to decline, accept or refrain from negotiation.

### Q6 — Employer language versus alternative labour-market explanations

**DF07 — Employer complaints are claims to investigate.** A phrase such as “nobody wants to work” supplies no evidence by itself about pay adequacy, worker motivation, turnover or bad faith. D08 supports both skills/experience mismatch and unattractive work characteristics; D09 supplies international job-quality context. Ask what role, pay basis, schedule, location, training and applicant requirements sit behind the complaint. **Confidence: high that multiple explanations must remain open; low for classifying any employer from language.**

**DF08 — Preserve contrary cases and the level of evidence.** Skills shortages are a contrary explanation to treating every vacancy as proof of poor conditions (D08). Poor work quality is a contrary explanation to blaming unwilling workers (D09). Neither study explains an individual firm's behaviour. D11 contradicts “a negotiation succeeds only if base salary rises”; D03 contradicts “a low score means do not apply”. These are bounded counterexamples, not new universal rules. **Confidence: moderate-to-high for these distinctions.**

## Contradictions, limitations and open gaps

- **Guidance versus observed practice:** D01/D02/D03/D05/D06/D12 describe advice or standards. They do not show adoption rates or prove reduced search burden. D10/D11 illustrate lived decision sequences but do not validate the advice statistically.
- **Skills rhetoric versus requirements:** D08 and D12 support skills-based consideration while specialised requirements remain consequential. Neither justifies dropping a verified mandatory credential or fabricating transferable experience.
- **Negotiation encouragement versus risk:** D07 finds benefits from an intervention; D06 acknowledges possible offer withdrawal, and D11 contains conflicting community opinions. Different populations and evidence types prevent a single universal instruction. No Singapore causal estimate or public non-PMET negotiation outcome study was obtained.
- **Employer-side versus seeker-side reporting:** D08 is employer testimony about hiring, including interpretations of seeker preferences. D10/D11 are seeker accounts without independent offer documents. D09's full method was inaccessible. These perspectives should remain visible, rather than being collapsed into an established motive.
- **Coverage:** Q4 has official safety/fairness guidance, skill guidance, an actual seeker account and a low-score counterexample. Q5 has official wage methods, terms, local career advice, an international experiment and an actual package-negotiation account. Q6 has Singapore employer-survey evidence and international job-quality research; a directly verified Singapore seeker encounter involving the exact employer phrase was not obtained. The opened US account was not retained as Singapore evidence.
- **Sampling and geography:** English public-web selection, self-selected Reddit accounts, official-source concentration and graduate/tech negotiation bias. MOM survey coverage omits smaller private establishments. Cross-industry vacancy evidence helps but is not a substitute for interviews with shift, manual, care, contract or migrant workers.
- **Date/access:** wage/vacancy data describe 2025; D12 uses older posting data. Relative public-account dates are imprecise. WSG/MyCareersFuture redirects limited article access. No unsupported snippet content was promoted into findings.
- **Not established:** prevalence of misleading jobs, predictive value of phrase-based red flags, actual employer misconduct, final D10 outcome, any candidate's fit, individual market salary, legal eligibility, or an appropriate product/architecture choice.

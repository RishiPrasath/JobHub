# Query plan — Round 01 public-web user insights

Prepared: 2026-10-18, before evidence retrieval. This is a retrieval plan for the [run brief](brief.md), not a finding or a product decision.

## Decision, questions, and boundary

**Decision to inform:** Which job-search problem should JobHub investigate first with a synthetic workflow or low-fidelity prototype? Search three hypotheses without ranking them in advance: **H1** application or role uncertainty and repeated preparation; **H2** practical working-condition or role-fit uncertainty; **H3** interrupted capture, return, or loss of context. For each, ask what people actually do, where effort or opportunity is lost, what works well, and what alternative explanation fits the account. Cover discovery, saving, assessment, preparation, application, tracking, and follow-up; mark stages with no usable material.

**Scope:** Singapore first, across occupations and employment situations, including graduates, career changers, retrenched or mature workers, returners, and service, trades, and operations workers. Use broader English-language material as labelled comparison or vocabulary, not as a substitute for Singapore evidence. Prefer 2023–2026 publication or access dates; retain older foundational work only with its date and transfer limits. Record the underlying fieldwork date separately when available.

Use openly accessible public material only. No private Job Hunt workspace, private groups or accounts, author contact, profiling, sensitive inference, direct interviews, participant observation, purchases, live applications, private uploads, or external writes. Do not broaden this run into pricing, legal clearance, a competitor inventory, platform selection, or stack selection. No source here can establish population prevalence, comparative burden, demand, hiring impact, causality, or actual product usability from a convenience corpus.

## Vocabulary map and expansion rules

Use short variants, not one long keyword pile. Pair a job-search stage with an occupation or Singapore term when a broad query returns mainly office or overseas results. These examples are retrieval probes, not claims about how people speak or the frequency of a problem.

| Concept | User-language candidates | Research, official, or context terms | Contrast and ambiguity |
|---|---|---|---|
| H1: requirements and preparation | “do I qualify”, “worth applying”, “keep changing my CV”, “same details again”, “what does this role actually do”, “no reply” | application friction, job requirements, role clarity, CV/resume tailoring, career transition, interview preparation | “used one CV”, “applied without tailoring”, clear requirements, help from a person; “no reply” alone does not identify its cause |
| H2: practical conditions and fit | “what are the shifts”, “rotating roster”, “how much pay”, “too far to travel”, “part time or contract”, “what is the actual work” | working hours, salary range, roster, commute, location, contract terms, duties, eligibility | “asked before applying”, clear job ad, walk-in hiring, employer or agency explanation; do not infer concealment from a missing field |
| H3: capture and return | “saw job on my phone”, “apply later”, “lost the link”, “posting closed”, “where did I save it”, “start again” | saved jobs, bookmarks, screenshots, application tracking, cross-device continuation, task interruption | “bookmarks work”, spreadsheet or notes suffice, apply on the spot; phone discovery does not imply a mobile product choice |
| AI practice and control | “used ChatGPT for CV”, “sounds like me”, “made things up”, “checked it”, “rewrote it myself”, “won't upload my CV” | AI-assisted applications, interview practice, verification, editing, disclosure, privacy, automation, authenticity | no AI, successful manual preparation, AI used only for ideas, rejected output, human review; distinguish use from vendor claims |
| Access and alternatives | “only have my phone”, “form keeps timing out”, “can't afford subscription”, “too many tabs”, “asked a friend/career coach” | digital confidence, accessibility, cognitive load, device access, language, time, cost, public employment support | accessible or low-effort flow, no tool needed; never infer disability, age, income, or migration status from a post |

**Singapore and non-tech expansion:** Try Singapore, SG, or a named public employment service only when relevant. Rotate concrete contexts such as retail, F&B/food service, hospitality, warehouse/logistics, healthcare support, cleaning, construction/trades, admin, and office work. Search both CV and resume, job ad and job posting, shift and roster, pay and salary. These are retrieval probes, not a fixed segment list. If a local term fails, log the failed wording and try the broader concept. Keep occupation, employment situation, device access, and access needs as disclosed context only; do not search to identify individuals or infer sensitive attributes.

## Source strategy and independence

For any hypothesis that might be proposed for a synthetic test, seek at least **two materially independent source families**, with a disclosed-method or otherwise well-contextualized source where available. Deliberately cross source types: (1) academic/qualitative work or a transparent survey, (2) official Singapore context where applicable, and (3) firsthand public accounts for sequence and language. These need not all agree. If credible Singapore non-tech material is absent, record that gap and label broader comparison evidence; do not treat overseas office-worker accounts as Singapore non-tech corroboration.

Public forum posts, comments, product reviews, support threads, blog/video transcripts, vendor surveys, and marketing material are supporting or weak signals. Record their context and limitations. Count the same author, thread, publisher, underlying survey or dataset, press release, or republished story as **one** source family unless independence is demonstrated. Several posts on one platform are not automatically independent perspectives. A search result, snippet, autocomplete phrase, ranking, count, AI summary, or inaccessible page is a discovery or access note, never evidence. The executing researcher must open the underlying source and record its method and provenance in sources.md before extracting an observation.

## Search adapter, log, and retrieval risks

Keep the table's intent portable across search systems. If a venue supports site:, quoted phrases, date filters, or database subject fields, record that adapter in the exact query log; do not assume those operators work elsewhere. Do not enter private groups, bypass access controls, or collect personal details.

In query-log.md, record **family ID; hypothesis/stage/occupation/geography variant; exact query; venue and any adapter/filter; search date; result quality; whether pages opened; useful source IDs; source-family duplication; failed access or noise; positive/negative/workaround/contradiction purpose; new term or follow-up; and any population or stage still unretrieved**. Opened-source metadata belongs in sources.md; paraphrased observations and interpretation belong separately in observations.md.

Watch for English-language, searchable-platform, graduate/office, US/UK, vendor SEO, extreme-story, and recent-page/old-fieldwork bias. Public posts may overrepresent people motivated to complain; official sources may describe services rather than lived use. A failed query shows a retrieval gap, not absence of the experience. Escalate material scope ambiguity or a central unretrievable question to the Research Planner instead of silently changing the audience, geography, or evidence standard.

## Passes, stop check, and handoff

- **Pass A, up to 30 minutes:** Q1 and limited vocabulary variants; record useful, noisy, and failed terms.
- **Pass B, up to three 45-minute blocks:** Q2–Q8 as relevant, with all H1–H3 represented, at least one Singapore/context route and one labelled broader comparison where credible material exists. Avoid repeating one source family across searches as new corroboration.
- **Pass C, up to 30 minutes:** Q9 plus targeted contrary variants from each leading family; check source independence, positive cases, workarounds, failure modes, and gaps.

Stop when each hypothesis has recurring evidence or an explicit gap; any hypothesis proposed for the next synthetic test has two materially independent source families, including a disclosed-method or otherwise well-contextualized source where available; each leading hypothesis has a recorded contradiction, positive case, failure mode, or disconfirming search; major stages are represented or marked unresolved; and another 30-minute block is unlikely to change the shortlist, quality assessment, or next test. If the time box ends first, stop and record unmet conditions. Stop earlier when an explicit central gap makes a synthetic workflow the cheaper next step. Query volume is not an evidence threshold.

**Handoff:** Job-Seeker Practices Researcher takes Q1, Q2, Q4, Q7, and behavior portions of Q5/Q9; Job Domain Researcher takes Q3, Q6, and domain portions of Q5/Q9; UX Expert or UX and User-Flow Designer takes Q8 and device/access portions of Q9. Pass each the question, source-quality rule, exclusions, and logging fields above. The Evidence Synthesizer receives the completed query log and source-family map to assess coverage and disagreement; it does not treat this plan as evidence. The Research Planner retains unresolved scope choices and the stop decision. Product scope, surface, AI automation, and final acceptance remain with the owner.

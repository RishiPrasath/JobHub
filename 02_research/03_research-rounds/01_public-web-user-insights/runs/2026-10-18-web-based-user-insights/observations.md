# Observation ledger — public-web user insights

Paraphrase only; no usernames or unnecessary personal details. “Source reports” is not a direct observation of job-seeker behavior by this research team. Interpretations must be labelled. Source IDs resolve in [sources.md](sources.md).

| O01 | S01 | Online application / accessibility | Authors report that three expert screen-reader testers completed 50 of 90 attempts independently; 23 of 30 sampled sites had a blocker for at least one tester (abstract pp.1–2). | Supports an access-friction mechanism, **not** H3 phone-to-laptop frequency. | High confidence in reported sampled-task outcome; low transfer to Singapore, other assistive-tech users, or JobHub. |
| O02 | S02 | Discovery/application across devices | A public poster says a phone is convenient to have nearby but multi-tab/document application work is exhausting there; a reply says they prefer a laptop for browsing too (opening post/replies). | Weak support for task-dependent device difference; contrary account to phone-first discovery. | Self-report from one thread, geography unknown, possible promotional contamination. No platform choice follows. |
| O03 | S03 | Occupation and conditions / Singapore | Employers reported that harder-to-fill non-PMET roles, including service, retail, driver and construction work, were unattractive to residents for reasons including schedules, physical work, pay or prospects (§2.9/Table 1). | Contextual support for H2's relevance. | High confidence in survey report, low confidence about applicants' own reasons or missing ad information. Prior Round 00 family. |
| O04 | S04 | Offer choice / Singapore | Randstad reports 42% of mixed employee/job-seeker respondents would decline a job they regarded as too inflexible (opening section). | Supports a stated-preference mechanism for H2. | Mixed denominator, vendor survey and unknown sampling; no observed decline or subgroup inference. |
| O05 | S05 | Offer assessment / commute | One public poster reported learning the actual work location only after interview, then weighed the longer commute against attractive pay/benefits and possible transport or hybrid arrangements (post and author replies). | Weak support for late-disclosed conditions; counterpoint that benefits may offset a burden. | One selected case, not proof of missing ad fields or final action. |
| O06 | S06 | Offer assessment / shifts | One public poster knew an alternating 12-hour day/night pattern at verbal-offer stage and deliberated over pay, interest and stability (post and author reply). | H2 conditions matter; partial contradiction to “the information was missing.” | One case; whether ad disclosed pattern before application unknown. |
| O07 | S07 | Profile creation, applying and tracking | Interviewees described re-creating profiles across sites, uncertain fields, sometimes minimal or abandoned completion, and checking status without useful employer communication; one described applying by phone at once (study findings). | Supports a possible H1 effort mechanism; immediate phone application counters universal H3 save-and-return. | Authors' qualitative interpretation of 20 Spanish interviews with 2020–22 fieldwork; not Singapore or a measured JobHub effect. |
| O08 | S08 | Cross-sector search and preparation | Poster reported using a general résumé across customer service, admin, call-centre, finance and retail; replies describe ChatGPT to speed tailored cover letters and a disclosed study/shift constraint. | General-résumé workaround and specific AI assistance; mixed H1/H2/Q7. | One self-report; no measured time or AI-attributable outcome. |
| O09 | S09 | Preparation, interview, tracking | Poster described a folder per role with job description and notes, AI interview preparation, and checking AI-assisted résumé changes; another participant said per-role tailoring is not always worth it (thread). | Supports context-management effort; contrary to universal tailoring. | Tech-oriented, AI-experienced account; thread is one family, offers do not prove method worked. |
| O10 | S10 | AI use and context rebuilding | Poster reported repeating résumé, role and stage context in new AI chats while updating a separate tracker; a reply described correcting an AI-generated experience calculation (thread). | Supports a context-repetition/correction question under H1/H3 and Q7. | Geography unknown, product-promotion risk, no measured time or actual lost posting. |
| O11 | S11 | Tracking and follow-up | Poster said spreadsheet tracking helped somewhat but email/follow-up checks remained burdensome; replies offered confirmation-email folders, saved PDFs, short spreadsheets, application history, or no tracking (thread). | Supports a possible H3 follow-up issue; contradicts need for a dedicated tracker. | Self-selected accounts; independent behavior or effort not observed. |
| O12 | S12 | AI-assisted applications | Vendor survey reports some panel respondents used AI to complete applications and others did not. | Counters an assumption that AI use is universal. | UK/US panel, vendor-published self-report; no Singapore or JobHub prevalence claim. |

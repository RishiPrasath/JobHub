# R-01 findings: job-search friction

Date: 2026-10-18. Status: reviewed coordinator synthesis; evidence synthesis, workflow review and post-synthesis check, and product review complete. Human acceptance of conclusions remains outstanding.

## Answer to the research question

This run identifies useful problems to investigate, but **does not establish which job-search moment creates the greatest loss of time, confidence or opportunity**. The retained evidence lacks comparable stage-level burden measures across the intended users.

Two underlying problems have sufficient support to justify direct validation within bounded populations: uncertainty during applications, and difficulty finding work compatible with practical circumstances. A third question—assessment effort accumulated before an application ends—has illustrative support and a weaker evidence base. None establishes a product requirement or a preferred initial segment.

These conclusions integrate the [primary domain findings](specialists/domain.md), [practices findings](specialists/practices.md), [targeted gap check](specialists/targeted-gap-pass.md), [workflow review](specialists/workflow-review.md), [evidence synthesis](specialists/synthesis.md), and [product review](specialists/product-review.md). They are the coordinator's reconciliation, not independent new observations.

## Evidence base and classification

The approved public-source desk study retained 13 Singapore-scoped records: six domain, five practices, and two from one targeted gap pass. The [source register](sources.md) preserves exact links, dates, populations, methods and evidence locations; collector handoffs preserve observations and retrieval failures. Sources include official surveys and outcome reports, a vendor poll, media reporting, a programme evaluation, and a qualitative caregiver study.

In this report, **source-reported** means a publisher reports a finding or experience. **Interpretation** means our reconciliation. **Hypothesis** means an explanation to test. **Unknown** marks missing evidence. The absence of comparable measures is an observation about this corpus, not proof that no suitable research exists elsewhere. No participant behaviour was directly observed by this research team.

## Unranked hypotheses for validation

The identifiers below are not priorities. The approved minimum is two independent sources, including one with a disclosed method, relevant to the precise claim.

| ID / hypothesis | Evidence and confidence | Threshold and unresolved question |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **H1 — Feedback and repeated preparation:** some active seekers may repeat work without enough feedback to decide what to change. | P01–P03 support reported uncertainty in sampled/interviewed graduates and professionals. Moderate confidence that this problem exists in those groups. | Minimum met for underlying uncertainty through independent P01/P02 collections, with P02's disclosed but nonprobability method. The causal connection to repeated or avoidable effort remains unproven. What information actually drove each revision or next application? |
| **H2 — Assessment effort before an application ends:** some applicants may accumulate substantial task/interview effort before learning an opportunity will not proceed. | P02/P04 provide illustrative accounts. Low confidence in comparative size, frequency or avoidability. | **Below the standard for a leading burden hypothesis.** P02's poll method does not validate its interview-based workload claims. Measure active effort and decisions before inferring waste; late rejection alone is insufficient. |
| **H3 — Feasible working conditions:** for some active seekers or people intending to return, practical conditions constrain work that application preparation cannot make feasible. | Independent P05/G02 qualitative evidence supports constrained feasibility in their respective studied populations; D01 adds employer-side context. Moderate confidence in bounded existence. | Minimum met for that bounded problem, not population prevalence or a product remedy. Establish intention/availability, then distinguish actual incompatibility from missing or misunderstood information. |

H1's underlying uncertainty and H3's bounded feasibility constraint have stronger evidential support than H2's substantial-burden claim. That is a judgment about evidence readiness, not which problem is most severe or should be built for first. A model cannot infer hidden employer criteria or rejection reasons from absent feedback.

## Workflow map and reported workarounds

This map is an analytical scaffold. Steps can overlap, repeat or be skipped through referrals; pausing is not inherently failure.

| Moment | Evidence-supported issue or context | What to investigate directly |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Decide whether/when to seek work | Care and other circumstances affect feasibility; intention varies (D04/G01/P05/G02). | Whether work is wanted and available now; constraints the person chooses to disclose. |
| Discover and assess an opportunity | Occupational conditions, reported exclusion and unclear expectations arise in different sources (D01/D05/P02). | Actual incompatibility versus absent information versus interpretation; when decisive facts became known. |
| Prepare and submit | Sources describe repeated effort and changes in tactics (P01–P03). | Time, reuse, verification, reasons for changes, assistance and interruptions. |
| Assess and interview | Some accounts describe tasks before an application ends (P02/P04). | Necessary versus avoidable work; communicated expectations and actual decisions to continue. |
| Wait, follow up or change approach | Silence and uncertainty are reported (P01/P03/P04). | What the person knew, inferred and did; active effort separately from elapsed waiting. |
| Accept/start, decline, pause or resume | Conditions and employment status can change; outcomes use different definitions (P04/P05/D03/D06). | Whether a role remained workable and why the next transition occurred. |

Reported workarounds include coaching, revised materials, mentors, contacts and supported employment arrangements (P03–P05). These are experiences to investigate, not proven remedies. The workflow review separates **conditions/exclusion**, **information uncertainty**, and **process effort**. The same episode can contain all three; a clearer explanation cannot itself change pay, schedules or employer conduct.

## Segment implications and gaps

| Context | Useful coverage | What is still missing |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Fresh graduates | University outcomes and a graduate poll/interviews (D06/P02/P03). | Non-degree pathways, representative process burden, and within-group variation. |
| Mature/retrenched workers and switchers | Re-entry outcomes, historical reported discrimination and selected accounts (D03/D05/P03). | Direct switcher workflows; current causes of delay; no inference that discrimination caused an outcome statistic. |
| Returners/caregivers | Population context plus qualitative/selected accounts (D04/G01/G02/P04/P05). | Active search versus intention, feasible conditions and the timing of information discovery. Non-participation is not a prospective-user count. |
| Service, trades and operational workers | Employer-side occupational evidence and selected supported-employment cases (D01/P05). | Worker voices, small employers, informal routes and contemporary comparable effort measures. |

Accessibility, language, digital confidence, device access, assisted/offline routes and cross-device continuity remain gaps. Care demands do not by themselves prove interrupted application forms or lost work. See [limitations.md](limitations.md) for the complete uncertainty record.

## Reconciliation and confidence safeguards

- Aggregate outcomes and difficult individual searches can coexist; they measure different things. An eventual offer does not establish an easy process.
- Caregiver population context and selected constrained-choice accounts use different denominators. Neither establishes that all non-participation is voluntary or that everyone wants to return.
- Shared data/publisher families are not independent corroboration. The source register records these dependencies.
- Preserve P01's respondent-level interpretation, P05's unresolved interview-count composition, G02's age-description inconsistency, D06's changed outcome definition, D02's superseded population observation and D05's historical period.
- No retained evidence establishes verified ghost vacancies, a general abandonment rate, causal intervention effectiveness or adjudicated employer wrongdoing.

## Next action and stopping disposition

Stop desk collection for this run. The bounded initial pass and one targeted pass are complete; their useful output is a qualified validation shortlist. Further source volume would not by itself resolve comparative behavioural burden.

Recommend a separately approved, consented recent-episode interview and workflow-observation study. Reconstruct pursued, stopped and successful opportunities; distinguish recalled time estimates from artifact-backed timings; separate active work, waiting, emotional effort and actual decisions. Include underrepresented routes and practical constraints. Test whether the three hypotheses survive counterexamples before choosing a feature or platform.

The [Product Strategist's proposed protocol](specialists/product-review.md) makes this next activity concrete: eight adults with recent episodes, varied audience and industry contexts, a measurement check after four interviews, a stop after eight plus one cross-case review, and explicit counterexamples for each hypothesis. This is a pragmatic proposal, not a representative sample or a guarantee of saturation. Scope, recruitment, consent, privacy and participation arrangements require a separate human research decision; no outreach is authorised by this recommendation.

## Source register and evidence

### P01 — Applicant silence and application volume

- Source: [Job Application Overload: 88% of job applications go ignored](https://www.robertwalters.com.sg/insights/news/blog/candidate-ghosting.html), Robert Walters Singapore; published 2025-06-03; accessed 2026-10-18.
- Geography/population: Singapore employers and professionals. Online poll, combined n=308; field period, recruitment, weighting and seeker/employer subgroup denominators undisclosed.
- Classification: source-reported survey responses. Evidence: article introduction and “Quality and quantity of applications affecting response timings,” extraction lines 286–308.
- Relevant claims: 88% of jobseekers **feel** applications are often ignored; 58% report over five applications weekly; 61% say a long application process would discourage them.
- Calibration: the headline does not establish that 88% of individual applications receive no reply. Stated discouragement is not measured abandonment. Body percentages for often/sometimes ignored sum to 101%, plausibly rounding but unexplained.
- Independence/limits: original vendor poll, separate from CNA collection; recruitment-agency commercial interests and professional-worker bias. No causal or representative-population inference. Confidence: moderate that respondents report the problem; low for population prevalence.

### P02 — Graduate uncertainty and preparation burden

- Source: [Long hunts, few replies: Singapore university graduates finding it tough in job market, CNA poll shows](https://www.channelnewsasia.com/singapore/fresh-graduates-university-job-search-cna-poll-5288556), CNA; published 2025-08-13, updated 2025-08-14; accessed 2026-10-18.
- Data: 2025-08-07–10 straw poll, 105 Singaporean/PR graduates, ages 22–27, eight Singapore universities, 2024/2025 cohorts. Questions included ratings and selected challenge/confidence options; recruitment and weighting undisclosed.
- Classification: self-reported survey and journalistic interviews. Evidence: “How CNA conducted the straw poll,” “Fierce competition, multiple interviews,” and “Understanding employer expectations”; lines 324–368.
- Claims: 56.2% selected understanding employer expectations as something that would improve confidence. Interviewees described unclear role suitability and repeated assessments. One reported building a portfolio website after watching peers, initially without replies.
- Independence/limits: original nonvendor collection, separate from P01; same publisher as P03, not an independent publisher replication. Poll is not representative, preferences do not prove effective interventions, and preparation time was not measured. Confidence: moderate for existence within this sample, low for comparative burden.

### P03 — Coping and changing tactics

- Source: [From rejection to reinvention: Some fresh graduates, retrenched workers find their way out despite sluggish job market](https://www.channelnewsasia.com/cna-insider/big-read/fresh-graduates-retrenched-workers-job-market-5217811), CNA TODAY; published 2025-07-04, updated 2025-07-07; accessed 2026-10-18. Original `/today/` URL redirects to this canonical location.
- Geography/population/method: Singapore graduates and retrenched workers, selected journalistic interviews. Interview dates, recruitment and total sample unspecified; retrospective experiences span earlier periods, including 2024–2025.
- Classification: reported experiences and expert commentary. Evidence: opening account and “Struggles and breakthroughs / Seeking help from mentors and career coaches,” extraction lines 44–106.
- Claims: silence left an interviewee uncertain whether jobs existed or qualifications fell short. Another reported switching from repeated generic applications to tailored materials with a coach, subsequently receiving an offer. Mentor support also addressed confidence.
- Independence/limits: distinct reporting from P02 but common editorial publisher; success-story selection, recall bias and no counterfactual. An offer following coaching cannot establish coaching caused it. Employer intent and actual vacancy availability remain unknown. Confidence: moderate for illustrated experiences, low for effectiveness or prevalence.

### P04 — Returning mother's repeated late-stage effort

- Source: [‘Too Senior For Some, Too Junior For Others’: Mid-Career Mum’s Struggle To Find Her Job Fit](https://www.womensweekly.com.sg/your-life/mid-career-mum-back-to-work-struggle-find-job-fit), The Singapore Women's Weekly; published 2026-02-28; accessed 2026-10-18.
- Geography/population/method: Singapore, one returning marketing professional with caregiving responsibilities, journalistic account plus WSG coach advice. Interview timing and selection undisclosed; career break began 2024, retrospective search through publication.
- Classification: single reported experience; advice separately attributed. Evidence: introduction, “Dealing with the emotional toll of rejections,” and “Planning the next step,” lines 19–43 and 88–92.
- Claims: late rejections followed interviews, tasks and presentations; a temporary cover role did not become permanent. Reported workarounds included friends' leads and professional contacts; desired work had to accommodate family life.
- Independence/limits: different publisher from P02/P03; WSG support promotion and coach framing may shape presentation. No population frequency, measured time, or evidence of discrimination established. Exclude personal medical/family identifiers. Confidence: illustrative only; advice is not observed adoption or proven benefit.

### P05 — Constraints beyond professional job applications

- Source: [Singapore Business Federation Foundation Employability Fund Evaluation Report 2023](https://sbffoundation.org.sg/wp-content/uploads/2024/03/SBF-Foundation-Employability-Fund-Evaluation-report.pdf), SBF Foundation; evaluation by Empact. Cover 2023; exact publication date unknown (URL suggests March 2024 upload); accessed 2026-10-18.
- Singapore supported vulnerable populations. Evaluation Oct 2022–Aug 2023; programme outputs July 2020–June 2023. Methods: semi-structured Zoom interviews, thematic analysis and programme records. Report states 26 interviews; diagram identifies eight employees and 14 employer representatives plus separate agency meetings. Methods printed pp10,61–64 confirm three stakeholder groups but do not fully reconcile the count; interview composition remains ambiguous.
- Source-reported findings: caregiving, wages and stable schedules constrain feasible employment; one beauty-services case describes nearby work, childcare-compatible hours and training. Evidence printed pp16,36,54–55.
- Independence/limits: original commissioned evaluation, separate from media/vendor polls; funder and service partners participate in selection. Employee selection favours successful supported employment. Includes non-office work, but no general prevalence or present-day policy inference.
- Older-source rationale: unusually explicit qualitative methods and otherwise weakly represented populations; use for hypothesis formation only. Confidence: moderate for mechanisms in selected supported cases, low for contemporary generalisation.

## Findings and confidence

These are specialist findings for coordinator reconciliation, not a ranking of stages.

1. **Uncertainty after effort deserves direct investigation.** P01–P04 converge on reported silence, unclear suitability or late-stage rejection. P01 and P02 are distinct survey collections, but only P02 discloses a defined field window and respondent characteristics. Confidence is moderate that this experience exists in the professional/graduate groups studied, low that it is their greatest burden or universal.
2. **Application volume is an incomplete measure of progress.** Sources describe changes in preparation, help-seeking and targeting, but none establishes the additional effort or causal benefit. A design that rewards application count would require separate justification.
3. **Feasible work depends on life constraints.** P04 and P05 suggest that work hours, care responsibilities and employment continuity can shape choices before résumé quality matters. The newer case is professional; the methodologically richer cross-industry study is older and selected. Treat any shared mechanism as a question for validation.

No retained source directly observes users operating portals, tracks minutes per stage, measures abandoned applications, or estimates opportunity cost across stages. Low response is not proof of a ghost vacancy; perceived mismatch is not proof that a candidate met all requirements.

## Assumptions, contradictions and coverage gaps

- Apparent tension between high effort and eventual successful outcomes is compatible with heterogeneous experiences; do not present successful cases as refuting difficulty or difficult cases as population collapse.
- A graduate's claimed benefit from clearer expectations is a stated preference, not proof a product can recover employers' hidden criteria.
- Coaching/referrals are reported workarounds, not evidence that more tailoring, networking or AI necessarily improves results. No reliable verification-behaviour evidence was retained for AI-generated applications.
- Retained media evidence is professional/degree-heavy. Mature workers, trades, operational roles, non-English speakers, disability, low digital confidence and device constraints remain insufficiently covered. P05 adds selected vulnerable groups but cannot close these gaps.
- P02 and P03 share CNA editorial provenance. P05 involves Daughters of Tomorrow among several programmes; any further DOT-derived evidence must be checked for shared participants or organisational framing before being called independent corroboration.
- Financial pressure may alter acceptable work and search persistence; this collection does not measure that causal pathway.

## Recommended next action

Carry these bounded hypotheses into synthesis without feature selection:

1. Unclear suitability and weak feedback may make applicants repeat effort without learning what to change.
2. Preparation and assessment work may accumulate before candidates can judge whether an opportunity is viable.
3. Care, schedule and income constraints may exclude seemingly suitable roles before application preparation begins.

Recommend a future consented recent-episode interview and workflow-observation study, stratified across graduates, mature/retrenched workers, returners and service/operational workers. Ask participants to reconstruct one recent application and one rejected/abandoned opportunity using optional redacted artifacts. Capture stage durations, interruptions, uncertainty, actual decisions and help used. Do not collect employer/third-party personal information unnecessarily. The current approval does not authorise recruiting or contacting participants. Comparative burden and effect of a proposed solution need fresh evidence; a prototype is premature unless an interaction uncertainty is isolated.

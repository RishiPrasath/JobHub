**Recommendation:** compare two Singapore entry hypotheses: evidence-backed preparation for people moving between adjacent occupations, and practical job-condition comparison for frontline workers. Begin with a bounded workflow spanning non-IT occupations. Neither is established as the segment with strongest unmet need. Retain JobHub as the chosen working name; clearance remains unresolved.

## Initial-market decision matrix

MOM reports capability gaps and difficulties involving schedules, physical demands and pay [PM01]. These employer reports do not establish software demand. See the earlier run for survey coverage; small private establishments are excluded. Vacancy counts cannot size JobHub's paying market.

The matrix contains **testability and product-risk hypotheses**, not measured need. Recruitment routes are unconfirmed.

| Candidate segment and non-IT examples | Proposed value and evidence burden | Reach/cost hypothesis | Decision implication |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Mid-career switchers: logistics coordination, customer operations, business administration | Translate documented tasks into adjacent-role evidence; medium burden, with role-specific vocabulary | Career services/training cohorts may provide access; willingness to pay unknown | Preferred preparation pilot: test whether explanations change a real shortlist |
| Non-degree/service/operational workers: retail, hospitality, warehouse work | Compare shifts, commute, actual pay components, duties and training; lower CV emphasis | Community/worker organisations; assisted participation and free access may matter | Equal-priority alternative pilot; stronger if conditions dominate writing needs |
| Fresh graduates: business, design, engineering, care-related study | Connect projects, placements and volunteering to requirements; avoid inventing professional experience | Education career services; potential institution-funded route | Useful comparator; abundant preparation substitutes may reduce switching |
| Retrenched/mature workers across sectors | Reuse verified experience, assess adjacent roles, prepare concise examples | Career services/outplacement; financial pressure may limit self-payment | Include across pilots; urgency is not evidence of purchase intent |
| Returners after caregiving or health breaks | Separate current skills, dated experience and work constraints; optional gap narrative | Returner/community programmes; interrupted participation likely needs accommodation | Include across pilots; do not require disclosure of private reasons |
| Licensed trades, clinical and other regulated occupations | Credential scope, expiry and jurisdiction can dominate suitability; high evidence burden | Specialist associations/training providers | Include boundary cases; defer broad eligibility advice until official rules are verified |
| International candidates | Separate transferable capability from authorisation, location and sponsorship uncertainty | Access and programme eligibility unverified; high jurisdictional burden | Include research participants, but do not promise immigration eligibility or sponsor availability |

**Recommended selection rule:** compare task frequency, demonstrated correction burden, reachability, exclusion risk, and cost per useful completed task. Start with operations/administration transitions plus retail/hospitality comparisons to expose different needs. Choose one delivery slice only after observation. If sourcing or personal networks dominate participants' difficulties, a preparation workspace may be the wrong entry product; retain a coaching companion or opportunity-comparison aid as alternatives.

Sample language, disability, digital confidence and caregiving across rows. Digital confidence does not establish occupational competence.

## Truthful skills and essential versus trainable gaps

**Recommendation:** represent a skill as a claim linked to a candidate-confirmed example, its date/context, and evidence status. Accept descriptions of work, placements, projects, volunteering and informal responsibilities without requiring confidential employer documents. Separate self-report, supplied artifact, and independently verified credential; none automatically proves proficiency.

For example, confirmed retail scheduling responsibilities can illustrate coordination, but cannot establish warehouse-system expertise or invented percentage improvements. Let candidates approve translations.

| Requirement class | Proposed handling |
| --- | --- |
| Verified legal/occupational prerequisite | Link the relevant authority, jurisdiction and effective date; show unresolved status when missing |
| Employer-stated essential | Preserve the posting's language; do not silently downgrade it or assume it is a legal rule |
| Preferred/negotiable | Identify the exact qualifier and evidence the candidate can offer; employer discretion remains unknown |
| Plausibly trainable | Suggest a learning route and question about employer training; label feasibility as an inference |
| Unspecified or ambiguous | Ask for clarification; do not equate missing evidence with absent ability |

MOM's qualification-flexibility findings support examining alternatives, not declaring every requirement negotiable [PM01]. Conversely, the US nursing profile distinguishes state licensing from other education and certification considerations [PM02]. An occupational taxonomy can suggest vocabulary; it cannot substitute for the vacancy or regulator.

Show capability evidence, work preferences, practical constraints and unresolved requirements separately. Recommend an explainable comparison over a universal fit percentage. Offer actions such as apply with an honest example, ask about training, verify a credential, or retain the role for later. Never imply that rewriting makes an unheld skill true.

## Company research and conflicting evidence

**Observed:** ACRA identifies Bizfile as its registration, filing and business-information portal [PM03]. SEC provides free access to EDGAR filings by public companies and other filers [PM04]. These are credible routes to particular records, not endorsements of employers. No company record or financial filing was purchased or analysed here.

**Recommended evidence hierarchy by claim:** use entity records for legal identity; dated filings for disclosed financial facts; employer career pages for the employer's own description; regulator/court records for the specific matter they document; attributed reporting for independent investigation; employee reviews for reported experiences. This is not a universal credibility score. Registration does not verify a recruiter, and a parent company's figures need not describe a subsidiary or team.

Retain entity identifier, URL, event/report dates, scope and contrary evidence. Distinguish registered address from workplace and group headcount from local staff. Missing disclosure does not establish weakness or misconduct.

For anonymous allegations, attribute the account, preserve its age and location, and avoid identifying individuals or presenting accusations as facts. Reposts are not independent corroboration. Compare accounts with employer responses and available records; a denial alone does not settle the issue either. Where sources conflict, present both and identify the unresolved point. Example question: “How are overtime and weekend coverage scheduled and compensated?” is more useful than an unsupported “toxic employer” label. Serious unresolved allegations warrant restrained wording and specialist review before public publication; they should not drive an automatic blacklist.

**Alternative:** begin with identity checks and questions; defer unvalidated reputation synthesis.

## Competition and sustainable positioning

The prior run already covers general assistants, Teal, Huntr, Simplify, CareersFinder and NTUC's AI Career Coach. Their documented overlap remains context, not a fresh capability audit. Additional inspected offerings broaden the comparison:

| Offering | Observed documentation / reported capability | Implication and unresolved evidence |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Indeed Career Scout [PM05] | Career/company questions, recommendations refined through feedback, mock-interview feedback; uses profile, activity and chat context | Job-board distribution can absorb intelligence features. Inspected US FAQ does not establish Singapore access, accuracy, pricing or export |
| Big Interview [PM06] | Interview preparation and resume feedback; personal page lists $39/month, $99/three months, $299/lifetime; enterprise licensing offered | Episodic and institution-funded models exist. Currency not explicit in inspected price text; no checkout, outcome or accessibility test |
| Sonara [PM07] | Markets automated discovery and application submission; displays multiple occupational categories | Competes on reduced effort and volume. No verified pricing, submission accuracy, approval behavior or Singapore coverage |
| Public career matching / Polaris [PM08] | Official FAQs describe complimentary CMP coaching, fit assessment and preparation; separately describe paid Polaris career planning for employed people | Human coaching is both substitute and possible channel. Do not claim all public services are free; current eligibility/capacity unverified |

Vendor claims demonstrate positioning, not effectiveness. No comparative ranking, privacy-implementation assurance, or “competitors lack evidence grounding” claim is supported. Indeed's data-use explanation is a disclosure, not an audit. Export completeness, deletion, source visibility and correction burden need matched tasks across substitutes.

**Positioning hypothesis:** “Understand an opportunity and prepare an application you can substantiate.” Test evidence reuse, explicit uncertainty, occupation-sensitive constraints and coach handoffs. These are copyable; defensibility requires demonstrated trust, repeat usefulness and distribution relationships.

**Recommended access model:** keep a useful manual path, basic comparisons, corrections and portable exports free; bound expensive live research transparently. Test an optional short preparation pass alongside a subscription. Successful users may leave after finding work, so retention alone is a poor success measure. Model contribution after research/model usage, support, payment fees and acquisition costs; no present cost or margin estimate is supported.

An institution-funded cohort offer is a credible alternative, supported as a market pattern by Big Interview's enterprise offer [PM06], not proof that a Singapore partner wants JobHub. Career services, training providers and community organisations are candidate partners. Propose user-controlled sharing and separate commercial referrals from recommendations; approval of those privacy/commercial terms remains with the owner. Avoid financing recommendations through undisclosed placement or course incentives.

Price listings do not prove willingness to pay. Compare access models after the same task and record actual choices in an authorised later study. No payment experiment or outreach occurred.

## Singapore versus US expansion

**Recommendation:** revalidate terminology, credentials, identity routes, job sources, compensation, authorisation and local expectations before expansion. Singapore statistics and service eligibility cannot describe US users.

The inspected US nursing example has state-specific licensing [PM02]; this establishes a concrete localisation issue, not a complete US regulatory model. ACRA and SEC also serve different record purposes and populations [PM03–04]; EDGAR is not a universal US employer directory. US state business registries and occupation boards would require separate checks. International-candidate guidance needs current official authorisation sources before implementation; this report makes no immigration-law determination.

Prefer one occupation and jurisdiction for a later expansion test. An alternative is supporting user-supplied US postings with explicit verification gaps, without local eligibility advice. Neither expansion nor architecture is approved by this recommendation.

## Naming screen

**Observed public signals:** Personnel Link JobHub presents Singapore recruitment and executive-search services [PM09]. Japan's jobhub.jp presents JobHub matching and outsourcing under Pasona JOB HUB [PM10]. These independently published uses establish employment-related name overlap. They do not measure consumer confusion or determine trademark ownership, infringement, or availability. The chosen name remains JobHub.

**Recommended clearance route:** document exact/variant and similar-sounding searches in IPOS Digital Hub, with goods/services classification reviewed for the eventual offering [PM11]. For US use, follow USPTO guidance covering federal marks plus common-law uses, state registers and other relevant resources [PM12]. Include domains, app stores and handles in practical brand screening. No trademark-database result set, domain availability, social handle or app-store clearance was established here. Obtain a qualified clearance assessment before substantial public brand investment; an explanatory descriptor may improve understanding but does not resolve rights.

## Testable hypotheses and stopping rule

These are proposed tests, not completed research:

| Hypothesis | Bounded next test and decision evidence |
| --- | --- |
| H1: adjacent-role evidence reduces correction work | Compare one real preparation task with the participant's existing method; inspect every claim, correction time and shortlist reasoning. Reconsider if profile upkeep outweighs reuse |
| H2: frontline users value condition comparison more than rewriting | Compare two postings with schedules/pay details and missing fields; observe the decision and questions raised. Prefer this slice if it repeatedly changes useful decisions |
| H3: company evidence improves calibrated decisions | Use synthetic conflicting sources; check whether people distinguish allegations, employer claims and records. Simplify if warnings create false certainty |
| H4: sustainable access has a buyer | After demonstrated value, test explicit short-pass/subscription choices and separately assess sponsor procurement needs. Reject a model if cost or refusal defeats viability |

Use a small purposive pilot across both workflows and varied access needs. Report individual failures and selection limits. Obtain approval of research/consent plans before recruitment; desk research cannot establish strongest need or willingness to pay.

# Round 03 brief — industries, job-seeking experiences and vacancy credibility

Created: 2026-10-18; executed: 2026-10-19. This is the planning baseline; the owner subsequently authorised substantive research, industry profiles and updated slides. Read the [dated execution brief](runs/2026-10-19-cross-industry-and-vacancy-credibility/brief.md) and [results](runs/2026-10-19-cross-industry-and-vacancy-credibility/README.md) for actual work, review and remaining gaps.

## Owner's research objectives

1. Understand different industries, not only IT. Investigate the particular difficulties, expectations, pressures, practices and positive experiences people describe while seeking work in each context.
2. Investigate fake/ghost postings, including cases where an organisation may advertise without an immediate intention to hire. Determine what can responsibly be established and how people respond to the uncertainty.
3. Produce profiles of the different industry contexts, clearly separating breadth-only summaries from deeper inquiry.
4. Update the research slides after collection, using the agent team and retaining uncertainty and source links.

"Each sector has its drama" is treated as a request for context and lived accounts, not a premise that every employer in a sector behaves badly. Include workable hiring processes, legitimate explanations and successful/manual alternatives as well as painful experiences.

## Agent-recommended questions

These operationalise the owner's objectives; they are not claims that the issues occur in each sector.

- How are jobs found: boards, agencies, referrals, walk-ins, institutional recruitment, portfolios or other publicly documented routes?
- What qualifications, licences, practical experience or work samples matter, and who can authoritatively verify a requirement?
- How do applications, interviews, auditions, trials, assessments, checks and offers differ? What is paid, unpaid, unclear or merely alleged?
- What causes reported effort, uncertainty, rejection, repeated preparation, unsuitable conditions, or loss of context? What alternatives work?
- How do hours, shifts, location, physical work, caring commitments, access, language and digital confidence affect feasible options?
- How should basic pay, allowances, variable compensation, benefits and negotiability be interpreted within the actual source population?
- What use, non-use and verification of AI is reported, versus merely promoted or recommended?
- Which apparent sector differences may instead reflect occupation, seniority, employment arrangement, geography or source bias?
- What evidence supports a posting's hiring status or attributed intent? What remains unknowable from public data?
- Which implications are shared, sector-sensitive, outside software's control, or still too weak for a product decision?

## Scope and unit of comparison

Singapore-first. International material is a separately labelled comparison and cannot silently replace missing local evidence. Prefer the most recent 24 months at execution; retain older foundational sources or accounts only with their actual observation period and transfer limits. Publication, underlying events and access dates are separate fields.

Use the 13 [research baskets](sector-matrix.md) for purposive coverage. They are not an official industry taxonomy, mutually exclusive economic counts or a complete census of Singapore industries. Tag employer industry separately from occupation/job function and employment arrangement. A bank's software engineer is not automatically an IT-sector employer; an agency placing a nurse is not the healthcare employer merely because it advertises the role. Record ambiguity rather than force a code.

Focus on discovering, assessing, preparing, applying, interviewing, deciding and returning to unfinished work. Workplace experiences matter when they explain job choice, a transition, conditions to clarify or hiring practices. This is not an unlimited collection of workplace grievances.

No direct participant access is assumed. Public accounts are reported experiences, not observed behaviour. No private-drive rescan, contact, applications, group joining, mystery shopping, purchases, paid transcripts, large-scale scraping, implementation or provider comparison is part of this plan.

## Study design and bounded budget

### Pass A: map breadth before choosing depth

Screen all 13 baskets with up to two initial query strings per basket: one context/requirements route and one direct-experience/practice route. Complete a row even when the result is an access or evidence gap. Reuse prior sources with their original IDs/dates; do not count repeated reports as independent support.

### Pass B: six contrasting deep dives

The coordinator and Query Strategist record the six selections and rationale before deeper searches. Select variation and important evidence gaps, not the sectors with the most dramatic search results, easiest English-language coverage or apparent market size. At least five selected baskets are non-IT. Include frontline/on-site and desk-based work, credential-led and portfolio/practical routes, and at least two within-basket role contrasts. If those contrasts cannot be populated, record the gap rather than inventing cases.

Use up to three additional initial query strings per selected basket. Across each selected basket, seek independently sourced context, a well-contextualised reported job-seeking sequence and a positive/contrary case or documented unsuccessful search for one. One story is not representative; official guidance is not behavioural evidence. A source can inform multiple questions, but has one family identity.

### Pass C: fake/ghost-posting strand

Reserve eight initial queries for definitions, intent evidence, research methods, platform practices, scam distinctions, positive/alternative explanations and applicant responses. Route sources into each sector only where the original context supports it; do not copy an IT/US survey into every sector. A cross-sector absence of evidence remains a gap.

### Search ceiling

| Allocation | Maximum query strings |
| --- | ---: |
| Pass A: 13 baskets × 2 | 26 |
| Pass B: 6 baskets × 3 additional | 18 |
| Pass C: cross-sector credibility strand | 8 |
| Shared fallback reserve, allocated in the log | 14 |
| Total ceiling | 66 |

These are ceilings, not quotas or evidence-strength scores. Count every executed search string, including adaptations, language variants and discovery searches, once. Direct page opens are logged separately. No specialist receives an extra private budget. Default reserve limit: three extra queries per unresolved family; the coordinator may reallocate remaining reserve with a recorded reason, never exceed 66 without a new scope decision. Check effort after each pass; stop early where further searching adds little. Do not promise saturation or any number of usable sources.

Charge repeated executions, failed searches and venue/filter changes too. Adopt a second ceiling of **160 source-inspection attempts** across all workers: each requested page/document/transcript retrieval or browser navigation counts, including failures and repeats; a batch charges each resource separately. Search-result snippets are discovery, not inspected sources. Reading already retrieved material locally does not incur another attempt. Keep the inspection counter beside the query counter. Stop at the first applicable ceiling and report partial coverage; neither limit is permission to omit verification or silently expand the run.

## Fake/ghost-posting classification and safeguards

Use a requisition-role-location-time episode as the case unit, not a whole company's character. Link syndicated copies; allow multiple labels where a listing is both duplicated and stale, for example. This is a public snapshot supplemented by dated historical public records, not scheduled monitoring or a six-week follow-up.

Keep employer authenticity, visible listing status, current external recruitment, eventual hiring outcome and contemporaneous intent as separate questions. No immediate hiring, eventual non-fill and no intention to hire are different claims. The working categories below are research labels, not determinations about individual employers:

| Category | What to establish | What must not be inferred |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Vacancy status unresolved | Listing/source dates and what is actually observable | No response, age or reposting does not reveal intent |
| Current external recruitment | Attributable, dated evidence of active external recruitment, inactivity or an explicitly unknown state | A visible page or authentic company alone establishes an active opening |
| Stale/closed, duplicated or syndicated | Same vacancy identity, source and timing, closure or duplication evidence | Every duplicate is a fabricated vacancy |
| Disclosed evergreen or talent pool | Employer's explicit statement and whether a current vacancy is promised | A transparently labelled pool is automatically deception |
| Changed circumstances | Documented freeze, cancellation, budget change, internal fill or delayed start, with dates | Original advertisement necessarily lacked hiring intent |
| Employer-reported no immediate intent | Original attributed admission or research response, its definition and time frame | Self-report independently proves every listing's status |
| Corroborated misleading vacancy | Credible independent evidence tied to the specific representation, requisition and period; any deliberate-deception claim additionally needs evidence of contemporaneous intent | Misleading information alone proves intent, or a generic survey/isolated complaint establishes named-company misconduct |
| Scam/impersonation | Evidence about the actor, identity and requested action | A scammer's copy proves the legitimate company created it |

Separate observable listing properties, applicants' beliefs, employers' statements, researchers' inferences and independently established facts. Record competing explanations and uncertainty. No numerical fake-job probability, company blacklist or phrase-based accusation is an output.

For every prevalence claim, inspect the original source: definition, geography, field date, recruitment, sample, respondent role, denominator, question wording, weighting, sponsor and uncertainty. A percentage of managers who say they have ever posted without immediate intent is not the percentage of all current Singapore ads that are fake. Search hits, unresolved applications and platform removals are not automatically the same measure.

Pay, eligibility, labour rules, licence requirements and platform policies require authoritative current sources and role/jurisdiction scope. No personalised legal, salary or employment-rights advice follows.

## Evidence collection contract

Before collection, create a dated run with brief, adopted query plan, exact query log and source ledger. Each source record needs:

- Qualified run/source/family IDs, original URL, creator, date accessed, publication date and underlying period.
- Geography, employer industry, occupation, arrangement, seniority/entry route and job-search stage; use unknown where not disclosed.
- Type: first-person report, interview study, quantitative research, official context, guidance, posting, employer statement, platform policy, vendor claim or synthesis.
- Methods/population/denominator where relevant, inspected passages/pages/timestamps, independence and source-access limits.
- Narrow paraphrased observation before interpretation, positive/contrary evidence and alternative explanations.
- Claim-level label: observed fact, source-reported claim, interpretation, hypothesis or unknown. Record missing survey methods explicitly; confidence does not replace attribution.
- Posting authenticity, visible status, current external recruitment, eventual outcome and employer intent as separate fields, with what evidence can and cannot establish each.
- Evidence confidence, transfer limits and research implications labelled separately from proposed product ideas.

Do not infer sensitive attributes, identify anonymous authors, retain private contact information or paste private material into searches. Minimise publicly visible personal details too; public availability alone does not justify retention. Use neutral, plain language in notes and queries. Translation must preserve the original language, tool/method and uncertainty; do not invent multilingual coverage if it was not achieved.

## Completion and handoff

Stop when all 13 breadth rows have evidence or explicit gaps; six deep-dive selections and reports exist; the credibility categories and source methods are examined; contrary searches and source-family overlap are documented; and independent review has resolved material overclaims or marked unresolved matters. Reaching the budget instead closes the run as bounded/partial, with the missing objectives identified. Never turn budget exhaustion into a completed finding.

The synthesis should explain observed/reportable commonalities, conditional differences, contradictions, uncertainty and exclusions. It may suggest questions or test candidates for [03_analysis](../../../03_analysis/), but must not select the first sector, market, platform, architecture, automation level, legal position or product scope. No ranking of sector "drama", deception or customer value is permitted from convenience sources.

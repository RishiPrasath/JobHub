# Findings: how job seekers actually job-hunt

Date: 2026-11-07. Desk research; see [limitations.md](limitations.md) for how far to trust it. Evidence tags: **S** = a source the agent opened (official, university or survey page); **Sn** = seen only as a search snippet or secondary write-up; **A** = anecdotal (candidate reports); **I** = inference, not stated by a source.

## The whole journey

```
SEARCH -> APPLY -> INTERVIEW -> OFFER TO START
   ^                                   |
   |__ "search going badly": diagnose, upskill, or change target
```

People move back and forth between stages: they keep searching while interviewing, hold offers while others are pending, and return to search when responses stop.

## 1. Searching for jobs

| # | Stage | What the person does | Information looked for | Decision | Pain points |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1.1 | Set target and criteria | Decides role or sector, seniority, location, pay floor, work arrangement; mid-career people also decide whether government-supported schemes apply | The filters portals offer (industry, minimum salary, employment type, support-scheme tags on MyCareersFuture) | Stay in field or switch | Filters are coarse, so people scan many irrelevant results (Sn) |
| 1.2 | Choose channels | Uses job boards, company career pages, referrals and networking, recruiters, social media. Most use one or two channels | Which channel works for their level | Which mix to use | Uneven access to networks and referrals (A: a small 2025 graduate poll) |
| 1.3 | Set alerts and saved searches | Creates alerts by job title; on MyCareersFuture alerts are per title, so people make several | Matching new postings | Which alerts to keep | Mismatched alerts (general practice, not sourced) |
| 1.4 | Screen postings | Reads the posting for fit, pay range, requirements, and employer identity | Is the role real, who is the employer, is it a scam | Apply or skip | Scams: a 2025 survey reports 35% of Singapore job seekers met job scams and about 40% lost trust in online platforms (Sn). A listing may also exist only to meet a legal advertising rule (I, see below) |
| 1.5 | Research the company and contacts | Checks company pages, reviews, and LinkedIn for connections | Reputation and any route in | Whether to seek a referral | Finding a route in when there is no contact |
| 1.6 | Shortlist | Keeps a short list to apply to | Priority and deadlines | Which to apply to first | No source found on how people track a shortlist |

Singapore points: employers must advertise a job on MyCareersFuture for at least 14 days before offering it to a foreign hire (S, MOM), so some postings may not be actively open to locals (I). In 2024 about one in three fresh graduates and young PMETs were active MyCareersFuture users and about six in ten of those found work within six months, though they also used other platforms (S, MOM reply, 23 Sep 2025).

## 2. Applying

| # | Stage | What the person does | Information and documents | Decision | Pain points |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 2.1 | Decide to apply | Weighs fit, pay, location, pass eligibility, whether the role is real | Posting, salary expectation, work-pass status | Apply or skip | Vague postings; many apply broadly and fast: nearly half in one 2026 Monster release (Sn) |
| 2.2 | Pick a route | One-click apply on a platform; the company's own portal (usually an applicant tracking system); recruiter submission; referral | Which route reaches a human | Which route | One-click apply may not reach a person; 41% in one US survey had never got an interview that way (Sn) |
| 2.3 | Tailor CV and cover letter | Adjusts the CV to the posting, uses the posting's keywords, writes a short letter | Master CV, tailored versions, cover letter, portfolio or certificates | How much to tailor | Parsing errors; university guides advise simple layouts, standard headings, no graphics or text boxes, and no keyword stuffing (Sn, US) |
| 2.4 | Complete the portal | Creates an account, retypes CV details, uploads documents, answers screening questions (right to work, notice period, expected pay), sometimes does an assessment or video interview | Employment dates, references, contact details, expected salary | What to answer on salary and notice | Duplicate data entry; 61% reported an upload or technical error in one Monster report, 57% abandoned an application as too complicated in one US survey (Sn); automatic knock-out questions |
| 2.5 | Submit and keep proof | Submits; many keep no copy or job reference | Reference and date | None | Hard to track later (guidance, not measured) |
| 2.6 | Follow up | Some send one polite email after one to two weeks if a contact is known | Contact name | Whether to chase | Guidance only, no evidence of effect |
| 2.7 | Track and wait | Spreadsheet or notes by role, date, channel, status | Status | Which to chase or drop | Silence: 59% of workers rarely hear back and 33% never, in a 2024 Monster poll (Sn); figures vary by source |

Singapore points: Singpass login is needed to apply on MyCareersFuture (Sn). For foreign professionals, the employer or its agent files the Employment Pass; the candidate does not apply for it directly. The pass has a salary threshold and a points test (40 points). Early questions for the candidate are whether the employer sponsors passes (S, MOM).

## 3. Interviewing

| # | Stage | What the person does | Information needed | Decision | Pain points |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 3.1 | Invitation | Receives email or call, gives availability | Who is calling, role still open | Accept slot | Silence after applying: 67% felt ghosted after applying and 28% after interviewing in one US remote-job survey (Sn) |
| 3.2 | Recruiter or HR screen (15 to 30 minutes) | Confirms background, interest, expected pay, notice period | A short career pitch, current and expected pay | Continue or withdraw | Disclosing salary; not knowing if the role is open (A) |
| 3.3 | Hiring-manager interview | Answers behavioural and role questions; prepares examples (STAR: situation, task, action, result) and questions to ask | Team problems, role scope, 3 to 5 prepared stories | Shortlist | Anxiety about behavioural questions; unclear number of interviewers |
| 3.4 | Assessment (varies most) | Online aptitude or personality tests, case studies, presentations, work samples, simulations | Test format | Pass or fail gate | Unfamiliar formats; no reason given for failing |
| 3.5 | Panel or group round | Panel (3 to 8 people, usually 3 for graduate roles per one guide); group exercises | Group dynamics | Progress | Unclear scoring |
| 3.6 | Final round | With a senior leader or the future manager | Fit and values | Progress | Long waits |
| 3.7 | Follow-up and waiting | Thank-you note, one polite chase | Feedback date | Whether to chase | Timing advice is from career blogs, not studies |
| 3.8 | Reference and background checks | Gives referees and documents; consents to checks | Referee contacts, certificates, employment dates; criminal record checks where relevant | Whom to name | Not knowing who is called; delays (vendor sources, Sn) |

Singapore points: employer rounds vary widely and Singapore evidence is anecdotal (Glassdoor reports describe two to four rounds, taking two weeks to two months in total). A 2017 Randstad Singapore survey found 89% across Singapore, Hong Kong and Malaysia thought a hiring process over four weeks was too long, and the average reported process had risen from 30 to 68 days (S, CIPD summary, 2017, dated).

## 4. Offer to first day

| # | Stage | What the person does | Information needed | Decision | Pain points |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 4.1 | Receive the offer | Verbal, then written; asks for written confirmation and sets a response date | Pay, bonus terms, start date, title, probation, notice period, leave, hours, insurance | Accept, negotiate or hold | Verbal offers not confirmed in writing; short deadlines |
| 4.2 | Check terms against contract and handbook | Compares letter, contract and any referenced handbook | Notice before and after confirmation, bonus conditions, probation criteria | Do terms match what was discussed | Bonus rules and notice only in a handbook; salary in the contract differs from the one agreed |
| 4.3 | Compare offers | Side-by-side sheet of base, bonus, allowances, leave, medical, commute, growth | Total cash plus benefits | Which to take | Higher base but worse bonus or notice; annual vs monthly figures |
| 4.4 | Negotiate, or decide not to | Asks for pay, sign-on, start date, leave, notice terms | Market pay for the role, own record | What to ask and when to stop | Fear of withdrawal; fixed pay bands; not knowing what is negotiable. US surveys (2013 to 2020, self-reported) put those who negotiate between 29% and 55% (Sn, dated) |
| 4.5 | Check the employer before accepting | Looks at reviews, asks people who work there, checks registration, checks the job exists | Registration status, turnover or layoff news, red flags such as urgency or pressure | Accept, decline, or ask for more time | Noisy reviews; little public information on small firms; fake-job scams, which Singapore police warn about repeatedly (advance fees, WhatsApp and Telegram task scams, impersonation of agencies and brands; S) |
| 4.6 | Accept or decline | Written acceptance, confirms start date; or declines politely | Signed contract | Final yes or no | A rescinded offer; reopening a verbal yes |
| 4.7 | Resign and work notice | Checks contract notice, resigns in writing, agrees last day, hands over | Notice terms, leave payout, counteroffer | Stay or go if counteroffered | Counteroffers: dated small surveys suggest most employers distrust people who accept one (Sn, old) |
| 4.8 | Onboard and probation | Signs, completes forms (Singpass, bank, tax; CPF through the employer), learns probation criteria | Probation terms, first pay date, reporting line | Settle or keep looking | Unclear probation criteria; role differs from offer; pro-rated first month |

Singapore points: Key Employment Terms must be given in writing (secondary sources citing MOM); a probation period does not automatically make a contract fixed-term and contract notice terms apply (S, MOM written answer, 9 Sep 2026); offers should state pay, benefits, duties and start date, and candidates should get enough time to consider (S, TAFEP, 2026).

## 5. When the search is going badly

People diagnose (channels, CV fit), widen the target, ask for CV feedback, try referrals, take a short course, or change role level or sector. Time spent searching tends to fall over a long spell and callbacks decline (US and academic sources; Sn). In Singapore, Workforce Singapore and e2i provide career coaching and matching, and Career Conversion Programmes give employers support to hire and train mid-career switchers (Sn).

## 6. How the processes differ by person

| Person | Search | Apply | Interview | Offer and start |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Fresh graduate | Campus portals, MyCareersFuture, recruiters; some start months before graduation | Portals; few documents beyond CV; competition is the main pain | Aptitude tests, preliminary interview, panel or assessment centre, final | Starting salary is the first anchor; unsure what is negotiable; high exposure to fake internship and task scams |
| Mid-career switcher | Networking and recruiters; sector change; whether support schemes apply | Heavy tailoring to explain the switch; keyword mismatch | Screen probes the switch; transferable skills | Pay cut or flat change; counteroffers; probation risk in an unfamiliar role |
| Experienced professional | Mostly recruiters, LinkedIn, referrals; often passive | Fewer applications | Fewer but more senior rounds | Negotiates whole package; long notice periods; reference check reaches current employer |
| Foreign professional | Needs an employer that sponsors a pass | Screening questions on pass status | Pass timing affects offers | Offer may depend on the pass; start date depends on pass timing; common target of sponsorship scams |
| Low-wage worker | Word of mouth, brokers, walk-ins, government career centres | Online-only steps are a barrier | Little evidence | Hourly or monthly pay and shifts matter more than benefits; agent fees and deposit scams |

Evidence for the foreign professional and low-wage worker rows is the thinnest (mostly snippets and commentary).

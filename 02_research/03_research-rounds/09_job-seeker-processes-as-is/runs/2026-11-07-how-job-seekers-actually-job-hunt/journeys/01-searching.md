# Journey: Searching for jobs
Status: as-is, desk research, no product ideas. Source: round 09.

## Scope and who
The journey starts when a person sets a target for the kind of job they want. It ends when they have a short list of postings to apply to, or when they decide the search is going badly and change course. It covers the five person types in the source: fresh graduates, mid-career switchers, experienced professionals, foreign professionals and low-wage workers. The setting is Singapore. Evidence is desk research, mostly search snippets (Sn) and old or US data. Treat it as a baseline to check, not a finding to rely on.

## Goal, trigger, end state
- **Goal:** find postings worth applying to. The findings do not state the goal in these words (I).
- **Trigger:** not stated in the sources. The findings describe the start as deciding a target role or sector, seniority, location, pay floor and work arrangement.
- **End state:** a short list of postings to apply to, in priority order (1.6). Otherwise the person loops back because "search is going badly" (section 5).

Singapore points: employers must advertise a job on MyCareersFuture for at least 14 days before offering it to a foreign hire (S, MOM), so some postings may not be actively open to locals (I). In 2024 about one in three fresh graduates and young PMETs were active MyCareersFuture users, and about six in ten of those found work within six months; they also used other platforms (S, MOM reply, 23 Sep 2025).

## Moments that matter
- **Stage 1.1, stay or switch.** The decision to stay in the field or change sector shapes every later filter and channel. Mid-career people add a question about support schemes.
- **Stage 1.2, channel mix.** Most people use only one or two channels (S/Sn, 2015 data), and access to networks is uneven (A).
- **Stage 1.4, apply or skip.** This is where scams and possibly non-open listings are filtered out by the person alone. 35% of Singapore job seekers met scams (Sn), and trust in online platforms fell for about 40% (Sn).
- **Stage 1.5, seek a route in or not.** With no contact, there is no clear route in (stage 1.5 pain point).
- **Section 5, search going badly (loop back to 1.1 or 1.2).** People diagnose channels and CV fit, widen the target, ask for CV feedback, try referrals, take a short course, or change role level or sector. Time spent searching tends to fall over a long spell and callbacks decline (US and academic sources; Sn). Workforce Singapore and e2i offer career coaching and matching; Career Conversion Programmes support employers hiring and training mid-career switchers (Sn).

## How it differs by person

| Person type | What changes in this journey |
|---|---|
| Fresh graduate | Campus portals, MyCareersFuture and recruiters; some start months before graduation. Competition is the main pain. |
| Mid-career switcher | Networking and recruiters; sector change; whether support schemes apply. |
| Experienced professional | Mostly recruiters, LinkedIn and referrals; often passive. |
| Foreign professional | Needs an employer that sponsors a pass. Thin evidence (mostly snippets and commentary). |
| Low-wage worker | Word of mouth, brokers, walk-ins, government career centres. Thin evidence (mostly snippets and commentary). |

## Gaps in the evidence
- How people track a shortlist: no source (findings.md; limitations.md).
- Singapore time to response, current job-search duration, and drop-off by stage: nothing found.
- Foreign professionals' search behaviour and low-wage workers' channels in Singapore: nothing found.
- Channel-use figures are from 2015 and 2016. Most survey figures are snippets, some from vendors with a commercial interest.
- Not verified: MyCareersFuture filter names and eligibility wording; whether the advertising rule makes some postings not open to locals (inference).
- Pages that did not load include the MyCareersFuture FAQ PDF and a LifeSG job-search guide.
- The research was done by small-model agents and the lead did not re-open primary sources.

## Update from the gap-fill research (2026-11-07)
Source: [gap-fill findings](../../2026-11-07-gap-fill/findings.md). Changes to the evidence above:
- **1.2 Channels:** Hays 2017 (Singapore): job boards 45%, networking 31%, social media 24%; Cisco 2023. Dated, so labelled dated.
- **1.3 Alerts:** Hays 2018 poll: only 16% of Singapore candidates receive phone job alerts. The claim that alerts are widely used was not sourced.
- **1.4 Screening postings:** scams are officially quantified (SPF: 5,575 job-scam cases, about $123.5M lost in 2025; 2,247 cases in H1 2026). MOM states some employers advertise only as a formality (extent unknown; this replaces the earlier inference). Official checks: ScamShield 1799, ACRA register, MOM Employment Agency directory.
- **Search going badly:** US and German studies: about 70 to 81 minutes a day; search time falls over a long spell.
- **Foreign professionals:** the employer or its agent applies for the Employment Pass; no survey of how they search. **Low-wage:** TWC2 2025 (172 Work Permit holders, self-selected): 65% found jobs through an agent, 5% online; no data on local low-wage workers.
- **Still unknown:** how people track shortlists (1.6) and whether alerts help.

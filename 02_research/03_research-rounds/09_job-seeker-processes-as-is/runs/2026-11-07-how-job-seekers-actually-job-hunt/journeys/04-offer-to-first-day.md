# Journey: From offer to first day
Status: as-is, desk research, no product ideas. Source: round 09.

## Scope and who
The journey starts when the person receives an offer (usually verbal, then written). It ends when they have signed, worked or left their notice, and are settling into probation. It covers fresh graduates, mid-career switchers, experienced professionals, foreign professionals and low-wage workers, with Singapore points where the source gives them. Evidence is desk research and mostly snippet-level (see gaps).

## Goal, trigger, end state
- **Goal:** Take the right job on terms they understand, and start it cleanly. (I)
- **Trigger:** An offer arrives, often verbal first, with a response date.
- **End state:** A signed contract, a resignation and handover done where needed, forms completed, and a clear view of probation terms and the first pay date.

## Moments that matter
- **Verbal to written (4.1):** the person needs the offer in writing with a response date; verbal offers and short deadlines are a worry.
- **Terms mismatch (4.2):** if contract or handbook differs from what was discussed, the person may loop back to negotiate (4.4) or hold the offer.
- **Negotiate or not (4.4):** fear of withdrawal and uncertainty about what is negotiable; Sn, dated, US data.
- **Verify before yes (4.5):** can send the person to decline, or to ask for more time; scam exposure is a worry (S).
- **Counteroffer (4.7):** a stay-or-go decision at resignation (Sn, old); staying loops back to the previous job.
- **Offer lost or reopened (4.6):** a rescinded offer or reopening a verbal yes can send the person back to search (round 09 section 5: when the search is going badly).

## How it differs by person

| Person type | What changes in this journey |
|---|---|
| Fresh graduate | Starting salary is the first anchor; unsure what is negotiable; high exposure to fake internship and task scams |
| Mid-career switcher | Pay cut or flat change; counteroffers; probation risk in an unfamiliar role |
| Experienced professional | Negotiates the whole package; long notice periods; reference check reaches current employer |
| Foreign professional | Offer may depend on the pass; start date depends on pass timing; common target of sponsorship scams. Thin evidence (mostly snippets and commentary) |
| Low-wage worker | Hourly or monthly pay and shifts matter more than benefits; agent fees and deposit scams. Thin evidence (mostly snippets and commentary) |

## Gaps in the evidence
- Singapore negotiation rates, typical pay uplift and counteroffer outcomes: nothing found.
- Negotiation and counteroffer figures are US or UK, 2013 to 2020, self-reported.
- Key Employment Terms content, notice-period bands, CPF rates and Employment Pass thresholds are not verified; the MOM page states some figures for 2027.
- Background check coverage and turnaround come from vendor blogs.
- Only a few official pages were opened; the lead researcher did not re-open sources. Haiku agents did the first pass, so treat the notes as leads.
- Low-wage workers in Singapore and foreign professionals: thin.
- Stages 4.3, 4.6 and 4.8 (except probation) carry no evidence tag.

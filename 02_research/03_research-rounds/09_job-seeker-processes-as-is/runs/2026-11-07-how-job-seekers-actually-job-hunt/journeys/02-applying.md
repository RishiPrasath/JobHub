# Journey: Applying for a job
Status: as-is, desk research, no product ideas. Source: round 09.

## Scope and who
The journey starts when a person sees a posting and weighs whether to apply (2.1). It ends when they have submitted and are tracking and waiting for a reply (2.7). It covers five person types: fresh graduate, mid-career switcher, experienced professional, foreign professional, and low-wage worker. Evidence is mostly search snippets, secondary write-ups, and old, US or vendor data (limitations.md), so treat all numbers as indicative.

## Goal, trigger, end state
- **Goal:** get a role in front of a human who can consider the person.
- **Trigger:** a shortlisted or newly seen posting, or a referral or recruiter lead (findings.md sections 1 and 2).
- **End state:** an application is submitted and the person is waiting, following up, or moving on. Interviewing starts only if a reply comes.

## Moments that matter
- **Stage 2.1, apply or skip:** the person must judge whether the role is real and open to them, with little to go on. The scam rate (35% of Singapore job seekers met scams, 2025 survey; Sn) makes this a trust decision too.
- **Stage 2.2, route choice:** picking a route that may never reach a person (41% no interview from one-click, US; Sn).
- **Stage 2.4, portal drop-off:** retyping and technical errors lead some to abandon (57%, US; Sn).
- **Stage 2.4, salary and notice answers:** asked early, with no stated guidance on what to say.
- **Stages 2.5 to 2.7, silence:** no reply sends people back to follow up, drop the role, or loop back to searching (findings.md "The whole journey" and section 5).

## How it differs by person

| Person type | What changes in this journey |
|---|---|
| Fresh graduate | Portals; few documents beyond the CV; competition is the main pain. |
| Mid-career switcher | Heavy tailoring to explain the switch; keyword mismatch. |
| Experienced professional | Fewer applications. Little else supported. |
| Foreign professional | Screening questions on pass status. The employer or its agent files the Employment Pass, so the candidate does not apply for it directly (S, MOM). Thin evidence. |
| Low-wage worker | Online-only steps are a barrier. Thin evidence. |

## Gaps in the evidence
- How people track applications: no source found.
- Time to response and drop-off by stage in Singapore: nothing found.
- Singapore evidence is thin; most figures are US, vendor, dated, or snippet-level, and some come from small or convenience samples.
- Not verified: MyCareersFuture application steps and eligibility wording.
- Follow-up and keeping proof are guidance, not measured.
- Foreign professional and low-wage worker rows are the thinnest; little on low-wage behaviour in Singapore.

## Update from the gap-fill research (2026-11-07)
Source: [gap-fill findings](../../2026-11-07-gap-fill/findings.md). Changes to the evidence above:
- **2.1 Postings:** MOM Fair Consideration Framework: advertise at least 14 consecutive days; foreign-hire postings must show a clear salary range (maximum at most twice the minimum); advertising only as a formality risks debarment of 12 to 24 months and prosecution. Pay quoting in other postings is unknown.
- **Foreign professionals (2.1, 2.4):** MOM: the employer or agent files; the candidate consents and fills a form; online decisions in 10 business days (up to 6 weeks for overseas firms without a Singapore office); no appeal for the candidate.
- **2.6 Follow-up and 2.7 Waiting:** no study shows following up changes outcomes; no Singapore days-to-reply figure; MOM does not track ghosting. How people track applications: still unknown.

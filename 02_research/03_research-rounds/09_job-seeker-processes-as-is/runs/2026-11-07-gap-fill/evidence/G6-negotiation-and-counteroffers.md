## Disagreements between sources
- Job-change uplift: a search snippet said JobStreet gives 12 to 15%; the page gives no figure and its percentages are examples. RGF's expected rises (17% and 28%) differ widely. Hays gives only a threshold (43% got more than 10%). No source gives a Singapore average from payroll data.
- Share who negotiate: US figures run from about 42% (Fidelity 2022) to 54% (Robert Half 2020); a Glassdoor figure of 41% in a snippet is in that range; graduates reported at 38% (NerdWallet). Differences come from samples and question wording.
- Counteroffer retention: short-term departure is 27 to 38% within 6 months (Japan, UK) and 47 to 74% within a year; the Singapore Hays data shows a different pattern (30% staying 12 months or more). The popular "80% leave" claim traces to no named survey.
- Employer willingness: Robert Half says 98% of CFOs are open to negotiating something; CareerBuilder's undated page says about three in four employers weren't flexible on starting pay for graduates. Samples, roles and years differ.
- Success rates: Hong Kong's raise success rate 77%; Fidelity's US figure 85%. They measure different things (raises versus offers).

## Still unknown
- Share of Singapore job seekers who negotiate an offer, and how many succeed (no named Singapore survey).
- A payroll-based average uplift for Singapore job changers (MOM wage tables not opened).
- Singapore counteroffer rates or retention after 6 or 12 months from a recent survey (only 2016 and 2017 data).
- What Singapore employers will concede beyond base pay.
- Whether fresh graduates in Singapore negotiate at all, and how.
- Any measured rate of offers rescinded after negotiation in Singapore or Asia.
- The original text of Hart, Bear and Ren (2024), and the source and year of the "57% never negotiated" MyCareersFuture figure and the Salary.com "87%" figure.

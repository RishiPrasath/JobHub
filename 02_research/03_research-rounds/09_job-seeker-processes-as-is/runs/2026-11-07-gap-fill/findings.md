# Gap-fill findings (2026-11-07)

Brief: [brief.md](brief.md). Raw agent notes: [evidence/](evidence/) (G1 to G8). Eight research agents (Sonnet-class) worked from the brief; the lead then re-checked consequential claims against the cited pages.

## Short answer
The gap-fill **fixed the rules-and-statistics gaps** (Singapore employment rules, official scam statistics, pass process, agent-fee limits, review-site bias) and **did not fix the behaviour gaps** (how long candidates wait, how they track applications, how often people negotiate, how low-wage workers search, what SMEs do). Those are not published in Singapore in a form we could find. For the review deck this means: facts about rules and scams can be shown with official sources; claims about what job seekers do must stay labelled as limited or unknown.

## What changed, by workstream
- **G1 Singapore rules:** notice tiers, Key Employment Terms (written within 14 days of the first day), probation notice, CPF rates, the 14-day advertising rule and its misuse penalties are now backed by official pages. Still not verified: the Employment Act text, any probation cap, how CPF pro-rates a mid-month start, the Workplace Fairness Act's recruitment duties and its small-firm exemption (targeted for end-2027), and which background checks need the candidate's own consent.
- **G2 timelines and ghosting:** no 2022 to 2026 Singapore figure for days to hear back. Best available: Employment Hero 2023 (employers, 515 leaders): about 42 days to hire in Singapore; Randstad 2017 (job seekers): process average rose from 30 to 68 days and 91% in Singapore would reject an offer after four weeks of process; Indeed 2026 (no sample stated): about 60% find excessive interview rounds a major frustration; a Robert Walters LinkedIn poll (self-selected): 88% say they are often ignored. MOM does not track ghosting; the Tripartite Standard on Recruitment Practices is voluntary and asks employers to tell unsuccessful interviewees the outcome. Graduates: 87.1% employed within six months in 2024, 79.5% in full-time permanent jobs; no time-to-first-job figure. MOM's median-duration-of-unemployment series exists but its values could not be read; worth fetching by hand.
- **G3 tracking, shortlists, alerts:** still no survey of how people track applications or whether alerts help, and no study that following up changes outcomes. Academic evidence on search effort (US/German): about 70 to 81 minutes a day; search time falls over a long spell; employed searchers apply less. Hays 2017 (Singapore): job boards 45%, networking 31%, social media 24%.
- **G4 foreign professionals:** Employment Pass facts are now from official pages: the employer or its agent applies; the candidate gives consent and fills a form; online applications are processed within 10 business days (up to 6 weeks for overseas firms without a Singapore office); the candidate cannot appeal a rejection; the candidate should not start work before the pass is issued; thresholds and the points framework (40 points) are dated, with higher figures from 1 Jan 2027 for new applications. No survey of how foreign professionals search. One alleged work-pass fraud case (forged approvals, Jul 2026) reported by a newspaper.
- **G5 low-wage workers:** official fee rules and Progressive Wage Model floors are now sourced. TWC2's 2025 survey (172 Burmese Work Permit holders, self-selected, not representative): 65% found jobs through an agent, 5% online, 87% paid, average fee about S$3,700 (labels in the report are inconsistent). Advocacy groups report much higher fees for construction workers (S$13,000 to 18,000), from a snippet. No data on how local low-wage workers search or on online barriers.
- **G6 negotiation and counteroffers:** no Singapore rate for how many negotiate or succeed. US: 54% negotiated (Robert Half 2020) or about 42% (Fidelity 2022), and 85% of those who countered got something. Hays Singapore: 43% of 2025 job-changers got raises above 10% (self-report, not payroll). Counteroffers: a UK survey of 2,000 employees (2021) found 53% of those offered accepted and 74% of the accepters had left within 12 months; Singapore data are from 2016 and 2017. Evidence that managers rarely withdraw an offer after a counter is from a review seen via a summary.
- **G7 interview practice:** one public-service agency lists two interview rounds with psychometric tests between them and an offer within two weeks of the final interview; other agency details are from blocked pages. The PDPC guidelines say a former employer's reference needs no candidate consent but the employer must notify. MOM (via a trade-press report) says employers should not be required to give rejection reasons. Nothing credible on how SMEs differ, on whether the current employer is contacted before an offer, or on named large-employer stage lists beyond candidate reviews.
- **G8 scams and verification:** official SPF statistics confirmed (see table). Main variants: online task jobs and social-media boosting, impersonation, and Singpass misuse. Official verification routes: ScamShield 1799, the ACRA register, and MOM's Employment Agency directory. MOM says it validates vacancies against MyCareersFuture and does not treat multi-portal posting as fictitious; no removal counts. A 2026 Indeed survey (1,000 Singapore respondents, read through a secondary report) puts official company emails and the role appearing on the company site as the top legitimacy signals. Review-site bias is documented in peer-reviewed work (not Singapore-specific).

## Corrections to earlier round-09 content
- The earlier note that Singapore job-scam totals were unverified is replaced by the SPF figures above.
- The earlier guess that some postings may not be open to locals (an inference) is now supported by MOM's own statement, with the extent unknown.
- Hays 2018: only 16% of Singapore candidates receive phone job alerts (a 2018 poll); the earlier claim that alerts are widely used was not sourced.

## What remains unknown (carry to the deck as labelled gaps)
Days to hear back and drop-off by stage in Singapore; how people track applications and whether alerts or follow-ups help; Singapore negotiation and counteroffer rates; how local low-wage workers and foreign professionals search; SME differences; whether the current employer is contacted before an offer; the Employment Act text, the Workplace Fairness Act's recruitment duties and exemption; MOM's median unemployment duration value; named large-employer stage lists from their own pages.

## Readiness for slides
Ready for a **review deck** that shows the processes, labels evidence strength, and lists these gaps openly. Not ready to claim measured behaviour for Singapore job seekers in several areas above. Further research there would need primary data (for example interviews or a survey of job seekers), not more desk research.

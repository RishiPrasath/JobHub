# Limitations and unresolved questions

## What this run cannot establish

- It does not establish Singapore-wide prevalence of AI use, tracking, resume tailoring or live interview assistance.
- It does not establish that any AI tool, format, match score or resume practice improves an individual’s hiring outcome.
- It does not establish how often employers use automated ranking or what thresholds a particular ATS applies.
- It does not establish the prevalence of harmful conditions in domestic work from a small qualitative study, nor dismiss them from a larger satisfaction survey.
- It does not provide comparable wage evidence for every industry, occupation, worker status or arrangement.
- It is not legal, accessibility-conformance or provider-security certification.

## Evidence limitations

Public sources overrepresent official programmes, regulated sectors, employers with resources to publish material and vendor guidance. User reports are self-selected and can be promotional or unverifiable. International experiments and surveys have different populations and labour markets. Tool policies and prices can change after the research date. No participant testing, screen-reader session, paid-tool benchmark or live application was performed.

## Unresolved questions for later work

1. Which role-fit fields are genuine hard gates, strong signals, trainable gaps or preferences in each target context?
2. How do residents, migrants, returners and career switchers actually discover roles across the four contexts?
3. Which work-pattern distributions matter most: shifts, deployment, live-in work, physical exposure, care duties or on-call expectations?
4. How often do employers accept adjacent experience without formal sector experience?
5. How should wage evidence handle part-time, live-in, agency-mediated, overtime and non-resident arrangements?
6. Can users identify and correct every AI-generated change without losing surrounding work?
7. Which document checks are useful across real Singapore submission channels?
8. What does “personal voice” mean to candidates and how can voice loss be measured?
9. What automation boundary do job seekers consider useful, safe and explainable?

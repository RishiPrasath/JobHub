# Round 04 research brief — draft

Status: owner briefing captured; a bounded job-posting-structure run has been executed. The wider Round 04 plan remains a draft.

## Decision context

How might JobHub support truthful, role-specific job preparation across a wider range of industries while keeping AI assistance understandable, reviewable and under the job seeker's control?

This draft extends Round 04 in two connected directions:

1. broaden the industry context beyond the 13 profiles already researched;
2. understand how resumes/CVs are adapted to different roles and industries, including content and formatting choices.

The two tracks must remain connected but analytically separate. An industry profile describes a hiring context; it does not prove that every employer or role in that industry uses the same process.

## Owner's research objectives

### Objective 1 — Extend industry coverage

Develop additional, clearly bounded profiles for industries or employment contexts that are absent, thinly covered, or materially different from the existing Round 03 set. Candidate areas for prioritisation include:

- agriculture, aquaculture and food production;
- utilities, energy, water and waste services;
- domestic work and household services;
- offshore, maritime and seafaring work;
- defence-related recruitment where public evidence is appropriate;
- executive, senior leadership and specialist search;
- other locally relevant sectors identified by the Research Planner after a coverage-gap review.

The final list must be selected using an explicit rationale based on evidence gaps, distinct hiring conditions, relevance to JobHub and feasible public-source coverage—not simply because a sector appears interesting or dramatic.

Each additional profile should investigate, where evidence exists:

- how people find roles and who intermediates the opportunity;
- typical eligibility, licences, credentials, practical experience or work samples;
- application, screening, interview, trial, assessment and offer stages;
- location, schedule, physical, language, access and travel constraints;
- pay structure, allowances, variable compensation and questions a candidate needs to clarify;
- role-specific preparation and evidence of fit;
- reported uncertainty, workarounds, positive routes and failure modes;
- what remains unknown because public evidence is weak or unavailable.

### Objective 2 — Research truthful resume/CV tailoring across industries

Identify techniques people and credible practitioners recommend or report for adapting a resume/CV to a specific role without inventing experience, qualifications, outcomes, employers, dates or metrics.

The research should compare how tailoring changes with:

- industry and occupation;
- seniority and career stage;
- regulated or credential-led work;
- practical, trade, frontline and shift-based work;
- portfolio, audition, commission and project-based work;
- office, professional, technical and digital roles;
- career changes, transferable skills, employment gaps and return-to-work situations.

### Objective 3 — Examine content and formatting choices

Investigate role-appropriate resume/CV content and presentation, including the trade-offs between applicant-tracking-system compatibility, human readability, accessibility, truthful evidence, visual design and the expectations of different industries.

This must examine formatting as a means of communication, not as a promise that any particular template guarantees an interview.

### Objective 4 — Identify suitable roles and understand personal market value

Research credible and time-tested techniques for finding roles that suit a person's skills, experience, preferences, constraints and goals. Examine how job seekers can make their preferences explicit, discover preferences they have not yet articulated, and compare possible roles without reducing fit to keyword similarity alone.

Also investigate how people can form a grounded view of their market value from their skills, experience, qualifications, demonstrated outcomes, role context, location, seniority and current market evidence. The research must distinguish informed self-assessment from unsupported salary claims, comparison anxiety or a tool-generated score.

### Objective 5 — Map the common structure of job postings

Identify the sections and fields that recur across the job boards and employer-facing recruitment systems already encountered in JobHub research. Understand which fields are consistently available, which are labelled differently, which are optional, and which are frequently missing or ambiguous.

The purpose is to create a research-backed vocabulary and comparison model for reading job postings across industries—not to assume that every board or employer uses one standard schema.

#### Preliminary working inventory from Chrome spot-checks

This is an initial observation from visible postings checked on 19 October 2026. It is a starting list for Round 4 and requires broader sampling before being treated as a common or prevalent structure.

- job title and sometimes a reference or requisition number;
- employer or recruiting intermediary;
- employer overview or company profile;
- location, worksite or travel area;
- employment type, such as full-time, part-time, contract, temporary or permanent;
- working hours, shift pattern, days per week, flexibility and overtime;
- salary, hourly rate, allowances, commission, bonus or other compensation details;
- role overview or introduction;
- duties, responsibilities or expected activities;
- required qualifications, licences, credentials and experience;
- preferred or additional qualifications and skills;
- work conditions, eligibility, language or residency requirements;
- benefits, training, development or employer promises;
- application route, contact details, apply button or external application link;
- application process, response expectations, closing date or next steps;
- posting metadata such as date updated, expiry date, closing date or response-time indicator;
- save, share, report or job-alert functions provided by the platform;
- related roles, employer vacancies or recommendation links;
- platform-specific tags, match scores, translations, categories or screening fields.

Initial visible examples include [Indeed Singapore](https://sg.indeed.com/viewjob?jk=9aba925da6377e2e), [MyCareersFuture](https://www.mycareersfuture.gov.sg/job/logistics/class-4-driver-%E2%80%93-port-delivery-operations-quantum-forward-6e362b2ae87a50d1fd6608e5109fa020), [FastJobs](https://www.fastjobs.sg/singapore-job-ad/2636450/vehicle-mechanic/rexy-management-pte-ltd/) and [Careers@Gov](https://jobs.careers.gov.sg/jobs/greenhouse/4007889201). ## Questions to investigate

### Industry coverage questions

- Which additional industries or employment contexts deserve a profile, and why?
- What is genuinely different about the job-seeking and hiring process in each selected context?
- Which apparent differences are actually caused by occupation, seniority, location, employment arrangement or regulation?
- What evidence is available from official sources, employers, professional bodies, worker accounts, public guidance and credible research?
- Which findings are Singapore-specific, and which are only international comparisons?

### Resume/CV tailoring questions

- What information should be prioritised for a particular role, and how should the job description be translated into truthful evidence from the candidate's background?
- How do candidates express transferable skills without relying on vague claims or overstating equivalence?
- How should responsibilities, achievements, tools, qualifications, licences, outcomes, portfolios and practical experience be represented in different contexts?
- How should a candidate handle missing qualifications, employment gaps, career changes, short contracts, freelance work, caregiving, volunteer work or limited experience?
- What should be changed for regulated, safety-sensitive or credential-led roles where eligibility must be clear and verifiable?
- When are chronological, skills-based, hybrid, portfolio-led, project-based or short-form formats recommended, and what are their limitations?
- What do ATS-readable and human-readable formatting practices have in common, and where do they conflict?
- What typography, structure, headings, file formats, links, tables, columns, graphics, icons, colour and accessibility practices are supported by credible evidence or guidance?
- How do resume expectations differ between desk-based, frontline, trade, creative, academic, executive and platform-mediated roles?
- Which tailoring practices are useful, which are merely popular advice, and which may create misleading or discriminatory signals?
- How do people use AI to tailor resumes, what do they verify and edit, and what failure modes arise from generic language, keyword stuffing, fabricated achievements, privacy exposure or loss of personal voice?

### Role-fit and market-value questions

- Which techniques for identifying suitable roles have durable support from career guidance, occupational research, job-seeker accounts or other credible evidence?
- How can a person record preferences such as work content, environment, schedule, location, commute, income, stability, autonomy, learning, culture, physical demands and purpose?
- How can someone distinguish a genuine preference from a temporary constraint, fear, assumption, unfamiliarity or reaction to a previous bad experience?
- How can role exploration help a person discover preferences through comparison, small experiments, reflection, transferable-skill mapping or conversations already available in public sources?
- How should a person compare a role's actual duties and conditions with their preferences when job-posting language is vague, incomplete or promotional?
- How can role fit account for non-negotiables, trade-offs, accessibility needs, caring responsibilities, energy, schedule, travel and financial requirements?
- What evidence helps determine whether a person's skills are transferable to a role, and where are bridging qualifications or supervised experience required?
- How can someone estimate market value using skills, proficiency, experience, credentials, outcomes, seniority, industry, geography, demand and compensation evidence?
- Which sources are suitable for understanding pay and value, and how should base salary, allowances, commissions, bonuses, benefits, contract terms and progression be separated?
- How should a person handle sparse salary data, biased self-reported figures, outdated listings, different job titles and mismatched role scopes?
- What is genuinely time-tested or repeatedly supported, and what is merely popular career advice, influencer content, recruiter marketing or an AI-generated recommendation?
- How can a tool explain a role-fit or market-value assessment, show the evidence behind it, expose uncertainty and allow the user to correct its assumptions?

### Trust and decision-support questions

- How can a tool show why a resume suggestion was made and which source or job requirement supports it?
- How can the job seeker approve, reject, edit or undo a suggested change?
- What evidence should be shown before a user accepts a rewritten bullet, skill match or formatting change?
- How should uncertainty be represented when a job posting is incomplete, ambiguous or potentially stale?
- What should the tool refuse to generate or flag for human review?
- Which claims should remain the user's responsibility to verify before submission?

### Job-posting structure questions

- Which fields recur across Indeed, MyCareersFuture, FastJobs, Careers@Gov and employer-hosted recruitment pages?
- What different labels refer to the same concept, such as “About you,” “Requirements,” “What we are looking for,” “Job details” or “Key responsibilities”?
- Which fields are structured platform metadata and which are free-text employer content?
- Which information is usually present for office, frontline, trade, shift, creative, regulated and public-service roles?
- Which fields are most often missing, vague, contradictory or buried in free text?
- How are pay, allowances, commissions, bonuses, overtime, benefits and “up to” claims represented differently across boards?
- How are location, commute, worksite, travel, shift and schedule requirements represented?
- How are employer identity, agency/intermediary status, client identity and application destination represented or obscured?
- Which dates describe posting, update, expiry, closing, response expectations or an underlying hiring event?
- How should a parser or assistant preserve the original wording when a section cannot be mapped confidently to a canonical field?
- What is the minimum viable posting record for role-fit, resume tailoring, compensation questions and vacancy-credibility checks?
- Which fields should be shown as unknown rather than inferred when they are absent from the posting?
- How should the system represent multiple locations, multiple arrangements, shift rotations, conditional pay or several application routes?
- Which platform-specific fields are useful to job seekers but should not be mistaken for employer-authored facts, such as match scores, badges, recommendations or response indicators?

## Scope and boundaries

- Singapore-first and public-web research unless a later brief explicitly authorises another source context.
- Use the existing 13 Round 03 profiles as the baseline; do not silently repeat them as new evidence.
- Treat the candidate industries above as a prioritisation pool, not a final scope decision.
- Separate industry, occupation, seniority, employment arrangement and hiring channel.
- Separate resume, CV, cover letter, portfolio, application form and profile fields; do not assume one document serves every context.
- Include international guidance only when its geography and transfer limits are explicit.
- Distinguish reported practice, official guidance, recruiter/employer advice, research evidence, marketing claims, interpretation and unknowns.
- Treat resume advice as guidance to investigate, not as a guaranteed outcome or universal rule.
- Preserve the principle that tailoring may reorganise and clarify truthful experience but must not create fictional information.
- Treat role fit and market value as multi-dimensional and uncertain; do not reduce them to a single score or imply that a person has a fixed worth.
- Do not present salary ranges, market comparisons or role recommendations without showing geography, date, role scope, source population and limitations.
- Do not contact job seekers or employers, join private groups, submit applications, upload personal documents, collect identifiable resumes, or make population-prevalence claims from convenience sources.
- Do not choose product scope, platform, technology stack, AI provider or automation level in this round.
- Do not treat the preliminary field inventory as a universal standard or infer missing posting information from title, employer, industry or similar listings.

## Research method

The eventual execution plan should use the smallest coordinated team needed:

- **Research Planner:** prioritise additional industries and turn the objectives into bounded questions;
- **Research Query Strategist:** build balanced query families for industry gaps, resume tailoring, formatting, ATS/readability, accessibility, truthful claims and disconfirming evidence;
- **Job Domain Researcher:** collect industry, hiring-route, role, credential and application-context evidence;
- **Job-Seeker Practices Researcher:** collect reported resume/CV preparation, tailoring, AI use, checking and workaround practices;
- **AI Tools Expert:** examine AI-assisted tailoring capabilities, failure modes, privacy and user-control implications;
- **UX Expert:** examine readability, accessibility, document comprehension and review/approval needs;
- **Evidence Synthesizer:** reconcile the two tracks and preserve source-level uncertainty;
- **Responsible AI Reviewer:** review fabrication risk, bias, privacy, explainability and human-control boundaries.

For the posting-structure track, the Job Domain Researcher should own the field taxonomy and source context; the Research Query Strategist should design balanced board and employer-ATS sampling; the Evidence Synthesizer should preserve label variants and missingness; and the Responsible AI Reviewer should review risks from silently inferring or normalising missing job information.

No specialist should edit the same evidence or synthesis file concurrently. The coordinator should assign separate files, merge contributions, and maintain a claim-level source register.

The final plan should specify:

- selected additional industries and the reason for each;
- source families and geography/date limits;
- a query portfolio covering support, contradiction, failure and positive cases;
- a bounded source/search budget;
- profile and resume-practice recording templates;
- a stopping rule based on decision coverage and evidence quality;
- explicit gaps where public research cannot establish a reliable answer.

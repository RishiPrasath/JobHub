## 1. Purpose

Produce a slide deck that lets the owner review, in one sitting:

1. Everything we have learned about the job-seeker domain (research).
2. A brief rundown of the experiments and prototypes, and what each taught us.
3. An initial proposal for what the app should be, derived only from those findings.

The owner reviews the deck, challenges it, and decides what to keep. The deck is a review tool, not a pitch.

## 2. Deliverable and tool

- **Tool: Open Design** (the local design workspace connected to this repository through its MCP). Microsoft PowerPoint and `.pptx` files are not used.
- **Format:** a browser-viewable slide deck made by Open Design from one of its deck skills (it lists deck skills such as `deck-swiss-international`, `deck-guizang-editorial`, `frontend-slides`, `slides` and several template decks). Open Design natively produces HTML decks, not binary `.pptx` or `.pdf` files. If a PDF or other export is wanted later, that is a separate step the owner decides on.
- **Length:** about 30 slides (about 20 minutes) plus an appendix of about 10 slides.
- **Where the work lives:** Open Design keeps its projects in its own data folder, outside this repository. The finished deck is copied into the repository at `_presentations/<timestamp>_<deck-title>/` (project root), with the slide content inside that folder. No folder is created until a deck is ready to save. The exact timestamp format is a small open detail (proposal: `YYYY-MM-DD-HHMM`).
- **Speaker notes:** each slide carries its source file paths so the owner can trace any claim.

## 5. Initial proposal for the app (starting hypothesis for the Product Strategist to develop and test against the evidence)

Working statement: **a companion that helps a job seeker check a role and its employer against evidence, keep track of where they are in the hunt, and decide what to do next, without ever scoring or judging them.**

Grounded in the findings:

- **Core job 1: "Is this employer real, and who exactly is it?"** Registry check plus web step to resolve the legal entity, with refusal when ambiguous or not live (experiment X2 and the registry service).
- **Core job 2: "What does this posting actually say, and what should I ask?"** Source-linked claims, unknowns marked, verification questions to take to the interview (AI experiments).
- **Core job 3: "Is this pay reasonable?"** Benchmark against the official wage table only after the user confirms the occupation, labelled with date, population and exclusions (X3, round 08).
- **Core job 4: "Where am I in the process and what is next?"** A light process tracker based on the four as-is processes (round 09).
- **What it will not do:** automatic fit scores, resume rewriting, legitimacy verdicts on employers, or submitting applications (AI experiments found unsupported inferences even with strict prompts).
- **Trust design:** every statement labelled as source-quoted, inferred, or unknown; corrections kept; no hidden scores.
- **First slice to build:** employer identity check plus posting breakdown with verification questions. Test with real postings and a small group of job seekers before anything else.
- **Open questions the deck must raise:** mobile versus web, who the first user is, how red-flag evidence is presented without accusing a company, and what official sources can be used (X0 limits).

This is a hypothesis for the owner to react to. The Strategist may reframe it, but must trace every element to a finding.

## 7. How Open Design is used (workflow and limits)

1. **Start in a session where the MCP is loaded.** The Open Design tools load only when a session starts in this repository, so this work needs a new session. (They are connected as a local, private MCP for this repository.)
2. **Create a new Open Design project** for the deck. It lives in Open Design's own storage, not in the repository.
3. **Brief card:** Open Design opens a brief card in its app that the owner completes (audience, tone, content type). The coordinator cannot fill it in for the owner.
4. **Pick the skill and design system** from what Open Design lists (deck skills, and design systems such as the ready-made brand and style options), after a short comparison by role 6.
5. **Start the run.** Open Design spawns its own agent. Runs typically take 5 to 30 minutes. The coordinator polls, tells the owner it is still working, and does not cancel or replace the work with hand-written files unless the owner asks.
6. **Read back and review:** the coordinator reads the result as text, hands it to the Claims Auditor and Reviewer, and gives the owner the preview link.
7. **Revise:** corrections go back as a follow-up run or small text edits, not a full redo, unless the owner decides otherwise.
8. **Save to the repository** at `_presentations/<timestamp>_<deck-title>/` (decided), and only the deck's slide content and assets. Open Design's database, settings and credentials stay out of the repository.

Limits found when exploring the tool:
- The MCP can read only text files, so images, video or audio would be copied from the project's folder on disk, not read through the MCP.
- Some skills call outside services and the Open Design Cloud account, and a run uses the account of whichever agent program is chosen. Each run therefore needs the owner's approval before it starts.
- The deck is an HTML deck. Real `.pptx`, `.docx` or `.pdf` files are not produced by Open Design.

## 8. Visuals and images

- **Charts and infographics** are specified from our own results (for example X2 correct versus wrong, X3 matchable share, process-stage diagrams) and built inside the deck by Open Design. No invented data.
- **Process and journey diagrams** use the four as-is processes from round 09.
- **Illustrations and images (decided: both):**
  - Generated by Open Design's image skills, approved run by run because they may use credits or outside services.
  - Licensed stock: the owner supplies or approves each file; the licence and source are recorded in the speaker notes. Who supplies stock is an open detail.
- No screenshots or logos of real products or companies that imply endorsement or accusation. Prefer imagery that does not depict real firms or identifiable people.
- Every visual names its source in the notes.

## 9. Quality rules

1. Every claim on a slide is traceable to a file in this repository or a cited source; the Claims Auditor signs off.
2. Label evidence strength on the slide: tested in an experiment, desk research, anecdotal, or inference.
3. Say what failed and what is unknown; do not present hypotheses as findings. Experiment 10's X1 failure and X3 inconclusive result stay in.
4. Name no company as doing wrong beyond what an official source states; experiment company names appear only as already recorded and only where needed.
5. No third-party page text reproduced beyond short attributed quotes.
6. Plain language; one message per slide; no slide over about 60 words except the appendix.
